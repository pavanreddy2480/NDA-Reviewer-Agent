# Delegation of Authority and Signature Policy

**Bayview Health Partners**
Policy owner: Office of the Chief Financial Officer, with the Office of the General Counsel
Applies to: all Bayview entities, officers, managers and workforce members
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Next scheduled review: January 1, 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Bayview Health Partners ("Bayview") is governed by a Board of Directors, which delegates authority to the Chief Executive Officer and, through the CEO, to other officers and managers. Because a signature or an expenditure can commit Bayview legally and financially, it is important that everyone knows who is allowed to approve what. This policy states how authority is delegated, the limits on that authority, how documents are signed, and what must be done when a request goes beyond a person's authority.

### 1.2 Scope

The policy applies to every commitment made on behalf of Bayview: purchase orders, service arrangements, leases, capital projects, grants, research budgets, settlements, employment offers and the agreements that accompany them. It applies to employees, physicians in administrative roles and any outside party who is authorized to sign for Bayview. It does not alter the powers of the Board of Directors, the bylaws of Bayview or the Medical Staff Bylaws.

### 1.3 Related policies

The policy is applied together with the Confidentiality Agreement Policy, the Business Associate and Vendor Policy, the Research Collaboration Policy and the Conflict of Interest Policy. Where another policy requires a particular approver for a particular kind of arrangement, that approver's consent must also be obtained, in addition to the signature authority described here.

---

## 2. Definitions and Principles

### 2.1 Definitions

**Authority** means the power to commit Bayview financially or legally.

**Delegate** means a person who holds authority by delegation from a higher officer, evidenced by this policy or by a written delegation letter.

**Commitment** means any promise by Bayview to pay money, provide goods or services, accept an obligation or give up a right.

**Total value** means the total amount payable by Bayview over the full term of the commitment, including all renewals the other side may exercise, expenses and taxes, or, for a revenue arrangement, the total amount expected to be received.

**Approver** means a person whose written approval is a condition of a commitment.

### 2.2 Principles

1. Authority comes only from this policy, the Board, or a written delegation. A job title alone gives none.
2. A commitment may not be split into smaller parts to avoid a limit.
3. Nobody may approve a commitment in which he or she has a personal financial interest, or that benefits a relative, or that commits a unit he or she does not lead.
4. Authority cannot be delegated further without written permission from the officer who gave it.
5. Authority of an absent officer passes to the officer's named deputy for a period not exceeding 30 days.
6. The person who requests a commitment may not be the same person who approves it.

## 3. Signature Authority Matrix

### 3.1 Spending and financial commitments

| Total value | Required approver and signer |
|---|---|
| Up to $10,000 | Department Director |
| Over $10,000 to $50,000 | Vice President of the sponsoring division |
| Over $50,000 to $250,000 | Chief Operating Officer or Chief Financial Officer |
| Over $250,000 to $1,000,000 | Chief Executive Officer |
| Over $1,000,000 | Chief Executive Officer with the approval of the Finance Committee of the Board |
| Over $5,000,000 or any real estate transaction | Board of Directors |

Capital expenditures are also governed by the capital budget process, and equipment purchases by the Clinical Equipment Procurement Policy.

### 3.2 Non-financial commitments

Non-financial commitments, such as data sharing arrangements, cooperative agreements and memoranda of understanding, are signed by the Vice President of the sponsoring division after review by the Office of the General Counsel. Commitments that involve patient information also need the approval of the Chief Privacy Officer, and those that give outside parties access to Bayview systems need the approval of the Chief Information Security Officer.

### 3.3 Signature by individuals and delegates

Directors may sign only if they hold a delegation letter from their Vice President. Physicians, researchers and other individuals may not sign for Bayview unless a delegation letter exists. Research investigators who need to sign a document for a study should follow the Research Collaboration Policy, and workforce members who are unsure whether they hold authority should ask Contract Administration before they sign anything.

### 3.4 Employment and compensation

Offers of employment are approved by Human Resources and the hiring executive in line with the compensation schedule. Physician compensation arrangements need the approval of the Chief Medical Officer, the Chief Financial Officer and the Compliance Office, because of the federal physician self-referral and anti-kickback laws. No physician compensation arrangement may be signed until Compliance has confirmed that it is consistent with fair market value.

### 3.5 Litigation and settlements

Only the General Counsel may authorize the filing or settlement of a legal claim. Settlements above $100,000 require the approval of the Chief Executive Officer, and settlements above $500,000 need the approval of the Board. Insurers are notified as required by the policy terms before any settlement is offered.

## 4. Legal Review and Requirements for Agreements

This section sets out the legal review that precedes signature, and some of the terms that the Office of the General Counsel requires in the agreements the policy covers. It does not replace the more specific rules in the Confidentiality Agreement Policy, the Business Associate and Vendor Policy and the Research Collaboration Policy.

### 4.1 Governing law

Agreements must be governed by the law of California or Delaware, and the General Counsel alone may approve any other governing law.

The General Counsel will consider a request to accept another law where the counterparty is a government body that cannot agree to a foreign law, or where the counterparty is an organization based abroad for which California or Delaware law is unacceptable on regulatory grounds. The approval must be in writing and must be filed with the signed agreement.

### 4.2 Penalties

An agreement may not impose liquidated damages or any other monetary penalty on a party simply for breaching it.

Bayview prefers that damages for breach be set by a court or arbitrator, based on proven loss. A fixed charge is often disproportionate and may be unenforceable under California Civil Code section 1671. The Office of the General Counsel may approve exceptions only to the extent they are limited to a reasonable estimate of actual harm.

### 4.3 Restraints on competition and trade

If an agreement is governed by California law, it may not restrict either party from competing or from engaging in a lawful profession or business.

California Business and Professions Code section 16600 makes most restraints on trade void, and, since 2024, makes it unlawful to include one in an agreement or to attempt to enforce one. A Bayview agreement that included such a restraint would expose Bayview to liability. If a counterparty insists, the matter goes to the Office of the General Counsel.

### 4.4 Process of legal review

The Office of the General Counsel reviews any agreement with a total value above $50,000, any agreement that involves patient information, any agreement with a physician, referral source or government agency, and any agreement that does not use a Bayview template. The business sponsor submits the proposed agreement through the Legal Intake portal with a summary of the business purpose, the value, the term and the people who have already approved it. Legal review usually takes five business days, and complex matters take longer. The sponsor should not give the other side a commitment about timing.

### 4.5 Conflicts and referral sources

Agreements with physicians or their families, with companies in which a Bayview officer has an interest, and with anyone who refers patients to Bayview are reviewed by the Compliance Office before signature. The Compliance Office confirms that the arrangement is at fair market value, serves a legitimate need, is documented and does not take account of the volume or value of referrals.

## 5. How to Sign

### 5.1 Form of signature

Signatures are made in ink or by Bayview's approved electronic signature platform. Only the platform account of the signer may be used, and signers may not share credentials. Signers sign in their official title, for example "Jane Smith, Vice President, Ambulatory Services".

### 5.2 Before signing

Before signing, the signer must check that the approvals required by this policy and by the related policies have been obtained and are recorded, that the document is complete and has no blank spaces, that the legal name of the counterparty is correct, and that any exhibits are attached.

### 5.3 After signing

A fully signed copy is sent to Contract Administration within two business days. The copy is saved in the repository, and the expiry or renewal date is entered into the tracking system. Signed originals of real estate and Board matters are kept by the Corporate Secretary.

### 5.4 Emergencies

In an emergency that threatens patient safety, the on-call administrator may commit funds up to $100,000 to deal with it, and the Chief Operating Officer may commit more. Written notice must be sent to the Chief Financial Officer and the General Counsel on the next business day, with supporting documents within five business days.

## 6. Oversight

### 6.1 Monitoring

Contract Administration reports to the Finance Committee every quarter on commitments by value band and approver, commitments signed without the proper authority, split orders, emergency commitments and exceptions. Internal Audit tests a sample every year.

### 6.2 Unauthorized commitments

A commitment made without authority does not bind Bayview unless the General Counsel ratifies it. A person who makes such a commitment may be personally responsible for it, and will be subject to discipline. Requests for ratification are made through Legal Intake, with a statement of what happened and why.

### 6.3 Delegation letters

A delegation letter must name the delegate, the type and amount of commitment, the period (not exceeding 12 months) and any limits. Letters are signed by the officer who delegates and sent to Contract Administration, which keeps the register of delegations. A letter ends at once when the delegate leaves the role.

## 6A. Special Categories of Authority

### 6A.1 Grants and philanthropic gifts

The Bayview Foundation accepts gifts and grants on behalf of Bayview. Gifts of up to $100,000 may be accepted by the Foundation President. Gifts between $100,000 and $1,000,000 require the approval of the Chief Executive Officer, and larger gifts need the approval of the Foundation Board. Any gift that carries a restriction on how it may be used, any gift of property other than cash or marketable securities, and any gift that names a building or program must be reviewed by the Office of the General Counsel and the Chief Financial Officer before it is accepted. Gift acknowledgements are prepared by the Foundation and signed by the person who has accepted the gift.

### 6A.2 Real estate and leases

Only the Board of Directors may approve the purchase or sale of real property. Leases of space with annual rent above $250,000 or with a term longer than five years need the approval of the Finance Committee, and smaller leases are approved by the Chief Financial Officer. The Real Estate department prepares a business case that includes the clinical need, alternatives, the cost per square foot, the cost of build-out, and the effect on licensure and certification. The Office of the General Counsel negotiates all leases, and the Vice President of Facilities signs the lease only after the Chief Financial Officer has approved it in writing.

### 6A.3 Medical staff and professional services

Arrangements with physicians for call coverage, medical directorships, clinical coverage and other professional services are approved by the Chief Medical Officer and the Compliance Office, and are signed by the Chief Operating Officer. Each arrangement must be documented in writing before services begin, must describe the services and the time they take, must provide for payment at fair market value as determined by an independent valuation when the annual amount is more than $75,000, and must be tracked in the physician arrangements database so that Compliance can confirm that time sheets and payments match. Retroactive documentation is not permitted, and any gap is reported to the Compliance Office at once.

### 6A.4 Information technology and data arrangements

Purchases of software, cloud services and data services are approved at the levels in Section 3.1, but the Information Security Office must approve the security design and the Privacy Office must approve any arrangement that involves patient information, before the signer signs. Software licenses that renew automatically are tracked by Information Technology, and the business owner is told 90 days before each renewal so that it can be reviewed.

### 6A.5 Governmental filings and regulatory submissions

Filings with regulators, including cost reports, licensure applications, certificate of need requests, community benefit reports, tax returns and breach notifications, are signed by the officer named in the applicable regulation or, if none is named, by the Chief Financial Officer for financial filings and by the Chief Compliance Officer for other filings. The signer must receive a written certification from the manager who prepared the filing that it is complete and accurate.

## 6B. Training and Communication

The Office of the Chief Financial Officer publishes the authority matrix on the intranet each January and whenever it changes. Every new director, vice president and department head receives a briefing on this policy within 30 days of starting. Contract Administration offers a thirty-minute refresher each year, which is required for anyone who signed more than five documents in the previous year. The Chief Financial Officer tells the Audit Committee each year how many people hold a delegation letter and how many commitments were reviewed.

---

## 7. Exceptions and Enforcement

### 7.1 Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy may be granted only by the Chief Executive Officer for financial limits, and by the General Counsel for legal review requirements, unless a section names another approver. Requests are in writing and explain the business need. All exceptions are recorded in the exception register.

### 7.2 Enforcement

Workforce members who violate this policy may be disciplined up to and including termination. A signer who is found to have signed without authority loses signing privileges until the Chief Executive Officer reinstates them. Anyone who becomes aware of a violation should report it to the Compliance Office or the Compliance Hotline.
