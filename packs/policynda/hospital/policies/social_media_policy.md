# Social Media Policy

**Bayview Health Partners**
Policy owner: Office of Communications and Marketing
Applies to: all workforce members, medical staff, students, volunteers and Bayview-managed accounts
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Next scheduled review: January 1, 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Social media lets Bayview Health Partners ("Bayview") reach the community, recruit staff, share health education and support patients. It also lets a careless post harm a patient, damage a colleague or embarrass the organization in a few seconds. This policy sets out the expectations for official Bayview accounts and for the personal use of social media by people who work for Bayview. It is intended to protect patients and the organization without limiting rights that the law gives employees.

### 1.2 Scope

The policy covers all online platforms and tools that allow people to publish or exchange content: social networks (such as Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, TikTok, YouTube, Reddit and Snapchat), blogs, microblogs, podcasts, online forums, review sites, messaging applications, group chats and image and video sharing sites. It applies on and off duty, from Bayview or personal devices, whenever the use could reasonably be connected with Bayview.

### 1.3 Related policies

Rules on patient information are in the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy and the Patient Rights Policy. The Conflict of Interest Policy applies to paid endorsements and sponsorships. The Workplace Safety Policy and the code of conduct apply to harassment online as they do in person.

---

## 2. Definitions

**Official account** means a social media account that is created in Bayview's name, or in the name of a Bayview hospital, clinic or program, and that is managed by an approved administrator.

**Personal account** means an account that a person opens in his or her own name and uses for personal purposes.

**Content** means text, photographs, video, audio, links, reactions, comments and any other material posted online.

**Brand administrator** means a person approved by the Office of Communications to post on an official account.

**Patient-identifying information** means anything that could allow a reader to work out who a patient is: name, face, voice, initials, room number, date of treatment, unusual condition, a physical description, a tattoo, a medical record number, or a combination of details, even if the name is not given.

## 3. Official Accounts

### 3.1 Creating an account

No department may create an account that uses the Bayview name or logo without the approval of the Office of Communications. A request names the purpose, the audience, two administrators, a plan for content and a plan for answering comments. Communications keeps a register of all official accounts and the passwords, which are held in a password manager, not on paper. Accounts are protected by multi-factor authentication.

### 3.2 Standards for posts

Posts on official accounts must be accurate, respectful, in plain language and consistent with the Bayview brand guide. They must be accessible, which means that photographs have alternative text, videos have captions and hashtags use capital letters between words. Health information posted must come from reviewed clinical sources and must carry a date. Official accounts do not give medical advice to individuals, and answer questions about care by inviting the person to call the clinic or use the patient portal.

### 3.3 Photographs and stories of patients

An official account may post a story, photograph, video or testimonial about a patient only after the patient has signed the Bayview media authorization, which is approved by the Privacy Office and describes where and for how long the content will be used. The patient may withdraw the authorization at any time, and Communications will remove content from Bayview-controlled channels as soon as it can. Staff do not ask patients for authorization while they are in pain, sedated, in crisis or waiting for urgent treatment. Content about minors needs the signature of a parent or guardian, and, where appropriate, the minor's assent.

### 3.4 Responding to comments and reviews

Administrators monitor official accounts each business day. They hide or remove comments that contain abuse, hate speech, threats, spam or personal health information, and they keep a screenshot of anything removed. When a patient posts a complaint, the administrator replies once, courteously and generally, in terms such as "We would like to hear more, please call our patient advocate," and never confirms that the person is or was a patient. Complaints are passed to the Office of Patient Experience.

### 3.5 Crisis communications

During a disaster, outbreak, security incident or other crisis, only the Communications team posts on official accounts about the event. Departments suspend scheduled posts when told to by Communications. Statements are approved by the incident commander and the Chief Executive Officer or designee.

## 4. Personal Use by Workforce Members

### 4.1 General principles

Workforce members are free to take part in social media on their own time. They are responsible for what they post. They should use good judgment, be honest, be respectful and remember that anything posted online can be copied, shared and found years later, even if it is deleted.

### 4.2 Protecting patients

Workforce members must never post patient-identifying information, even in a closed group, a private message or a post that disappears. They may not post photographs or recordings of patients, patient rooms with a patient visible, whiteboards, screens, wristbands, charts or specimen labels. They may not share stories of cases in a way that lets a reader work out who the patient is. They may not look for patients on social media or "friend" a current patient. Responses to a patient's public review are left to the Communications team. A violation can also be a breach under HIPAA and California law, and is dealt with under the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy.

### 4.3 Professional boundaries

Clinicians should not accept friend requests from current patients or their families or give personal medical advice through social media. If a patient contacts a clinician through a personal account, the clinician politely invites the person to use the patient portal or telephone line. Supervisors should not require direct reports to follow them online.

### 4.4 Speaking for Bayview

Only people authorized by Communications may speak on behalf of Bayview. When a workforce member says something about Bayview, or about health topics, on a personal account, he or she should make it clear that the views are personal, for example by adding "Opinions are my own." Workforce members who identify themselves as Bayview employees should not use the Bayview logo, uniform or hospital grounds in a way that suggests an official endorsement. Press and media inquiries are referred to Communications.

### 4.5 Respectful conduct

Posts that harass, bully, threaten or discriminate against colleagues, patients or others are not acceptable, whether they are made at work or elsewhere, and may breach Bayview's policies against harassment and discrimination. Nothing in this policy prevents employees from discussing wages, hours and working conditions with each other, or from other activity protected by Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act or California Labor Code sections 232, 232.5 and 1102.5.

### 4.6 Working time and devices

Personal use of social media should not interfere with work or with patient care. In patient care areas, personal phones should be used only for urgent matters or for approved purposes. Use of Bayview networks may be monitored in accordance with the acceptable use rules, and users have no expectation of privacy on Bayview systems.

### 4.7 Political activity and fundraising

As a tax-exempt organization, Bayview may not take part in political campaigns. Workforce members may support candidates in their personal capacity, but may not use official accounts, Bayview resources or the Bayview name, and should make clear that they do not speak for Bayview. Posting fundraising appeals for an outside cause on official accounts requires Communications approval.

### 4.8 Recruitment and references

Hiring managers should not search the personal social media accounts of candidates for protected characteristics, and use only publicly available professional information in line with Human Resources guidance. Workforce members should not post references or recommendations for former colleagues on behalf of Bayview.

## 4A. Practical Guidance

### 4A.1 Examples of acceptable and unacceptable posts

Acceptable: sharing Bayview's published news about a new clinic opening; posting a photograph of colleagues at a community event when everyone in it has agreed; telling followers that you are proud to work in nursing; reposting a public health message from the county health department; and describing a hard day at work in general terms without any detail about a person.

Unacceptable: posting a photograph from an operating room, even without a patient's face; describing a patient's unusual injury along with the date and the neighborhood; commenting in a group chat about a celebrity who was seen in the emergency department; posting a picture of a whiteboard with names; tagging a patient in a thank-you post; and replying to a public review with details of the treatment given.

### 4A.2 Student and trainee accounts

Students, residents and fellows are bound by this policy in the same way as employees. Their schools and training programs may have additional rules, and the stricter rule applies. Program directors should remind trainees at the start of each rotation that they must not post material from clinical rotations.

### 4A.3 Group chats and messaging applications

Messaging applications other than Bayview's secure messaging tool may not be used to share patient information, including photographs of wounds, images, test results or schedules that list patients by name. A workforce member who is added to a group chat that contains patient information should leave it and tell the Privacy Office. Group chats for staffing and scheduling are allowed if they do not include patient details.

### 4A.4 Live streaming and video

Live streaming from any Bayview patient care area is prohibited. Video of public events on Bayview grounds may be recorded by Communications, which places notices at the entrance. Workforce members who record video at work for training or education use only Bayview-approved devices and tell everyone in the area before recording begins.

### 4A.5 Accounts of physicians in private practice

Independent physicians on the medical staff who use social media to promote their own practices must make clear that they are in private practice and not speaking for Bayview. They may not use the Bayview name or logo in advertising without approval from Communications.

### 4A.6 Impersonation and fake accounts

Workforce members who find an account that appears to impersonate Bayview, a Bayview facility or a Bayview leader should report it to Communications. Communications reports the account to the platform, saves evidence and, with the Office of the General Counsel, decides whether to take further steps. Employees should not respond to impersonation accounts or share their content.

## 5. Advertising, Endorsements and Influencers

When Bayview works with an influencer, or when a physician posts about a product or service, the post must disclose any payment or free item, as required by the Federal Trade Commission's Endorsement Guides. Official accounts do not use before-and-after photographs, claims of superior results or guarantees of outcomes. Reviews on third-party sites are not solicited in return for rewards. Communications reviews all paid campaigns, and the Compliance Office reviews any campaign that mentions a specific physician or service line.

---

## 6. Responding to Problems

### 6.1 Reporting

A workforce member who sees a post that may expose patient information, harm Bayview's reputation, threaten a person or break this policy should report it to Communications and to the Privacy Office, giving a link or screenshot. He or she should not respond online. The Privacy Office decides whether the incident is a breach.

### 6.2 Removal and escalation

Communications asks the platform to remove content that exposes patient information, and, with the Office of the General Counsel, deals with defamation, impersonation or fake accounts. Security assesses any threat against staff or patients and may call law enforcement.

### 6.3 Corrections

If Bayview posts a mistake, the administrator corrects it quickly and openly, adding a note where possible, and informs the Communications Director.

---

## 7. Training and Enforcement

All new workforce members are told of this policy at orientation, and managers and brand administrators complete a training module each year. Violations are handled under the corrective action policy, and the penalty depends on the seriousness of the violation and its effect on patients and colleagues. Violations that expose patient information can lead to immediate termination, and licensed clinicians may be reported to their licensing boards. Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith is prohibited.

---

## 8. Exceptions and Review

Requests for exceptions are made in writing to the Chief Marketing Officer, who consults the Privacy Office and the Office of the General Counsel when needed. Communications reviews the policy every year, and also whenever a platform changes in a way that affects its provisions.
