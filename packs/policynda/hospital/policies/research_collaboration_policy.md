# Research Collaboration Policy

**Bayview Health Partners**
Policy owner: Office of Research Administration, with the Office of the General Counsel
Applies to: all Bayview investigators, research staff, students and administrators
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Next scheduled review: January 1, 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Bayview Health Partners ("Bayview") participates in clinical and translational research through its teaching hospitals, its outpatient research clinics and its cancer, cardiovascular and neuroscience programs. Much of that work is done together with outside collaborators: universities, medical device and pharmaceutical companies, other health systems, patient advocacy foundations and government agencies. Collaboration lets Bayview patients take part in studies that would otherwise be out of reach, and it brings in funding that supports care and education.

Collaboration also creates risks. Patients may be exposed to uncertain interventions, personal data may be shared, intellectual property may be lost, and the objectivity of researchers may be questioned. This policy sets out the requirements Bayview applies to research collaborations, assigns responsibilities, and describes the review steps that must be completed before work begins.

### 1.2 Scope

This policy applies to:

- sponsored clinical trials, investigator-initiated studies and observational studies carried out at Bayview or by Bayview staff;
- laboratory and translational collaborations with academic and industry partners;
- the transfer of biological specimens, data and materials in either direction;
- joint grant applications, subawards and consortium participation; and
- early scientific conversations that precede a formal study, including meetings with prospective sponsors.

It does not apply to routine clinical care, to quality improvement projects that are not intended to produce generalizable knowledge (these are governed by the Quality Department's procedures), or to the purchase of research equipment (Clinical Equipment Procurement Policy).

### 1.3 Related policies

This policy is applied together with the Confidentiality Agreement Policy, the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy, the Delegation of Authority and Signature Policy, the Conflict of Interest Policy and the Medical Records Retention Policy. Institutional Review Board (IRB) procedures are published separately by the Human Research Protection Program.

## 2. Definitions and Roles

### 2.1 Definitions

**Research** means a systematic investigation designed to develop or contribute to generalizable knowledge, as defined in the Common Rule, 45 C.F.R. Part 46.

**Principal Investigator (PI)** means the person responsible for the design, conduct and reporting of a study at Bayview.

**Collaborator** means an outside organization or individual that takes part in a study, supplies funding, materials or data, or receives data or materials from Bayview.

**Sponsor** means the organization that initiates, funds or manages a study.

**Human Research Protection Program (HRPP)** means the office that supports the IRB and oversees the protection of research participants.

**Data use agreement** means a written agreement that governs the transfer and use of data, including limited data sets.

**Material transfer agreement** means a written agreement that governs the transfer of biological or chemical materials.

### 2.2 Roles

**Office of Research Administration (ORA)** receives proposals, negotiates budgets, reviews sponsor terms with the Office of the General Counsel, and keeps the research agreement files.

**Institutional Review Board (IRB)** reviews every study that involves human participants and decides whether it may begin, continue or must stop.

**Privacy Office** reviews each study for compliance with HIPAA and California privacy law, including authorizations, waivers and data use agreements.

**Conflict of Interest Committee** reviews investigators' financial interests and decides how conflicts will be managed.

**Office of the General Counsel** advises on legal terms and intellectual property.

**Principal Investigator** is responsible for conducting the study correctly and for informing ORA of any change that affects an agreement.

## 3. Early Scientific Conversations and Protection of Shared Information

Many collaborations start with informal conversations in which a Bayview investigator and an outside party exchange ideas, preliminary results and plans. Those conversations are valuable and should not be discouraged, but they need a written foundation. This section lists the points that Bayview requires in the written foundation. The detailed rules for such agreements are in the Confidentiality Agreement Policy, and the rules on personal data are in the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy.

### 3.1 Obtaining similar information from others

When Bayview is the receiving party, the agreement must preserve Bayview's freedom to obtain similar information lawfully from a third party.

Bayview research groups often work on the same questions as other laboratories. Without this protection, a Bayview investigator who received a sponsor's data on a biomarker could be accused of misuse for using a published or independently supplied result on the same biomarker. The provision must be preserved when Bayview is the recipient of information.

### 3.2 Independent development

Every research agreement must preserve Bayview's right to develop similar information independently, so that our own investigators are not blocked from their programs.

The point of the provision is practical. A hospital has many investigators with overlapping interests, and an agreement that stops any of them from pursuing a line of work that is already under way in their laboratory would damage Bayview's research mission. Investigators who believe a sponsor may have taken advantage of this provision should talk to ORA before they continue.

### 3.3 No transfer of rights

The agreement must confirm that the recipient receives no license or other right in the information we share.

Any license to Bayview intellectual property is granted only through a license agreement approved by the Technology Transfer Office and the Office of the General Counsel. Investigators must not say or imply to a collaborator that the collaborator will receive rights to a discovery.

### 3.4 Publicity

The agreement must prohibit the recipient from disclosing that the collaboration exists or that the agreement was negotiated.

Announcements about partnerships are made by Bayview's Communications office, normally after the study has been approved by the IRB, and only with the consent of the other party. Investigators do not announce collaborations on social media or at conferences before that consent.

### 3.5 Procedure for early conversations

1. The investigator contacts ORA and describes the discussions, the collaborator, the nature of the information that may be exchanged and whether any patient data is involved.
2. ORA issues a Bayview template from the Office of the General Counsel, or reviews the collaborator's draft under the Confidentiality Agreement Policy.
3. The investigator signs only if authorized under the Delegation of Authority and Signature Policy. In most cases, ORA signs on the investigator's behalf.
4. No materials or data are exchanged until the signed document is on file.
5. The investigator notes in the study file what information was exchanged, so that later publication and patent questions can be answered.

## 4. Study Approval

### 4.1 Required reviews

No research begins until the following steps are completed:

- scientific review by the department, to confirm that the question is important and the design is sound;
- review by the IRB, including approval of the protocol, consent form and any recruitment materials;
- review by the Privacy Office, including the HIPAA authorization or waiver;
- review of the budget by ORA, to make sure that the amount covers Bayview's actual costs including those of pharmacy, imaging, laboratory, nursing and data management;
- review by the Conflict of Interest Committee of every investigator's disclosures; and
- legal review of the sponsor's terms by the Office of the General Counsel.

### 4.2 Informed consent

Participants must give voluntary, informed consent in writing, unless the IRB has waived it. The consent form describes the purpose, procedures, risks, benefits, alternatives, costs, compensation, the extent of privacy protection, and the participant's right to withdraw without penalty. Short forms and translated forms are available for participants who do not read English. A copy is given to the participant and the original is kept in the study file.

### 4.3 Budgets and payments

All payments for a study go through ORA. Investigators may not accept direct payments from sponsors. Indirect costs are charged at Bayview's negotiated federal rate or the sponsor's rate, whichever is higher, and any request to reduce the rate needs the approval of the Vice President for Research. Participant payments must be reasonable and may not be so large as to cause undue influence.

### 4.4 Billing compliance

Studies that involve services billed to Medicare, Medicaid or private insurance require a coverage analysis before enrollment. Items and services provided only for research are charged to the study, not to the patient's insurance. ORA and Revenue Cycle reconcile study charges every quarter.

## 5. Data, Specimens and Materials

### 5.1 Data sharing

Data may leave Bayview only as permitted by the IRB approval, the participant authorization or waiver, and the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy. Data should be de-identified wherever possible. A limited data set requires a data use agreement that has been reviewed by the Privacy Office. Direct identifiers are not transferred unless the IRB and Privacy Office have found that they are essential.

### 5.2 Specimens and biobanking

Specimens are stored in the Bayview Biorepository under written procedures. The consent form must say whether specimens will be kept and used in future research. Participants who withdraw may ask that their remaining specimens be destroyed. A material transfer agreement is required before specimens leave Bayview.

### 5.3 Data security

Research data containing personal information must be kept on Bayview-managed systems or on cloud services that have been reviewed by the Information Security Office. Data may not be stored on personal drives, unencrypted portable media or consumer file sharing services. Principal Investigators must report any loss or exposure of data to the Privacy Office within 24 hours.

### 5.4 Publication and data ownership

Bayview investigators have the right to publish their results. Sponsors may review a manuscript for a short period before submission so that they can request patent filings, but may not require changes to results or conclusions. The Technology Transfer Office manages inventions and patents. Data collected at Bayview belongs to Bayview, subject to the rights granted to the sponsor in the written study agreement.

## 6. Integrity and Safety

### 6.1 Research integrity

Investigators must follow the Public Health Service policies on research misconduct. Allegations of fabrication, falsification or plagiarism are referred to the Research Integrity Officer, who conducts an inquiry. Retaliation against those who report in good faith is prohibited.

### 6.2 Adverse events

Serious adverse events and unanticipated problems are reported to the IRB within the timelines in the HRPP procedures, normally within 5 business days, and sooner when the event is life-threatening. The PI informs the sponsor as required by the protocol.

### 6.3 Monitoring and audit

ORA and HRPP conduct routine audits of a sample of active studies each year. Audits cover consent documentation, protocol adherence, drug accountability, regulatory files and billing. The PI must correct any finding within 30 days. Serious or continuing non-compliance is reported to the IRB and, where required, to federal agencies.

### 6.4 Conflicts of interest

Investigators disclose their financial interests annually and whenever they change. An investigator with a significant financial interest in a sponsor may not take part in the consent process unless the Conflict of Interest Committee has approved a management plan. The plan may require disclosure in the consent form, independent monitoring, or removal from the study.

## 6A. Study Operations

### 6A.1 Recruitment and advertising

Recruitment materials, including flyers, letters, web pages, social media posts and scripts, are reviewed and approved by the IRB before use. Materials must not promise a cure, overstate benefits or use language that implies that the study treatment is safe or effective. Payment for participation may be mentioned but should not be emphasized. Investigators may contact their own patients about a study, but may not pressure them, and must make clear that the decision does not affect their care. Direct contact with patients of other physicians requires the permission of those physicians.

### 6A.2 Vulnerable populations

Studies involving children, pregnant women, fetuses, prisoners, adults who cannot consent, students and employees receive extra review. The IRB determines whether the study meets the added protections in Subparts B, C and D of 45 C.F.R. Part 46 and in California law, including the Protection of Human Subjects in Medical Experimentation Act. For adults who may lack capacity, the consent process includes an assessment of capacity and the involvement of a legally authorized representative where needed. Employees and students are not recruited by their supervisors.

### 6A.3 Registration and results reporting

Clinical trials that fall under federal law must be registered on ClinicalTrials.gov before the first participant is enrolled, and summary results must be posted within 12 months of the primary completion date. ORA keeps a register of the studies and sends reminders to the PIs. The Compliance Office checks the register once a year and reports gaps to the Vice President for Research.

### 6A.4 Investigational drugs and devices

Investigational drugs are stored, dispensed and tracked only by the Investigational Drug Service in Pharmacy. Investigational devices are logged and stored by the PI according to FDA rules, with a device accountability record that shows each use. Investigators who sponsor a study under their own IND or IDE take on the duties of a sponsor, and ORA and the Office of the General Counsel will advise them on these duties.

### 6A.5 Subawards and consortia

When Bayview receives a federal award and passes part of the funds to another organization, ORA performs a risk assessment of that organization, including its financial systems, audit history and capacity, and monitors its progress at least every year. When Bayview is a subrecipient, it follows the terms of the prime award and the flow-down requirements, and ORA tracks invoices, reports and deadlines.

### 6A.6 Research staff and training

All research staff must complete training in human subjects protection, good clinical practice and responsible conduct of research before they begin work, and refresh it every three years. PIs ensure that each person's role is documented on a delegation log signed by the PI, and that no one performs a task that he or she has not been trained or authorized to perform. Clinical research coordinators are overseen by the Clinical Research Unit, which holds a monthly meeting.

## 7. Exceptions, Enforcement and Review

### 7.1 Exceptions

An exception to this policy may be granted by the Vice President for Research, with the agreement of the General Counsel when a legal requirement is affected. Requests state the provision, the reason and the safeguards. An exception never excuses compliance with law or IRB requirements.

### 7.2 Enforcement

Failure to follow this policy may lead to suspension of research privileges, notice to the sponsor or funding agency, disciplinary action under the corrective action policy and, if the failure is serious, referral to licensing boards. Anyone who knows of a violation should report it to ORA, the Compliance Office or the Compliance Hotline.

### 7.3 Policy review

ORA reviews this policy each year with the HRPP, the Privacy Office and the Office of the General Counsel, and proposes changes to the Research Committee of the Board.
