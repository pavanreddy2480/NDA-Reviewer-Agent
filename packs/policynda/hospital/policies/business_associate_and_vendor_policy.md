# Business Associate and Vendor Policy

**Bayview Health Partners**
Policy owner: Supply Chain Services, with the Office of the Chief Privacy Officer
Applies to: all Bayview facilities, departments and shared services
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Next scheduled review: January 1, 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Bayview Health Partners ("Bayview") buys a very wide range of services. Information technology vendors host our electronic health record, billing companies submit claims, transcription and imaging companies handle patient information, staffing agencies supply nurses and technicians, and consultants advise on everything from construction to revenue cycle. Each of these relationships creates risk for patients and for Bayview. This policy sets out how Bayview selects, onboards, monitors and exits vendors, and how it treats vendors that are business associates under HIPAA.

The policy aims to make sure that:

- every vendor that touches patient information is identified before it receives any, and is bound by a business associate agreement;
- vendors are assessed in proportion to the risk they pose, so that a caterer is not put through the same review as a cloud hosting provider;
- the people at Bayview who select and manage vendors know who has to approve what; and
- when a vendor relationship ends, Bayview's information comes back or is destroyed and access is withdrawn.

### 1.2 Scope

This policy applies to every purchase of goods or services from an outside organization, whatever its size, and to every individual contractor who works under Bayview's direction. It does not apply to the purchase of clinical equipment, which is covered by the Clinical Equipment Procurement Policy, except that any equipment that stores or transmits patient information is also subject to Sections 5 and 6 of this policy.

### 1.3 Related policies

This policy works alongside the Confidentiality Agreement Policy, the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy, the Delegation of Authority and Signature Policy, and the Medical Records Retention Policy. The confidentiality agreement that precedes most vendor relationships is governed by the Confidentiality Agreement Policy, and the sections of this policy that bear on it are set out in Sections 3 and 4 below.

## 2. Definitions and Roles

### 2.1 Definitions

**Vendor** means any outside organization or individual that supplies goods or services to Bayview for payment, including consultants and staffing agencies.

**Business associate** has the meaning given in 45 C.F.R. 160.103: a person or organization that creates, receives, maintains or transmits protected health information on behalf of Bayview, other than as a member of the workforce. Billing companies, cloud hosts, shredding companies, transcription services, accreditation consultants who review charts, and law firms that receive patient information are typical business associates.

**Subcontractor** means a person or organization to whom a vendor delegates a function that involves Bayview information.

**Risk tier** means the classification, set by Supply Chain Services with the Information Security Office, of how much harm could result from a vendor's failure. There are three tiers: Tier 1 (high), Tier 2 (moderate) and Tier 3 (low).

**Business owner** means the Bayview manager who is responsible for the relationship with a vendor.

### 2.2 Roles

**Supply Chain Services** runs the vendor master file, issues requests for proposals, handles onboarding and keeps the register of vendors and agreements.

**Information Security Office** performs security assessments, reviews system access requests and monitors vendor performance on security matters.

**Privacy Office** determines whether a vendor is a business associate, reviews the business associate agreement and tracks incidents involving vendors.

**Office of the General Counsel** reviews non-standard terms and handles disputes.

**Business owner** nominates the vendor, completes the intake form, supervises the vendor day to day, and tells Supply Chain Services when the relationship will end.

## 3. Vendor Selection and Onboarding

### 3.1 Staff hiring and vendor staff

A vendor agreement may not contain a clause that bars Bayview from approaching or hiring the vendor's personnel, because we routinely recruit clinical and information technology staff from staffing vendors.

Any vendor that asks for such a restriction should be referred to the Office of the General Counsel. The Office will discuss the vendor's concern, which is usually a worry about losing staff that it has trained, and will normally address it through a placement fee schedule in the commercial terms instead.

### 3.2 Protection of vendor-supplied systems

Each vendor agreement must forbid the vendor from reverse engineering, decompiling or disassembling any system, software or device that Bayview makes available.

This protects Bayview's interface engines, test environments, patient portal code that Bayview has licensed from others and medical devices that carry third-party firmware. Information Technology reports every case in which a vendor has been found probing a Bayview system without authorization to the Information Security Office.

### 3.3 Vendor intake

The business owner starts the process by completing the vendor intake form in the procurement system. The form asks:

1. What goods or services are being bought, for how long, and for what estimated value?
2. Will the vendor create, receive, store or transmit patient information or other personal data?
3. Will the vendor have access to Bayview networks, applications or facilities?
4. Is there an existing vendor that could supply the same thing?
5. Will any part of the work be done by a subcontractor or outside the United States?

Supply Chain Services uses the answers to assign a risk tier. Any vendor that will handle patient information is Tier 1 or Tier 2 and is referred to the Privacy Office for a business associate determination.

### 3.4 Competitive selection

Purchases above $50,000 in annual value require at least three written quotes or a request for proposals, unless the Chief Financial Officer approves a sole-source purchase in writing. For purchases between $10,000 and $50,000, the business owner obtains two quotes. Purchases below $10,000 may be made from an approved vendor on a purchase order. The Delegation of Authority and Signature Policy determines who must approve the final selection.

### 3.5 Security assessment

Tier 1 vendors complete the full security questionnaire and provide their most recent independent assessment report (for example a SOC 2 Type II report or HITRUST certification). Tier 2 vendors complete a shorter questionnaire. Tier 3 vendors are not assessed unless their work changes. The Information Security Office documents each assessment, records the vendor's remediation commitments and sets a date for reassessment, which is annual for Tier 1 vendors, every two years for Tier 2 and on change for Tier 3.

### 3.6 Conflicts of interest and exclusion screening

Before a vendor is added to the vendor master file, Supply Chain Services checks it against the federal List of Excluded Individuals and Entities and the System for Award Management exclusion list. The check is repeated monthly. A vendor found on either list is suspended immediately and the Compliance Office is notified. Business owners and evaluators also complete a conflict of interest disclosure under the Conflict of Interest Policy before they take part in a selection decision.

## 4. Business Associates and the End of a Relationship

### 4.1 Return or destruction of personal data

Where an agreement involves personal data, the vendor must be bound to return or destroy that data at the end of the relationship.

For business associates this is also a HIPAA requirement: the business associate agreement must say that the vendor will return or destroy all protected health information at termination, if feasible, and, if not feasible, extend protections and limit further use. The confidentiality agreement is a second layer, and it should be consistent with the business associate agreement. The general rule on return and destruction for all agreements is set out in the Confidentiality Agreement Policy.

### 4.2 Return deadline

Return or destruction must be completed within 30 days after Bayview asks for it or the agreement ends.

Business owners should diarize the due date and request written confirmation from a named officer of the vendor. If the vendor needs more time because of a technical constraint, such as data stored in backup tapes that rotate on a longer cycle, the business owner refers the request to the Privacy Office. The Privacy Office can agree that the vendor will keep the backup protected and will destroy it when it rotates, but the 30-day period in this section is the standard.

### 4.3 Time allowed when Bayview is the recipient

When Bayview is on the receiving side, the agreement must give Bayview at least 10 days to complete return or destruction so that litigation and records holds can be checked first.

Bayview cannot destroy information that is subject to a litigation hold, a regulatory inquiry or a records retention requirement. The minimum is designed to allow Health Information Management and the Office of the General Counsel to check for holds before anything is destroyed. See the Medical Records Retention Policy for the holds procedure.

### 4.4 Offboarding checklist

When a vendor relationship ends, the business owner completes the following checklist and sends it to Supply Chain Services:

- confirm that all Bayview badges, tokens and keys have been collected;
- ask the Information Security Office to disable all vendor accounts and remote access, and to confirm that this was done on the day of termination;
- confirm that all Bayview-owned equipment has been returned and that any storage media have been wiped to NIST 800-88 standards;
- obtain the vendor's written certification of return or destruction;
- check that all invoices have been received and that the final payment is approved;
- record any lessons learned in the vendor file.

Supply Chain Services does not close the vendor file until the checklist is complete.

## 5. Safeguarding Information in Vendor Relationships

### 5.1 Location of data

Where personal data is involved, the agreement may not permit the recipient to store, access or process that data outside the United States, unless the Chief Information Security Officer has approved the arrangement.

The Chief Information Security Officer considers the sensitivity of the data, the legal regime in the destination country, the vendor's controls and whether comparable services are available in the United States. Approvals are recorded in the vendor file and reviewed whenever the vendor is reassessed. A vendor that has not been approved must route its support, development and hosting entirely through facilities in the United States. Incidents are reported under the notification procedure described in the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy.

### 5.2 Business associate agreement

A Bayview business associate agreement must be signed before a vendor receives any protected health information. The Privacy Office maintains the standard form, which requires the business associate to use and disclose protected health information only as permitted, to apply the HIPAA Security Rule safeguards, to report breaches and security incidents, to make sure that subcontractors accept the same restrictions, to give individuals access to their records, to make its books available to the Department of Health and Human Services, and to return or destroy the information at the end. Changes to the standard form need the written approval of the Chief Privacy Officer.

### 5.3 Subcontractors

A business associate may use a subcontractor only if the business associate has a written agreement with the subcontractor that imposes the same restrictions. Vendors must tell Bayview of any new subcontractor that will handle Bayview information, before the subcontractor begins work. The business owner records the notice in the vendor file and forwards it to the Privacy Office.

### 5.4 System access

Vendor staff who need access to Bayview systems are given individual accounts. Shared accounts are not permitted. Access is limited to the systems the person needs, granted for a fixed period of no more than 12 months, and recertified by the business owner when it expires. The Information Security Office reviews all vendor accounts quarterly.

### 5.5 On-site vendors

Vendor representatives who visit patient care areas must register with Security, wear a Bayview vendor badge, follow the infection control rules of the department they visit and, where they will be in a patient care area, show evidence of the immunizations that Employee Health requires. Vendor representatives may not photograph patients, screens or records.

## 6. Monitoring and Performance

### 6.1 Vendor scorecards

Business owners of Tier 1 and Tier 2 vendors complete a scorecard every year. The scorecard rates delivery against the service expectations, quality, responsiveness, security performance, invoicing accuracy and compliance with this policy. Supply Chain Services uses the scorecards in renewal and sourcing decisions and reports a summary to the Finance Committee every year.

### 6.2 Incident reporting

A business owner who learns of any vendor error or incident that could affect patient information, patient safety or Bayview operations must report it within 24 hours to the Privacy Office, the Information Security Office and Supply Chain Services. The Privacy Office decides whether the incident is a breach that must be reported under HIPAA and the California Health and Safety Code, and takes charge of the investigation.

### 6.3 Financial review

For Tier 1 vendors, Supply Chain Services reviews the vendor's financial statements annually. If a vendor shows signs of financial distress, the business owner and Supply Chain Services prepare a contingency plan, including the cost of moving to another vendor.

### 6.4 Insurance

Vendors must carry the insurance that Risk Management specifies for their tier, including commercial general liability, professional liability, workers' compensation and, for Tier 1 vendors, cyber liability coverage of not less than $5 million. Certificates are collected before the vendor starts work and are renewed annually.

## 6A. Special Categories of Vendors

### 6A.1 Staffing agencies

Staffing agencies must verify that each worker has a current license, background check, health screening and immunizations before the worker is placed. Human Resources audits the agency's files twice a year. Agency workers complete Bayview orientation, including privacy and safety training, before they begin and may not receive access to the electronic health record until training is complete. The maximum length of an agency assignment is 12 months, after which the manager must either convert the position or ask Supply Chain Services for an extension.

### 6A.2 Cloud and software vendors

Cloud vendors must show evidence of encryption of data at rest and in transit, role-based access, logging of access to protected health information, regular vulnerability testing, backup and recovery capability, and a plan for continuity. Software that is installed on Bayview systems is scanned by the Information Security Office before deployment. Vendors must tell Bayview of material security vulnerabilities in their products within a reasonable time after they learn of them.

### 6A.3 Consultants and professional services

Consultants are selected for expertise that Bayview does not have. The business owner defines the scope and the expected deliverables in writing, sets milestones and reviews the work before payment. Consultants who have been public officials or who also advise a competitor are disclosed to the Compliance Office before engagement.

### 6A.4 Shredding, storage and waste vendors

Vendors that handle paper records, electronic media and waste that may contain patient information must provide certificates of destruction, maintain a secure chain of custody with locked containers and tracked vehicles, and allow Bayview to inspect their facilities on notice. Medical waste vendors must have the registrations that California requires.

### 6A.5 Emergency purchases

When a vendor is needed in an emergency that threatens patient safety or the continuity of services, the business owner may engage the vendor immediately and must send the intake form to Supply Chain Services within three business days. A business associate agreement must still be in place before any protected health information is shared.

---

## 7. Exceptions

An exception to any part of this policy may be requested by the business owner through Supply Chain Services. Requests must state the provision, the reason, the risk and any compensating controls. Unless this policy names another approver, exceptions are decided by the Director of Supply Chain Services for Tier 3 vendors, by the Vice President of Supply Chain for Tier 2 vendors and by the Chief Operating Officer for Tier 1 vendors, after consultation with the Privacy Office and the Information Security Office. Exceptions that relate to the handling of personal data also need the agreement of the Chief Privacy Officer. All exceptions are recorded in the vendor file and reported to the Compliance Committee quarterly.

---

## 8. Training and Awareness

New business owners complete a thirty-minute vendor management module within sixty days of assuming responsibility for a vendor. Supply Chain Services runs a workshop twice a year for department managers. The Privacy Office provides specific training on business associates to anyone who regularly manages Tier 1 vendors.

---

## 9. Enforcement

Failure to follow this policy may result in disciplinary action under the corrective action policy. A purchase made without following the intake and approval steps may be refused payment. Vendors that violate this policy or the terms of their agreements may be suspended or removed from the vendor master file. Suspected violations should be reported to Supply Chain Services, the Compliance Office or the Compliance Hotline.
