# Clinical Equipment Procurement Policy

**Bayview Health Partners**
Policy owner: Clinical Engineering and Supply Chain Services
Applies to: all purchases and leases of clinical equipment at Bayview facilities
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Next scheduled review: January 1, 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Clinical equipment is among the largest and most consequential purchases that Bayview Health Partners ("Bayview") makes. Infusion pumps, ventilators, imaging systems, patient monitors, surgical robots, laboratory analyzers and beds directly affect patient safety, clinical quality and the budget. A poor purchase can mean injury, downtime, expensive repairs, training problems and a device that does not work with the electronic health record. This policy establishes a consistent way to decide what clinical equipment Bayview needs, to evaluate options, to choose among them, to bring new equipment into service safely and to retire it responsibly.

### 1.2 Scope

The policy applies to the purchase, lease, loan, rental, trial and donation of any equipment that is used to diagnose, monitor, treat or support patients, or to process specimens, including accessories, software, and service plans. It applies to new, refurbished and used equipment. Equipment valued at $5,000 or more, or any item that connects to the Bayview network, plugs into an electrical outlet in a patient care area or carries a patient safety risk, is covered regardless of cost. Routine consumables, furniture and office equipment are covered by the Business Associate and Vendor Policy and the ordinary purchasing procedures of Supply Chain Services.

### 1.3 Related policies

The Delegation of Authority and Signature Policy sets who may approve a purchase at each spending level. The Conflict of Interest Policy governs gifts and financial interests of evaluators. Where a device stores or transmits patient information, the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy and the Business Associate and Vendor Policy also apply.

## 2. Definitions and Roles

### 2.1 Definitions

**Clinical equipment** means any device, instrument, apparatus, machine or system, including software, used for patient care or for processing patient specimens.

**Capital equipment** means equipment with a purchase price of $5,000 or more and a useful life of more than one year.

**Total cost of ownership** means the purchase price plus installation, training, service, supplies, software upgrades, interfaces, disposal and the cost of the space the equipment occupies, over the expected life.

**Biomedical engineering** means the team within Clinical Engineering that inspects, tests, maintains and repairs devices.

**Value Analysis Committee (VAC)** means the multidisciplinary group that evaluates new products and technologies for clinical effectiveness, safety and cost.

**Requester** means the clinician or manager who asks for the equipment.

### 2.2 Roles

**Requester** identifies the clinical need, completes the request and serves on the evaluation team.

**Clinical Engineering** assesses technical requirements, safety, maintenance needs and compatibility, leads acceptance testing and keeps the equipment inventory.

**Supply Chain Services** runs the quoting or bidding process, manages negotiation with suppliers and keeps the records.

**Value Analysis Committee** reviews requests above the thresholds in Section 4 and recommends decisions.

**Information Security Office** reviews connected devices for cybersecurity.

**Finance** confirms funding and analyzes the business case.

**Infection Prevention** reviews cleaning and reprocessing requirements.

## 3. Identifying the Need

### 3.1 Request

A requester submits an equipment request form that states the clinical need, the number of patients and procedures affected, the equipment now in use, evidence supporting the choice, the funding source, the space and utilities required, the expected impact on staffing and training, and the date by which the equipment is needed. Requests for replacement include the age, repair history and downtime of the current item.

### 3.2 Capital planning

Requests for capital equipment are collected each year during the capital budget cycle, which runs from March through June. Finance and the Capital Committee rank them using a scoring model that weights patient safety and regulatory requirements first, then quality improvement, then financial return, then strategic fit. Urgent requests outside the cycle are assessed by the Chief Operating Officer and the Chief Financial Officer. The Chief Medical Officer is consulted on any item that affects clinical practice.

### 3.3 Standardization

Bayview favors standard models across its hospitals so that staff can move between units without retraining, supplies can be shared and service can be consolidated. A request for a model that is not on the standard list must explain why a standard model does not meet the need.

## 4. Evaluation and Selection

### 4.1 Thresholds

| Estimated total cost of ownership | Evaluation required |
|---|---|
| Under $10,000 | Requester and Clinical Engineering review; one quote |
| $10,000 to $50,000 | Two quotes and Clinical Engineering technical review |
| $50,000 to $250,000 | Three quotes or a request for proposals, a trial where possible, and Value Analysis Committee review |
| Over $250,000 | Formal request for proposals, a multidisciplinary evaluation team, a site visit or reference calls, and Value Analysis Committee recommendation |

### 4.2 Evaluation criteria

Evaluators score each option against written criteria agreed before proposals are opened. Standard criteria include:

1. clinical performance and evidence of safety and effectiveness, including FDA clearance or approval status and the manufacturer's recall history;
2. usability, based on hands-on trials by the staff who will use the device;
3. compatibility with existing devices, the electronic health record, the picture archiving system and the hospital network;
4. cybersecurity, including a completed Manufacturer Disclosure Statement for Medical Device Security, patching practice, support for encryption and authentication, and the ability to segment the device on the network;
5. total cost of ownership over the expected life;
6. service quality, including response times, availability of parts, remote support and loaner equipment;
7. training and clinical education support;
8. cleaning and reprocessing needs;
9. environmental and energy aspects, including space, weight, power, heat and disposal; and
10. the supplier's financial stability and reputation.

### 4.3 Trials and demonstrations

Trials of equipment in patient care areas must be approved by Clinical Engineering and Value Analysis before the device is brought in. The device is inspected, tagged and tested before use. Patients are told about the trial when appropriate. Staff are trained, and a supervisor is named as responsible. Trials usually last no longer than 30 days. Suppliers' representatives may not operate a device on a patient except as an observer in a limited training role approved by the department.

### 4.4 Decision

The evaluation team writes a short recommendation that records the options considered, the scores, the total cost of ownership and the reasons for the preferred choice. Supply Chain Services confirms that the process was followed. The approval authority is set by the Delegation of Authority and Signature Policy. A requester or evaluator who has a financial interest in any supplier must disclose it and be recused.

## 5. Purchasing and Delivery

### 5.1 Purchase order

Supply Chain Services issues a purchase order that identifies the equipment by model and configuration, lists accessories, software and services, states price, delivery date and installation arrangements, and requires the supplier to supply operator manuals, service manuals, wiring diagrams, parts lists and calibration instructions. The order is not released until the funding source has been confirmed.

### 5.2 Leases and rentals

Leases and rentals of equipment are evaluated on the basis of total cost, and the Chief Financial Officer must approve any lease that is not recorded as a capital lease. Short-term rentals to cover emergencies or planned downtime are arranged through Clinical Engineering and Supply Chain Services.

### 5.3 Donated and loaned equipment

Equipment offered as a gift or loan is subject to the same technical and safety review as purchased equipment. Donations must be accepted in writing by the Foundation, and must not be tied to the purchase of other products or influence a clinical decision. A loan of equipment from a supplier should be for a fixed period and requires a signed loan form that identifies the equipment.

### 5.4 Receiving and acceptance testing

Equipment is delivered to Clinical Engineering, not directly to the department. Biomedical engineers check the item against the order, inspect it for damage, assign an asset tag, record it in the inventory, perform an incoming inspection and electrical safety test, check software and firmware versions, confirm that cybersecurity settings are in place, and verify the functions and alarms. Equipment is released to the department only after it passes these tests and the department has confirmed that its staff are trained.

### 5.5 Installation

Large systems such as imaging equipment require a site readiness plan that covers construction, shielding, power, cooling, network connections, infection control risk assessment and downtime procedures. Facilities and Clinical Engineering sign off on completion. Radiation-producing equipment is inspected and registered with the California Department of Public Health before clinical use.

## 6. Use, Maintenance and Safety

### 6.1 Training

Staff who use a new type of equipment must complete training and demonstrate competency before using it on patients. Records of training are kept by the department and Clinical Engineering. The supplier provides initial training, and Clinical Education offers refreshers.

### 6.2 Preventive maintenance

Clinical Engineering keeps an inventory of all clinical equipment and a schedule of inspection and preventive maintenance based on the manufacturer's recommendations and risk. High-risk life-support equipment is inspected at least every six months, and other equipment annually or as the schedule states. Results are logged. Equipment that fails inspection is tagged and removed from service until it is repaired.

### 6.3 Repairs

Staff who find a problem with a device remove it from use, label it, and report it to Clinical Engineering. Repairs are made by Clinical Engineering or by an approved service provider. Records of repairs are kept for the life of the equipment.

### 6.4 Recalls and safety alerts

Clinical Engineering monitors FDA recalls, alerts and notices from manufacturers. When there is a recall, Clinical Engineering identifies the affected devices within one business day, tells the departments and removes devices from use if required. Patient safety events involving devices are reported to the FDA as the Safe Medical Devices Act requires, within 10 working days for a death or serious injury.

### 6.5 Cybersecurity

The Information Security Office keeps an inventory of connected devices, scans them regularly, applies patches in coordination with manufacturers, and isolates unpatched devices on separate network segments. Devices that cannot be secured are retired or have compensating controls approved by the Chief Information Security Officer.

## 7. Replacement and Disposal

### 7.1 Replacement planning

Clinical Engineering tells Finance each year which equipment is nearing the end of its useful life, based on age, maintenance cost, parts availability and manufacturer support. The list informs the capital plan.

### 7.2 Disposal

Equipment that is no longer needed is removed from service by Clinical Engineering. It is cleaned, any stored patient information is erased in line with NIST Special Publication 800-88, and the asset record is updated. Equipment in good condition may be redeployed, sold, donated or traded in. Items with hazardous parts are disposed of under the Environmental Health and Safety procedures and California law. Equipment is never given away or sold without Clinical Engineering confirming that the data have been cleared.

## 7A. Special Categories

### 7A.1 Information technology and software as a medical device

Software that is itself a medical device, or that drives clinical decisions, such as decision support, artificial intelligence image analysis and remote monitoring applications, is evaluated by the Value Analysis Committee regardless of its cost. The Clinical Informatics team assesses whether the software is accurate across the patient populations Bayview serves, how it is validated, how its performance will be monitored after go-live, and how clinicians can override it. Information Security reviews the software before it connects to any Bayview system.

### 7A.2 Imaging and radiation equipment

Purchases of imaging equipment include a shielding design by a qualified medical physicist, a plan for dose management, a plan for quality assurance and a plan for connection to the picture archiving system. The Radiation Safety Officer must approve the plan before the order is placed. Acceptance testing, including image quality tests, is carried out by a physicist and recorded.

### 7A.3 Surgical robots and high-cost technology

Because surgical robots and other high-cost technologies carry substantial training demands and long-term costs, a proposal includes a surgeon training plan, credentialing criteria set by the Department of Surgery, expected case volumes, a financial analysis that includes disposables and service, and an outcomes tracking plan. The Board's Quality Committee is told of any purchase above $1,000,000.

### 7A.4 Point-of-care testing devices

Devices for testing at the bedside are approved by the Laboratory Director. Operators must show competency before use, results must flow into the electronic health record, and quality control records are kept as required by the Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments.

### 7A.5 Beds, mattresses and patient lifts

Clinical Engineering and Nursing evaluate beds, support surfaces and lifts for pressure injury prevention, fall prevention, bariatric needs, ease of cleaning and caregiver ergonomics. Standard models are used in each unit type, and any exceptions are approved by the Chief Nursing Officer.

### 7A.6 Sustainability

Evaluators consider the environmental cost of equipment, including energy use, anesthetic gases, single-use components and packaging. Where clinically equivalent options exist, the option with the lower environmental cost is preferred. Single-use devices that are reprocessed by FDA-registered reprocessors may be considered if Infection Prevention and the clinical department agree.

---

## 8. Monitoring and Reporting

Clinical Engineering reports each quarter to the Value Analysis Committee and Safety Committee on equipment uptime, completion of preventive maintenance, repair times, recalls and incidents. Supply Chain Services reports annually on spending by category, savings, standardization and compliance with this policy. Internal Audit tests a sample of purchases each year.

---

## 9. Exceptions and Enforcement

Exceptions to the thresholds and processes in this policy may be approved by the Chief Operating Officer on the recommendation of the Value Analysis Committee, in writing, and only in an emergency or where only one supplier exists. Any exception is recorded in the procurement file. A purchase made without following this policy may be refused payment, and the person who made it may be disciplined under the corrective action policy.
