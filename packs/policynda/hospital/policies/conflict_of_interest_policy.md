# Conflict of Interest Policy

**Bayview Health Partners**
Policy owner: Office of Compliance and Ethics
Applies to: directors, officers, employees, medical staff members, researchers and volunteers
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Next scheduled review: January 1, 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Bayview Health Partners ("Bayview") is a California non-profit public benefit corporation that holds charitable assets in trust for the community. The people who govern and work for Bayview must therefore act in its interest and not for personal gain. Conflicts of interest, actual or apparent, can damage trust, distort clinical and business judgment, and expose Bayview to penalties under tax-exemption rules, the federal Anti-Kickback Statute, the Stark physician self-referral law, California Corporations Code sections 5233 and 5227, and the research financial disclosure rules of the Public Health Service and the Food and Drug Administration.

This policy describes what a conflict is, how people disclose conflicts, how they are reviewed and managed, and what happens when the rules are broken.

### 1.2 Scope

The policy applies to the following "covered persons":

- members of the Board of Directors and its committees;
- officers and senior leaders;
- all employees, with the extra disclosure duties set out below for those in positions of influence;
- physicians and advanced practice providers on the medical staff, whether employed or independent;
- investigators and research staff; and
- volunteers who sit on committees.

It also applies to a covered person's family members, defined as spouse or domestic partner, parents, children, siblings, grandparents, grandchildren, and the spouses and partners of those people, as well as anyone who lives in the covered person's household.

---

## 2. Definitions

**Conflict of interest** means a situation in which a covered person's financial, professional or personal interest, or that of a family member, could influence, or appear to influence, the person's judgment or actions in doing his or her duty to Bayview.

**Financial interest** means any of the following: ownership or investment interest (other than publicly traded mutual funds), a compensation arrangement including salary, consulting fees, honoraria, royalties, or in-kind payments, a position as director, officer, partner or employee of an outside organization, and intellectual property rights.

**Significant financial interest** for research purposes means financial interests that, together with those of a spouse and dependent children, exceed $5,000 in the preceding 12 months from a publicly traded entity, or any equity interest in a non-publicly traded entity, or any royalty income related to the research.

**Gift** means anything of value given without full payment, including meals, tickets, travel, lodging, services, discounts and loans.

**Interested person** means a covered person who has a conflict of interest in a matter under consideration.

**Compliance Officer** means the Chief Compliance Officer or designee.

## 3. Types of Conflicts

### 3.1 Financial and business relationships

A conflict may arise when a covered person or family member has an ownership interest in, serves as an officer or director of, is employed by, or is paid by an organization that does or seeks to do business with Bayview, competes with Bayview, or is a referral source. It may also arise when a covered person takes part in the decision to buy a product from a company in which he or she holds shares.

### 3.2 Gifts and entertainment

Covered persons may not accept gifts from any current or prospective vendor, supplier, referral source or patient family that could look like an effort to influence a decision. Gifts of nominal value, defined as items worth $50 or less in a single instance and no more than $150 from one source in a calendar year, such as a box of cookies shared by a department, are allowed if they are not cash or cash equivalents. Meals provided in connection with an educational event are allowed if they are modest and incidental. Industry-sponsored meals in clinical areas are not allowed except for an occasional modest working lunch for an educational session approved by the department chair. Pharmaceutical and device samples may not be accepted for personal use.

### 3.3 Outside activities and employment

A covered person must disclose and seek approval for outside employment, consulting, teaching or board service that could take time from the person's work at Bayview, use Bayview resources, or compete with Bayview. Bayview resources, including staff time, equipment, supplies, facilities and the Bayview name, may not be used for an outside business. Employees may not recruit Bayview patients to a private practice.

### 3.4 Family members and personal relationships

A covered person may not supervise, hire, evaluate, set pay for or approve payments to a family member or a person with whom he or she has a romantic or close financial relationship. When such a relationship exists, the matter is referred to Human Resources and the Compliance Officer, who will arrange a different reporting line.

### 3.5 Corporate opportunities

A covered person may not take for himself or herself a business opportunity that Bayview is pursuing or is likely to pursue, or that the person learned of through his or her position at Bayview, without first offering it to the Board and receiving written approval.

### 3.6 Research and industry relationships

Investigators must report significant financial interests related to their research responsibilities to the Conflict of Interest Committee before applying for funding, at least once a year and within 30 days of discovering or acquiring a new one. Investigators with an interest related to a study may not take part in selecting participants, obtaining consent or analyzing results unless a management plan allows it. Bayview posts on its website information about financial conflicts that have been found to relate to federally funded research, as federal rules require. Details are set out in the Research Collaboration Policy.

### 3.7 Industry payments and public reporting

Many Bayview physicians receive payments from manufacturers. Bayview reminds covered persons that the Open Payments program (Sunshine Act) makes those payments public, and that they should check their records on the CMS website each year and correct errors.

## 4. Disclosure and Review

### 4.1 Duty to disclose

Covered persons must disclose any actual or potential conflict as soon as they become aware of it, and in any case before taking part in a decision that it could affect. Directors, officers, and employees at manager level and above, members of the medical staff and investigators also complete an annual disclosure statement in the compliance system by January 31. New covered persons complete a statement within 30 days of joining.

### 4.2 Review process

The Compliance Officer reviews each disclosure and sorts it into one of three categories: no conflict, a manageable conflict or a conflict that cannot be managed. A manageable conflict is addressed with a written management plan. The plan may require one or more of the following: removal from a decision, divestiture, an independent review of the person's work, limits on the outside activity, additional disclosure to patients or participants, or oversight by another person. Conflicts of directors and officers are reviewed by the Governance and Nominating Committee of the Board. Conflicts of investigators are reviewed by the Conflict of Interest Committee.

### 4.3 Decisions by the Board

When a transaction involves a director or officer, or a family member, the disinterested members of the Board or the committee must decide, after the interested person has disclosed all material facts and left the room. Before approving a transaction in which a director has a financial interest, the Board must find that the transaction is fair and reasonable to Bayview and in its interest, that Bayview could not have obtained a more advantageous arrangement with reasonable effort, and that it has considered alternatives. For compensation of executives and certain transactions with insiders, the Board follows the rebuttable presumption procedure of the Internal Revenue Code and its regulations: approval by independent persons, reliance on comparability data and contemporaneous documentation of the basis for the decision.

### 4.4 Records

The Compliance Office keeps disclosure statements, management plans and decisions for at least 10 years. Minutes of the Board record that the conflict was disclosed, that the interested person left the room, and how the vote went.

## 5. Specific Rules for Key Groups

### 5.1 Directors and officers

Directors and officers owe duties of loyalty and care to Bayview. They must not vote on matters in which they have an interest, may not use their positions for personal gain, and must disclose their other board seats. No more than 49 percent of the Board may be "interested persons", meaning persons who have received compensation from Bayview in the previous 12 months or are related to such persons, as California Corporations Code section 5227 provides.

### 5.2 Purchasing and procurement staff

Staff who select vendors, evaluate bids or approve purchases must complete a disclosure for each significant purchasing decision, must not accept any gift from a vendor in the selection process, and must withdraw from any decision in which they have a personal interest. The Clinical Equipment Procurement Policy applies these rules to equipment decisions.

### 5.3 Medical staff

Members of the medical staff must disclose financial relationships with Bayview, including those that are covered by Stark exceptions, and must follow the credentialing rules about disclosure. Referral decisions must be based on the patient's needs and choice. No payment may be offered, requested or received in return for a referral.

### 5.4 Faculty and teaching roles

Residents, students and faculty who work at Bayview must not allow industry relationships to affect teaching content. Educational events may be supported by industry only through unrestricted grants that are managed by the Graduate Medical Education office, which makes sure that speakers, topics and materials are chosen without sponsor influence.

## 6. Reporting Concerns and Enforcement

### 6.1 Reporting

Anyone who believes a conflict has not been disclosed or managed properly should tell a manager, the Compliance Officer or the Compliance Hotline, which is available around the clock and accepts anonymous reports. Bayview prohibits retaliation against any person who makes a report in good faith or who takes part in an investigation.

### 6.2 Investigation

The Compliance Officer will promptly look into each report and may involve Internal Audit, Human Resources and the Office of the General Counsel. The covered person will be given a chance to explain. The results are reported to the Governance and Nominating Committee when a director or officer is concerned, and otherwise to the Compliance Committee.

### 6.3 Consequences

A covered person who fails to disclose a conflict, who gives false information, or who ignores a management plan may be disciplined, up to and including removal from the Board, termination of employment, loss of medical staff privileges, repayment of any improper benefit and, where the law requires, referral to a regulator. Violations by research staff may also be reported to federal funding agencies.

## 6A. Worked Examples

**A vendor offers tickets.** A purchasing analyst who is preparing a bid for laboratory analyzers is offered two tickets to a baseball game by one of the bidders. The analyst must decline, because the ticket value exceeds the nominal limit and the bidder is in a selection process. The analyst should tell the Compliance Office about the offer.

**A physician owns shares.** A surgeon on the Value Analysis Committee owns shares worth $20,000 in a company that makes a surgical stapler that is under review. The surgeon must disclose the interest before the review, leave the room while the committee discusses the stapler, and not vote on it. The Compliance Officer records the disclosure and the recusal in the minutes.

**A manager's relative applies.** A department manager's brother applies for a position in the manager's department. The manager must tell Human Resources, may not take part in interviewing or hiring him, and must not supervise him if he is hired. Human Resources arranges for another leader to be his supervisor.

**An outside board seat.** A vice president is invited to join the board of a company that sells billing software to hospitals. The vice president must request approval in writing. The Compliance Officer is likely to approve only if the vice president will have no involvement in Bayview decisions about billing software, and the Governance and Nominating Committee is told.

**A grateful patient.** A patient gives a nurse a card with a $100 gift card to thank her for care. The nurse politely declines the gift card, which is cash equivalent, and invites the patient to make a donation to the Bayview Foundation. Cards and small non-cash items, such as flowers shared with the team, may be accepted.

**A research sponsor offers consulting.** An investigator running a trial for a device company is offered consulting work with the same company. The investigator must disclose the offer to the Conflict of Interest Committee before agreeing. The Committee may approve the work only with a management plan, which may limit the investigator's role in the trial.

---

## 6B. Roles of Specific Committees

The **Governance and Nominating Committee** reviews the disclosures of directors and officers and decides on management plans for them. The **Conflict of Interest Committee** is made up of three physicians, two researchers, a representative of Finance and the Compliance Officer, and meets monthly. It reviews research-related disclosures and recommends management plans. The **Compliance Committee** receives the annual summary of all disclosures and the results of testing. Committee members must themselves disclose conflicts and are recused from their own matters.

---

## 7. Training and Monitoring

Covered persons receive training on this policy when they join and every two years, and Board members receive an annual briefing. The Compliance Office tests disclosures against the vendor master file, the payroll system and the Open Payments database every year to look for undisclosed relationships. Results are given to the Compliance Committee, and trends are included in the annual compliance report to the Board.

---

## 8. Review of the Policy

The Compliance Office reviews this policy each year with the Office of the General Counsel and presents proposed changes to the Governance and Nominating Committee for approval. Exceptions to this policy may be made only by the Governance and Nominating Committee, in writing, and only if the exception is consistent with law.
