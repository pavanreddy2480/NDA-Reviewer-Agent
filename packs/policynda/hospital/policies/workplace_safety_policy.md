# Workplace Safety Policy

**Bayview Health Partners**
Policy owner: Environment of Care and Employee Safety Department
Applies to: all workforce members, outside workers and visitors at Bayview facilities
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Next scheduled review: January 1, 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Health care is among the most hazardous industries in the United States. Nurses, technicians, transporters, environmental services staff and others are exposed to infectious disease, sharps, hazardous drugs, heavy lifting, violence, radiation and slips and falls. Bayview Health Partners ("Bayview") is committed to providing a safe and healthy workplace, and this policy sets out how it meets the requirements of the Occupational Safety and Health Act, the California Division of Occupational Safety and Health (Cal/OSHA) standards, including the Injury and Illness Prevention Program required by Title 8 California Code of Regulations section 3203, the Aerosol Transmissible Diseases standard, the Workplace Violence Prevention in Health Care standard (Title 8 section 3342), and The Joint Commission's Environment of Care standards.

### 1.2 Scope

The policy applies to every employee, physician, student, volunteer and outside worker on any Bayview property. Vendors and outside workers who work on site must follow the policy as well as any more specific rules that apply to their tasks. Patients and visitors are asked to follow signs and instructions from staff.

---

## 2. Definitions and Responsibilities

### 2.1 Definitions

**Hazard** means any condition, practice or substance that could cause injury or illness.

**Near miss** means an event that could have caused injury or illness but did not.

**Recordable injury** means a work-related injury or illness that must be recorded on the OSHA 300 log: one that results in death, days away from work, restricted work, transfer to another job, medical treatment beyond first aid or loss of consciousness.

**Personal protective equipment (PPE)** means gloves, gowns, masks, respirators, eye protection, face shields, hearing protection and similar items.

**Safety Committee** means the multidisciplinary committee, chaired by the Chief Operating Officer, that oversees workplace safety.

### 2.2 Responsibilities

**Leaders** make sure that their areas are safe, that employees are trained, that incidents are reported and investigated, and that resources are available.

**Employee Safety** runs the Injury and Illness Prevention Program, conducts inspections, maintains the OSHA logs, provides training and investigates serious events.

**Employee Health** gives immunizations, tests for tuberculosis, fits respirators, manages exposures and works with the managers on return to work.

**Security** responds to violence and threats, and manages access control.

**Facilities** maintain fire protection, electrical and ventilation systems, medical gas, emergency power and water systems.

**Employees** follow safe procedures, use PPE and equipment correctly, report hazards, injuries and near misses at once, and take part in training.

## 3. Injury and Illness Prevention Program

### 3.1 Hazard identification

Employee Safety and departmental leaders inspect each work area at least every quarter, using a checklist for the particular area. Employees may report hazards at any time through the safety reporting system or by calling Employee Safety, without fear of reprisal. Hazards that pose an immediate danger are corrected or the area is closed until correction is complete. Others are given a risk rating and a target date for correction: 24 hours for high-risk items, 30 days for medium-risk items and 90 days for low-risk items.

### 3.2 Incident reporting and investigation

Injuries, illnesses, exposures and near misses are reported to the supervisor and to Employee Safety on the same shift. A supervisor completes an incident report within 24 hours. Employee Safety investigates every recordable injury and every near miss that could have been serious, using a root cause method, and sends a report to the leader and to the Safety Committee. Serious injuries, defined as deaths, in-patient hospitalizations, amputations and loss of an eye, are reported to Cal/OSHA within 8 hours.

### 3.3 Training

New employees receive safety orientation before beginning work and a department-specific session within 30 days. Annual training covers fire safety, hazard communication, bloodborne pathogens, ergonomics, workplace violence prevention, emergency codes and PPE. Employees who work with hazardous drugs, radiation, lasers, compressed gases or in confined spaces receive additional instruction. Training records are kept for at least 3 years.

### 3.4 Communication and employee participation

Employee Safety publishes a monthly safety bulletin, holds a safety huddle in every unit each week, and posts the results of inspections on departmental boards. Each facility has a safety committee that includes front-line staff chosen by their colleagues. Employees may ask for copies of the injury logs, and employee representatives may view them.

## 4. Specific Hazards

### 4.1 Bloodborne pathogens and sharps

Bayview follows the Bloodborne Pathogens standard and the California Sharps Injury Prevention requirements. Safety-engineered devices are used wherever they are available. Sharps are discarded at the point of use into puncture-resistant containers that are replaced when they are three-quarters full. Needles are not recapped by hand. Employees are offered the hepatitis B vaccine free of charge. After an exposure, employees wash the site, report to Employee Health at once, and receive evaluation and any needed prophylaxis within two hours. The source patient is tested with consent as California law provides.

### 4.2 Infection prevention and respiratory protection

Standard precautions apply to every patient. Isolation precautions, such as contact, droplet and airborne, are set out in the Infection Prevention manual. Staff who may be exposed to aerosol transmissible diseases are fit-tested annually for N95 respirators or are issued powered air-purifying respirators. Airborne infection isolation rooms are checked for negative pressure each day when in use. Employees with symptoms of a communicable illness must stay home and call Employee Health.

### 4.3 Workplace violence prevention

Bayview maintains a written Workplace Violence Prevention Plan, as required by California Labor Code section 6401.8, with the participation of employees. The plan includes assessment of risk for each unit, controls such as panic buttons, alarm systems, security presence, controlled entry, and furniture arrangements that reduce risk, training for all staff in recognizing and de-escalating aggression, and a log of every violent incident. Employees who have been threatened or injured receive support from Employee Health and the Employee Assistance Program, and may report their case to law enforcement. Retaliation against anyone who reports violence is prohibited.

### 4.4 Safe patient handling and ergonomics

Manual lifting of patients is reduced to the minimum. Lifts, slide sheets, transfer belts and other devices are provided on every unit. Employees receive training on how to use them. Any lift of a patient who cannot bear weight requires a mechanical lift or at least two trained staff. Offices are assessed for ergonomics on request.

### 4.5 Hazardous drugs and chemicals

Pharmacy and nursing handle antineoplastic and other hazardous drugs under USP 800. Closed-system transfer devices, ventilated cabinets and chemotherapy gloves and gowns are used. Spill kits are on each unit that uses these drugs. Safety Data Sheets are available for all chemicals on the intranet and in print, and containers are labeled. Employees who may be exposed to formaldehyde, glutaraldehyde, ethylene oxide, waste anesthetic gases and similar substances take part in exposure monitoring as required.

### 4.6 Radiation, lasers and magnetic fields

Radiation safety is directed by the Radiation Safety Officer, who sets dose limits, monitors badges, controls access to rooms and surveys equipment. Laser safety officers set controls for surgical and cosmetic lasers, including eye protection and warning signs. Strong magnetic fields in MRI suites require screening of everyone who enters and strict controls on ferromagnetic objects.

### 4.7 Slips, trips and falls

Floors are kept clean and dry. Spills are cleaned at once or marked with a sign until cleaned. Cords are not run across walkways. Stairwells and exits are kept clear and lit. Employees wear slip-resistant shoes in kitchens, operating rooms and wet areas.

## 5. Emergency Preparedness

### 5.1 Fire safety

Each facility has a fire plan using the RACE method: rescue, alarm, contain, extinguish or evacuate. Fire drills are held at least once every quarter on each shift in each building. Fire doors are closed, exits are kept clear and extinguishers are inspected monthly. Hot work such as welding requires a permit from Facilities. Smoking is prohibited on all Bayview property.

### 5.2 Emergency codes

Bayview uses standardized emergency codes announced overhead and by text message: Code Red (fire), Code Blue (medical emergency), Code Silver (person with a weapon), Code Pink (infant or child abduction), Code Gray (combative person), Code Orange (hazardous material spill), Code Yellow (disaster) and Code Triage. Cards listing the codes are on all badges.

### 5.3 Disaster preparedness

The Emergency Operations Plan is based on the Hospital Incident Command System. It covers earthquakes, wildfires, power failures, pandemic and mass casualty events. Each hospital maintains supplies for at least 96 hours, runs a full-scale drill twice a year, and takes part in the county health care coalition. Employees are told how to prepare their own families so that they can report for work during a disaster.

### 5.4 Utilities failure

Emergency generators are tested monthly under load and serve the critical areas. Medical gas shut-off valves are labeled and unit staff are trained to use them. Water management plans, including Legionella control, are maintained by Facilities.

## 6. Injured Employees and Return to Work

An employee who is hurt at work reports immediately to the supervisor. Employee Health evaluates the injury and sends the employee to the designated workers' compensation clinic if needed. The claim form (DWC-1) is given within one working day. Employee Safety and Human Resources work with the employee and the treating provider to identify temporary modified duty so that the employee may return to work as soon as it is safe. Employees who cannot return are told about their rights to leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act and the California Family Rights Act and are considered for reasonable accommodation under the Americans with Disabilities Act and the Fair Employment and Housing Act.

## 7. Outside Workers, Visitors and Volunteers

Outside workers who do construction or maintenance at Bayview follow the Infection Control Risk Assessment and Interim Life Safety Measures procedures. Dust barriers, negative air machines and pressure monitors are used to protect patients when work is carried out near care areas. Outside workers sign in and out with Security, wear identification and report any injuries to Facilities at once. Visitors are told where to go in an emergency, are asked not to enter restricted areas and must follow screening requirements during outbreaks. Volunteers receive training in line with their duties.

## 7A. Wellness, Fatigue and Support

### 7A.1 Employee assistance and peer support

Bayview offers free, private counseling through the Employee Assistance Program, which is open at all hours. The Peer Support Team, made up of trained nurses, physicians and other staff, provides support to employees who have been through a traumatic event, such as the death of a child, a serious error, a violent incident or a suicide. Employees do not need their supervisor's permission to use the programs, and their use does not appear in personnel files.

### 7A.2 Fatigue and scheduling

Long shifts and short rest periods raise the risk of errors and injuries. Managers schedule so that employees have at least 10 hours off between shifts whenever feasible, avoid more than three consecutive 12-hour shifts, and let employees take meal and rest breaks as the California Industrial Welfare Commission wage orders require. Employees who feel too tired to drive home after a long shift may use the on-call rooms or request a ride home through the safe ride program.

### 7A.3 Temperature, noise and indoor air

Facilities monitors temperature and humidity in patient care areas and workspaces and responds to complaints within one business day. During periods of high outdoor heat, Bayview follows the indoor heat illness requirements of Cal/OSHA for areas without climate control, such as kitchens and loading docks, and provides water and cooling breaks. When wildfire smoke raises the air quality index above 150, outdoor staff are offered N95 respirators and indoor air handling is adjusted. Noise levels are measured where employees work near loud equipment, and hearing protection is provided when levels exceed the action level.

### 7A.4 Pregnancy and reproductive hazards

Employees who are pregnant or planning pregnancy can talk in confidence with Employee Health about hazards such as radiation, anesthetic gases, hazardous drugs and infectious diseases. Modified duty is available, and nobody will be penalized for asking. Employee Health works with the employee's physician to set suitable restrictions.

### 7A.5 Commuting and parking safety

Security patrols parking structures at all times and offers escorts to vehicles at night. Lighting is checked each month. Employees who feel unsafe may call Security from any house phone or emergency call box. Bicycle storage and showers are available at the main hospitals.

### 7A.6 Safety recognition

Each quarter, the Safety Committee recognizes departments with the best injury prevention results, the best near-miss reporting and the most improved hazard correction rates. Recognition is not based on low injury counts alone, because that could discourage reporting.

---

## 8. Monitoring, Enforcement and Review

Employee Safety reports each quarter to the Safety Committee and each year to the Board on injury rates, claims, inspections, corrective actions, drills and training completion. Targets are set each year. The Environment of Care rounds are held every quarter, and results are given to leaders.

Employees who fail to follow safety rules may be coached, retrained and, for serious or repeated violations, disciplined under the corrective action policy. No employee will be disciplined for refusing to do a task that he or she reasonably believes poses an imminent danger, if the employee has told the supervisor and cannot be reassured. Bayview prohibits retaliation against employees who report hazards or injuries.
