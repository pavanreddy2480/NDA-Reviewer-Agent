# Confidentiality Agreement Policy

**Bayview Health Partners**
Policy owner: Office of the General Counsel
Applies to: all Bayview hospitals, clinics, research units and shared services
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Next scheduled review: January 1, 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Bayview Health Partners ("Bayview") is a California non-profit hospital group. In the course of caring for patients, running our clinics, purchasing technology and billing services, and collaborating with academic and industry researchers, we regularly receive information from outsiders and give information to them. Some of that information is highly sensitive: patient records, financial results, clinical protocols, security architecture and unpublished research findings. This policy sets out how Bayview handles stand-alone confidentiality agreements, which this policy calls "agreements" for short, so that the people who negotiate them know what a good agreement looks like and who must be asked when an agreement departs from it.

The policy is meant to be used by non-lawyers. A department manager who is asked by a vendor to sign a confidentiality agreement before a product demonstration should be able to read this policy and understand what the agreement needs to say, who can help, and how long the review should take. Legal staff use the same policy to review non-standard drafts and to explain their decisions consistently.

### 1.2 Scope

This policy applies to every workforce member who negotiates, reviews, signs or administers a confidentiality agreement on behalf of Bayview or any Bayview-controlled entity, including employed physicians, residents, nurses, administrators, researchers and contractors who act with Bayview's authority. It applies to agreements in which Bayview is the party sharing information, the party receiving information, or both.

It does not apply to the following, which are governed by other Bayview policies:

- Business associate agreements required by HIPAA, which are covered by the Business Associate and Vendor Policy.
- Terms of employment and the workforce handbook, which are covered by Human Resources policies.
- Patient authorizations and consents, which are covered by the Patient Rights Policy and the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy.
- Clinical trial sponsor agreements and data use agreements, which are covered by the Research Collaboration Policy, except for the stand-alone confidentiality agreement that often precedes them.

### 1.3 Relationship to other policies

This policy should be read together with the Business Associate and Vendor Policy, the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy, the Delegation of Authority and Signature Policy, the Research Collaboration Policy and the Medical Records Retention Policy. Where those policies state a requirement that bears directly on a confidentiality agreement, this policy points to them rather than repeating them. If two Bayview policies appear to conflict, the Office of the General Counsel decides which applies and records the decision in the exception register described in Section 9.

## 2. Definitions and Roles

### 2.1 Definitions

**Agreement** means a written confidentiality agreement, whether titled non-disclosure agreement, confidentiality agreement, confidential disclosure agreement or something similar, that Bayview proposes to sign.

**Counterparty** means the individual or organization on the other side of the agreement. Counterparties are classified as vendors, customers, partners or employees. A vendor supplies goods or services to Bayview. A customer buys goods or services from Bayview, for example a payer or an employer health plan. A partner collaborates with Bayview on a joint project, including a research or education project. An employee or other workforce member signs an agreement in his or her individual capacity.

**Personal data** means protected health information as defined by HIPAA, and any other information that identifies or could reasonably be used to identify a living individual, including information covered by the California Consumer Privacy Act and the California Confidentiality of Medical Information Act.

**Disclosing party** and **receiving party** have their ordinary meaning. In a mutual agreement Bayview is both. When this policy refers to "the recipient" it means whichever party is receiving information under the clause being discussed.

**Workforce member** has the meaning given in the HIPAA Privacy Rule: employees, volunteers, trainees and others whose conduct is under Bayview's direct control.

### 2.2 Roles and responsibilities

**Office of the General Counsel.** Owns this policy, maintains the template library, answers questions and reviews every agreement that is not on an unmodified Bayview template. The General Counsel is the final authority on interpretation.

**Chief Compliance Officer.** Is the approving officer for exceptions that relate to the duration of an agreement, as described in Section 6. The Chief Compliance Officer also receives the quarterly exception report.

**Chief Privacy Officer.** Advises on any agreement that involves personal data and is the approving officer for exceptions that relate to personal data, as described in the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy.

**Chief Information Security Officer.** Advises on agreements that involve access to Bayview systems or networks.

**Business sponsor.** The workforce member or department leader who needs the agreement. The business sponsor identifies the counterparty, describes in one or two sentences what information will be exchanged and why, and is responsible for keeping the information inside the stated purpose.

**Contract Administration.** A small team in Supply Chain Services that logs agreements, tracks expiry dates, and stores the signed copies in the agreement repository.

## 3. Core Protections Bayview Requires

This section describes the protections that every agreement needs, whatever the counterparty and whatever information is being exchanged. A draft that does not provide these protections should not be signed until it has been corrected or an exception has been recorded under Section 9.

### 3.1 Verbal disclosures

Every agreement must expressly extend its protection to information that is conveyed verbally, including remarks made during case conferences, committee meetings, site visits and telephone calls.

Clinicians and researchers often share their most useful observations in conversation, long before anything is written down. An agreement that protects only documents and files leaves those conversations exposed. Business sponsors should not rely on a promise to follow up in writing; the agreement itself has to cover what is said.

### 3.2 Legally compelled disclosure

If a counterparty is compelled by a statute, regulation, subpoena or other legal process to disclose our information, the agreement must require it to give Bayview advance notice of that demand.

The purpose of the notice is to give Bayview a chance to seek a protective order or to ask the requesting authority to narrow its request. For information that includes personal data, the notice also lets the Privacy Office decide whether Bayview itself has a notification or accounting obligation to the affected patients. Business sponsors who receive a legal demand notice from a counterparty should forward it to the Office of the General Counsel the same day.

### 3.3 Purpose limitation

Each agreement must restrict the recipient to using our information solely for the specific purpose that the agreement describes.

Business sponsors are responsible for writing a purpose statement that is specific enough to be useful. "To evaluate a proposed revenue cycle software platform for the Bayview East campus" is a good statement. "For business purposes" is not. If the purpose changes during the relationship, the agreement should be amended before any new use begins.

### 3.4 Intake and triage

The following procedure applies to all agreement requests.

1. The business sponsor submits a request through the Legal Intake portal, giving the counterparty's legal name, its classification under Section 2.1, a short statement of purpose, whether any personal data will be exchanged, and the date by which the agreement is needed.
2. A legal intake analyst confirms the classification and, if the request is incomplete, returns it within one business day.
3. If the counterparty will accept the Bayview template without changes, the analyst issues it and the business sponsor sends it for signature in accordance with the Delegation of Authority and Signature Policy.
4. If the counterparty proposes its own paper, the analyst compares the draft with this policy and with the redline checklist in the template library, and returns a markup to the business sponsor. Most reviews are completed within three business days.
5. Anything that the analyst cannot resolve is referred to a staff attorney, and anything involving an exception is routed under Section 9.

Under no circumstances may a workforce member sign an agreement, or accept the counterparty's terms by clicking through a web form, without completing this procedure.

### 3.5 Standard templates

The Office of the General Counsel maintains three Bayview templates: a mutual template for general business discussions, a one-way template for situations in which Bayview is the only disclosing party, and a short research template for early scientific conversations. Each template is reviewed annually and updated for changes in this policy and in the law. Business sponsors should always offer the Bayview template first. Offering it first shortens negotiation, because Legal does not have to review it again.

## 4. Defining and Identifying Protected Information

### 4.1 Scope of the definition

In agreements with vendors and partners, the definition of protected information may not be confined to technical material, since most of what Bayview shares is clinical, financial or operational.

Vendors in particular often propose a definition drawn from the technology industry, which refers only to source code, schematics, specifications and similar items. For a hospital, the information that matters is different: patient volumes, payer rates, staffing plans, quality metrics, the layout of our clinical networks, and the findings of internal reviews. A narrow definition would leave all of that unprotected.

### 4.2 Marking

Whenever personal data will be exchanged, the agreement may not make protection conditional on the information being labelled, stamped or otherwise identified as confidential in advance.

Patient information is rarely stamped. It appears in screens, reports, voicemail, email chains and hallway conversations, and a marking requirement would turn every unlabelled item into a potential gap. Business sponsors should not agree to a clause that obliges Bayview to mark information as a condition of protection. Counterparties may ask Bayview to identify the kinds of information it intends to share; describing categories in the purpose statement is acceptable.

### 4.3 Working with the definition in practice

When reviewing a counterparty's draft, analysts read the definition together with the exclusions that follow it. The exclusions normally cover information that is already public, information the recipient already held, and information the recipient lawfully obtained elsewhere. These are routine. Analysts raise a question only if an exclusion is so wide that it could swallow the definition, for example one that excludes anything that "could be found in published literature" even where Bayview's use of it is novel. Disagreements are escalated to a staff attorney.

## 5. When the Relationship Ends

### 5.1 Return and destruction

On termination or expiry, the agreement must oblige the recipient either to return or to destroy the information it received from Bayview.

For the timing of return and for the rules that apply where personal data is concerned, see the Business Associate and Vendor Policy. For the way these obligations interact with records that Bayview itself must retain, and for how long other confidentiality obligations continue after the end of the relationship, see the Medical Records Retention Policy.

### 5.2 Certification and follow-up

When a recipient confirms that it has returned or destroyed the information, the business sponsor files the confirmation with Contract Administration. Contract Administration closes the agreement in the repository only after the confirmation is on file. If no confirmation has arrived after the due date, Contract Administration sends two reminders at ten-day intervals and then refers the matter to the Office of the General Counsel.

### 5.3 Information Bayview must keep

Bayview itself is subject to retention obligations. A letter of confirmation from a counterparty never obliges Bayview to destroy records that the law requires it to keep, and a counterparty's destruction obligation does not extend to Bayview's own copies of the signed agreement or its correspondence. Questions about this should be directed to Health Information Management.

## 6. Duration

### 6.1 Minimum term

Protection obligations must remain in force for a minimum of 36 months, and the Chief Compliance Officer is the only person who may approve a shorter term.

A request to shorten the term must be made in writing through the exception process in Section 9. The Chief Compliance Officer considers the sensitivity of the information, how quickly it loses its value, and whether Bayview can secure its interests in another way. Where the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy sets a separate minimum for obligations that continue after termination, that policy should also be consulted.

### 6.2 Maximum term

No agreement may set the protection period at more than 120 months.

Very long terms create tracking burdens, and counterparties often resist them. Where Bayview believes that a particular item of information needs protection for longer, the business sponsor should raise it with the Office of the General Counsel. The minimum and maximum should be read together: the permitted range for the term of protection is from the figure in Section 6.1 to the figure in this section.

### 6.3 Calendar and renewals

Contract Administration records the start date and the expiry date of every agreement in the repository and sends an expiry reminder to the business sponsor ninety days beforehand. The business sponsor decides whether the relationship has ended or whether a new agreement is needed.

## 7. Administration

### 7.1 Signature authority

Who may sign an agreement for Bayview is set out in the Delegation of Authority and Signature Policy. The business sponsor may not sign unless the policy says so. A signed agreement without proper authority is referred to the General Counsel for ratification or withdrawal.

### 7.2 Repository and records

The signed copy, the final draft, the redline history and any exception decision are kept in the agreement repository. Records are retained for the period set out in the Medical Records Retention Policy and the corporate records schedule. Access to the repository is restricted to Legal, Contract Administration, Compliance and the business sponsor of each agreement.

### 7.3 Training

All managers and above, and everyone in Supply Chain Services, Information Technology, Research Administration and Revenue Cycle, complete a thirty-minute online module on this policy within sixty days of starting in the role and every two years thereafter. The Office of the General Counsel delivers live briefings for research investigators twice a year. Attendance is recorded in the learning management system.

### 7.4 Common questions

*Can I share information before the agreement is signed?* No. Wait until the agreement is signed. If the matter is urgent, ask Legal Intake to expedite.

*Does the policy apply to a counterparty's online portal?* Yes. Clicking "I agree" is signing.

*Who do I call if a counterparty complains about a Bayview term?* Call Legal Intake. Do not negotiate legal terms yourself.

*What if the counterparty is a government agency?* Government agencies often cannot sign Bayview's template. Send the agency's form to Legal Intake.

## 8. Monitoring and Reporting

Contract Administration prepares a quarterly report for the Chief Compliance Officer and the General Counsel. The report lists the number of agreements signed by counterparty classification, the average time to completion, the number of exceptions requested and granted, and any signed agreements that did not follow this policy. The Compliance Committee of the Board receives an annual summary.

Internal Audit reviews a random sample of twenty-five signed agreements each year to confirm that the intake procedure was followed, the signatures are authorized and the confirmation of return or destruction was filed. Findings are reported to the Audit Committee.

## 9. Exceptions

### 9.1 How to request an exception

If a business need requires an agreement that departs from this policy, the business sponsor submits an exception request through Legal Intake. The request states which provision cannot be met, why, what risk results and what Bayview proposes to do about the risk. Legal Intake routes the request to the officer who is authorized to decide it. That officer is named in the section of this policy, or the related policy, that contains the requirement. Where no officer is named, the General Counsel decides.

### 9.2 Recording

Every decision is recorded in the exception register, kept by the Office of the General Counsel. The record shows the date, the requester, the decision, the officer, any conditions and the expiry of the exception. Exceptions last for the life of the agreement unless the decision says otherwise. The register is used to see whether a pattern of exceptions suggests that the policy should change.

### 9.3 No informal exceptions

A verbal statement from any manager or officer does not waive a requirement of this policy. Only a recorded decision does.

---

## 10. Enforcement

Violations of this policy are handled under the Bayview corrective action policy. A workforce member who signs an agreement without authority, or who discloses information outside the agreement's purpose, may be subject to discipline up to and including termination of employment or of the relationship with Bayview. Workforce members who suspect a violation should report it to their manager, to the Office of the General Counsel or to the Compliance Hotline. Retaliation against anyone who makes a report in good faith is prohibited.
