# Anti-Bribery Policy

**Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc.**
Policy owner: Chief Compliance Officer
Policy approver: Audit Committee of the Board of Directors
Effective date: January 8, 2025
Review cycle: annually
Document number: KPM-CO-002

---

## 1. Purpose

1.1 Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc. ("Keystone") wins business by making good parts, delivering them on time and standing behind them. We do not win business by paying for favors. We never offer, give, request or accept a bribe, whether in the United States or anywhere else, whether to a government official or to someone in a private company, and whether in cash or in any other form.

1.2 This policy explains the laws that prohibit bribery and corruption, including the US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA), the Travel Act, the federal bribery statute for domestic officials, state commercial bribery laws, the UK Bribery Act 2010 where it applies, and Mexico's General Law of Administrative Responsibilities. It describes what is prohibited, which situations carry the highest risk, what approvals are needed for gifts, hospitality and other benefits, how third parties are managed and how to raise concerns.

1.3 A bribe harms the community by distorting competition, and it harms the company by exposing it to criminal penalties, debarment from government work, loss of customers and reputation damage. Companies and individuals have been prosecuted for conduct that took place in a single transaction, and the fine for a corporate violation can run to tens of millions of dollars.

## 2. Scope and Definitions

2.1 **Scope.** This policy applies to all directors, officers and employees of Keystone and its subsidiaries worldwide. It also applies to third parties that act on Keystone's behalf, such as sales agents, customs brokers, consultants, freight forwarders and distributors, to the extent that they interact with officials or customers in Keystone's name.

2.2 **Definitions.**

- **Bribe:** anything of value offered, promised, given, requested or accepted to improperly influence a decision or to secure an improper advantage.
- **Anything of value:** includes cash, cash equivalents, gifts, meals, entertainment, travel, lodging, loans, favors, employment or internships, charitable or political contributions, discounts, use of facilities, and any other benefit.
- **Government official:** any officer or employee of a government, whether at the federal, state, local or foreign level, of a government agency or a state-owned or state-controlled company (including a state-owned airline, utility or university or hospital), a public international organization, a political party, a party official or a candidate. It also includes anyone acting for or on behalf of such a body.
- **Facilitating payment:** a small payment to an official to speed up a routine, non-discretionary government action, such as processing a visa or clearing goods through customs.
- **Third party:** any person or organization that is not a Keystone employee and acts for or with Keystone.
- **Red flag:** a fact or circumstance that suggests a risk of improper payment.

## 3. Prohibited Conduct

3.1 **No bribery.** Employees and third parties must not offer, promise, authorize or give anything of value to any person, including a government official, in order to obtain or retain business, to influence an official act or decision, or to secure any improper advantage.

3.2 **No acceptance.** Employees must not request or accept anything of value from any person if it could influence, or appear to influence, a business decision. Rules on supplier gifts are in section 6 and in the Supplier Engagement Policy.

3.3 **No facilitating payments.** Keystone prohibits facilitating payments. If an employee is asked to make such a payment and fears for personal safety, the employee may make the payment and must report it to the Chief Compliance Officer as soon as it is safe to do so, so that it can be recorded correctly. A payment made under threat of imminent harm to health or safety is not a violation if it is promptly reported.

3.4 **No indirect bribery.** Employees must not do through another person what they cannot do directly. Using an agent, consultant, distributor or any other intermediary to pay a bribe is a violation. Knowledge includes willful blindness or deliberately ignoring warning signs.

3.5 **Accurate books and records.** All transactions must be recorded accurately, in reasonable detail and in the correct account. No false, misleading or incomplete entry may be made for any reason. No off-book accounts or unrecorded funds may exist. Payments must be supported by proper documentation and be approved under the Delegation of Authority register.

3.6 **Political and charitable contributions.** No Keystone funds, facilities or resources may be given to a political party, candidate or committee, in any country, without the written approval of the Chief Executive Officer and the Chief Compliance Officer. Charitable donations made at the request of an official or customer must be approved by the Chief Compliance Officer after due diligence on the recipient and must not be made to influence a decision.

## 4. Gifts, Meals, Entertainment and Travel

4.1 **General principle.** Modest gifts and hospitality are normal in business, but they must be infrequent, reasonable, openly given, appropriate to the occasion, never conditioned on a favor, and compliant with the recipient's own rules. Cash and cash equivalents, such as gift cards, are never permitted.

4.2 **Private-sector recipients.** Employees may give customers or prospective customers meals and modest gifts, provided the value does not exceed $150 per person per occasion and $500 per person in a calendar year. Gifts and entertainment above these limits require the prior written approval of the employee's vice president and the Chief Compliance Officer. All such expenses must be recorded accurately in the expense system, with the names of recipients and the business purpose.

4.3 **Government officials.** Anything of value for a government official requires the prior written approval of the Chief Compliance Officer, irrespective of amount, except for light refreshments at a business meeting valued below $25 per person. Many governments have strict rules, and some set a limit of zero.

4.4 **Travel and lodging.** Keystone may pay for reasonable travel and lodging expenses of a customer or official only where they relate directly to a legitimate business purpose such as product demonstrations, plant inspections or training, and only with the prior written approval of the Chief Compliance Officer. Payments are made directly to the vendor and not to the guest. Side trips, spouses and family members may not be funded.

4.5 **Receiving gifts.** Employees may accept unsolicited gifts or entertainment of nominal value, up to $50 per source per year. Anything more valuable must be declined or reported to the employee's manager and the Compliance team, who will decide whether it should be returned or donated. Offers during an active bid or negotiation must be declined.

4.6 **Employment and internships.** Offers of employment, internships or work experience to relatives or associates of government officials or customers must be reviewed by Human Resources and the Chief Compliance Officer before they are made, to ensure that merit and not influence is the reason for the offer.

## 5. Third Parties

5.1 **Risk-based due diligence.** Before engaging a third party that will interact with government officials or customers on Keystone's behalf, the business owner must complete a due diligence questionnaire and submit it to Compliance. Due diligence includes verifying the identity and ownership of the third party, checking references, searching public records and media, screening against restricted-party lists, assessing the need and qualifications of the third party, and confirming the reasonableness of compensation.

5.2 **Approval.** High-risk third parties, such as agents or customs brokers operating in countries with a Transparency International Corruption Perceptions Index score below 40, require the approval of the Chief Compliance Officer before engagement. Approval is valid for two years, after which due diligence is refreshed.

5.3 **Compensation.** Fees and commissions paid to third parties must be consistent with the services provided and with market rates. Payments must be made only to the third party named in the engagement, by bank transfer to an account in the country where the services are performed, and not in cash or to a third party's personal accounts. Unusually high commissions, success fees or requests for advance payments are red flags.

5.4 **Monitoring.** Business owners must monitor third parties' work, review invoices for adequate detail and report red flags. Compliance reviews high-risk third parties every year and conducts audits of a sample.

5.5 **Training and certification.** High-risk third parties must receive a copy of the Partner Code of Conduct and confirm annually that they have not made and will not make improper payments in connection with Keystone's business.

## 6. Common Risk Areas

6.1 **Customs and logistics.** Delays at ports and border crossings, particularly in Mexico and in emerging markets, create pressure to pay. Customs brokers must be told in writing that facilitating payments are prohibited. Unusual customs charges or "expediting fees" must be questioned and documented.

6.2 **Permits and inspections.** Requests from regulators or inspectors for payments to obtain a permit, pass an inspection or avoid a fine must be refused and reported. Fees paid to a government must be official, published and paid to the government itself, supported by a receipt.

6.3 **Government and state-owned customers.** Interaction with officials of defense ministries, public hospitals and state-owned manufacturers requires extra care. Keystone employees who work on such opportunities must complete advanced training.

6.4 **Mergers and acquisitions.** Any acquisition, joint venture or investment must include anti-corruption due diligence, and acquired businesses must be brought within this policy in a defined period after closing.

6.5 **Sponsorships and trade shows.** Sponsorship of events or participation in a trade show where government officials will be present must be reviewed by the Chief Compliance Officer.

## 7. Red Flags

7.1 Employees must report to Compliance, and must not proceed until cleared, if they notice that a third party or a customer: has a reputation for corruption; is recommended by an official; asks that payment be made to a different person, a different country or an unrelated account; asks for payments in cash; refuses to confirm that it will comply with anti-corruption law; has a family or business relationship with an official; lacks the qualifications to do the work; proposes a commission that is higher than normal; or requests that its identity be concealed.

7.2 Compliance documents the review of every red flag, the actions taken and the resolution. A transaction may not go ahead with an unresolved red flag.

## 8. Training and Communication

8.1 All employees complete anti-bribery training on joining and annually afterward. Employees in sales, purchasing, logistics, finance, customer service and senior management and employees who deal with government officials complete a longer course with scenarios. Compliance tracks completion.

8.2 The Chief Compliance Officer provides regular reminders, especially before major trade shows and holiday gift-giving periods.

## 9. Reporting Concerns and Non-Retaliation

9.1 Employees who know or suspect a violation must report it immediately to their manager, the Chief Compliance Officer, the General Counsel, Human Resources or the ethics line, which is available around the clock, in English and Spanish and allows anonymous reports where the law permits.

9.2 Keystone will investigate every report promptly and fairly, and will limit the circle of people informed to those who need to know. Employees must cooperate with investigations.

9.3 Retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith or cooperates with an investigation is strictly prohibited and is a violation of this policy.

## 10. Roles and Responsibilities

10.1 **Audit Committee.** Approves this policy and receives a report on compliance at least twice a year.

10.2 **Chief Compliance Officer.** Owns this policy, provides advice and approvals, oversees due diligence, delivers training, investigates reports and reports to the Audit Committee.

10.3 **Vice presidents and directors.** Set the tone, ensure compliance in their functions and approve expenses in accordance with this policy.

10.4 **Internal Audit.** Tests controls, including expense reports, third-party payments and gifts register, and reports its findings.

10.5 **Finance.** Maintains accurate books and records, applies payment controls and flags unusual transactions.

10.6 **Employees.** Follow this policy, complete training and report concerns.

## 11. Exceptions

11.1 There are no exceptions that allow bribery or inaccurate record-keeping. Requests for exceptions to procedural requirements, such as the timing of due diligence, may be approved only by the Chief Compliance Officer in writing and must be documented.

## 12. Enforcement

12.1 Violations of this policy may result in discipline up to and including termination of employment, and, where appropriate, referral to law enforcement. Third parties that violate this policy may have their relationships terminated.

12.2 Managers are accountable for violations that they knew of, or should have known of, within their teams.

## Appendix A. Scenarios

A.1 **Scenario one: a lunch for a customer's purchasing team.** A Keystone sales manager wants to take four buyers from a private aerospace customer to lunch at a local restaurant near the customer's plant. The bill is expected to be about $480, or $120 per person. This is within the $150 per person limit and is a normal courtesy. The sales manager records the names, the purpose and the amount in the expense system. If the lunch were at a high-end restaurant and cost $250 per person, the sales manager would need prior written approval from the vice president and the Chief Compliance Officer.

A.2 **Scenario two: an official's visit.** A state-owned airline's engineering director plans to visit Keystone's Dayton plant to inspect a production lot. The sales team proposes to pay for his business-class flights, a weekend in New York and a gift for his wife. Business travel directly connected with inspecting the plant can be considered, but only with the prior approval of the Chief Compliance Officer, and the payment must go directly to the airline and hotel. Side trips and gifts for family members are not permitted and must be removed from the proposal.

A.3 **Scenario three: a customs broker's request.** The Mexican customs broker for the Monterrey cell calls the logistics manager to say that a shipment of tooling has been held at the border and that the shipment can be released the same day if an extra fee of $600 is paid in cash to the inspector. The logistics manager must refuse, tell the broker that no payment of this kind will be made, and report the request to the Chief Compliance Officer. The delay is a business problem that can be solved with documentation and escalation through legitimate channels. A cash payment to an inspector is a bribe, and the broker's request is a red flag that requires review of the broker relationship.

A.4 **Scenario four: a consultant's success fee.** A sales agent proposes to help Keystone win an order from a ministry of defense in return for a success fee of 12 percent, payable to an account in a third country. The fee is higher than market rates, the payment would go outside the country where the services are performed, and the agent refuses to name the people he will meet. Each of these facts is a red flag. The business owner must stop, submit the due diligence questionnaire to Compliance, and may not engage the agent unless the Chief Compliance Officer clears every flag and approves.

A.5 **Scenario five: a supplier's gift.** A supplier's account manager sends a bottle of whisky valued at $90 to a Keystone buyer's home during the holiday season, while Keystone is evaluating bids for a new tooling program in which the supplier is a bidder. The buyer must decline or return the gift, notify the manager and tell Compliance about the offer. The $50 annual limit is exceeded, and the timing during an active bid makes acceptance improper whatever the value.

A.6 **Scenario six: a charitable donation.** The mayor of a town where Keystone is considering building a new warehouse asks Keystone to donate $20,000 to a local youth sports foundation. The request comes at the same time as Keystone is seeking a zoning variance. The donation may not be made unless the Chief Compliance Officer has performed due diligence on the foundation, has confirmed that it is a legitimate charity and has concluded that the donation is not intended to influence the variance decision. In most cases like this one, the donation should be postponed until after the decision.

A.7 **Scenario seven: a request to hire.** A senior official at a customer asks a Keystone manager to give a summer internship to his nephew. Human Resources and the Chief Compliance Officer must review before any offer is made. If the nephew meets the standard qualifications, competes with other candidates, and is evaluated in the normal process, the offer may be appropriate. If the offer would be a favor in exchange for business, it is prohibited.

A.8 **Questions to ask yourself.** Would I be comfortable if this payment, gift or arrangement appeared on the front page of a newspaper? Would my manager and the Chief Compliance Officer approve if I asked first? Is the amount reasonable and the recipient's reason for receiving it clear? Does it comply with the recipient's rules? If the answer to any question is uncertain, stop and ask Compliance before going forward.
