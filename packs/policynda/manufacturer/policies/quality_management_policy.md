# Quality Management Policy

**Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc.**
Policy owner: Director of Quality
Policy approver: Chief Operating Officer
Effective date: April 1, 2025
Review cycle: annually, and at each management review
Document number: KPM-QA-001

---

## 1. Purpose

1.1 Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc. ("Keystone") makes parts that fly, that are implanted, that carry fluids at high pressure and that hold heavy loads. A part that is out of tolerance may be an inconvenience in one application and a catastrophe in another. Customers choose Keystone because they trust that a part labeled with a given revision, material and tolerance really is that part, every time. This policy sets out how we earn and keep that trust.

1.2 The policy establishes Keystone's quality management system (QMS), which is certified to ISO 9001 at all plants, to AS9100 at the Dayton and Toledo plants, and to ISO 13485 at the Erie plant. It defines the quality principles that apply to every employee, assigns responsibility for the elements of the system and sets the minimum expectations for process control, inspection, nonconforming product, corrective action, calibration, supplier quality, training and continual improvement.

1.3 Quality is not the exclusive responsibility of the Quality department. Every operator, programmer, inspector, buyer, planner and manager contributes to it, and every person has the authority and the obligation to stop work when they believe a part is not right.

## 2. Scope and Definitions

2.1 **Scope.** This policy applies to all products and services that Keystone designs, makes, tests, packages or ships, at all plants and for all customers. It applies to all employees and to temporary workers. Elements of it flow down to Keystone's suppliers through the Supplier Engagement Policy.

2.2 **Definitions.**

- **Conformance:** fulfillment of a requirement of a drawing, specification, standard, customer instruction or regulation.
- **Nonconformance:** failure to fulfill a requirement.
- **First article inspection (FAI):** a complete, documented verification that a production process can make a part that meets all requirements, performed before the first production lot and after significant changes.
- **Key characteristic:** a feature of a part, identified by the customer or by Keystone, whose variation has a significant effect on fit, form, function, performance or safety.
- **Statistical process control (SPC):** the use of statistical methods to monitor and control a process.
- **Corrective action:** action to eliminate the cause of a nonconformance so that it does not recur.
- **Preventive action:** action to eliminate the cause of a potential nonconformance.
- **Material review board (MRB):** the cross-functional group that decides the disposition of nonconforming product.
- **Escape:** a nonconforming part that reaches a customer.

## 3. Quality Principles

3.1 **Customer focus.** We determine, understand and meet customer requirements, including the explicit requirements on drawings and purchase orders and the implicit requirement that the part will work as intended.

3.2 **Process approach.** We manage activities as interrelated processes with defined inputs, outputs, owners and measures. We understand variation and control it at its source rather than inspecting it out at the end.

3.3 **Prevention over detection.** Time spent in planning, fixturing, programming and proving out a process is cheaper than time spent in sorting and rework. We use failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA), control plans and error-proofing in new and changed processes.

3.4 **Evidence-based decisions.** We make decisions using data. Charts, capability studies and trend reports are posted in the cell where they are produced, so that the people who run the process can see how it is behaving.

3.5 **Stop-work authority.** Any employee may stop a process that is producing, or may produce, nonconforming product. No employee will be disciplined for exercising this authority in good faith. A supervisor who overrides a stop must notify the quality manager immediately.

3.6 **Continual improvement.** We set annual quality objectives, review performance at management reviews and invest in improvements through kaizen events, Six Sigma projects and employee suggestions.

## 4. Planning and Release of New Products

4.1 **Advanced product quality planning.** For new parts, the program manager leads a cross-functional team to plan the process using the APQP method. The plan covers design feasibility, process flow, FMEA, control plan, measurement system analysis, tooling and fixtures, gauges, packaging, and a schedule for FAI and production part approval.

4.2 **Feasibility review.** Before Keystone commits to a new part, engineering, manufacturing and quality review the drawing for manufacturability, tolerance stack-ups, material availability, special processes, inspection capability and any customer-specific quality requirements. Concerns are documented and resolved with the customer before quoting is completed.

4.3 **First article inspection.** FAI is performed in accordance with AS9102 or the customer's requirement. All characteristics, including notes, material and special process certifications, are verified and recorded. The FAI report is approved by Quality before shipment of production quantities. A full or partial FAI is repeated after a change in design, material, supplier, process, tooling, machine location or after a lapse in production exceeding twenty-four months.

4.4 **Production part approval.** Where customers require it, a production part approval package is submitted with dimensional results, capability studies, material and process certifications and sample parts. Shipments do not begin until the customer approves.

## 5. Process Control

5.1 **Work instructions and routers.** Each part has a router that lists the operations in order, the machine or workstation, the work instruction, the gauges and the inspection points. Only the released version may be used.

5.2 **Setup verification.** After setup and before each production run, the operator produces a first piece, which is inspected by the operator and verified by an inspector or lead before the run continues. The result is recorded in the traveler.

5.3 **In-process inspection.** Operators measure characteristics at the frequency defined in the control plan. For key characteristics, SPC is used. Control charts are reviewed each shift. Process capability (Cpk) must be at least 1.33 for key characteristics, or 1.67 where the customer requires it. If capability is not demonstrated, 100 percent inspection is applied until the process is improved.

5.4 **Special processes.** Special processes such as heat treatment, plating, anodizing, passivation, welding and non-destructive testing are performed only by processes and personnel qualified under the applicable specification. Pyrometry is performed in accordance with AMS2750, and records are kept for the life of the part plus the retention period required by the customer.

5.5 **Identification and traceability.** Material is identified by heat, lot or batch from receipt through shipment. Where customers require serialization, each part is individually marked and the traceability record links the part to the material, the machine, the operator, the inspection results and the shipment.

5.6 **Foreign object damage and debris.** Aerospace work areas follow foreign object damage prevention practices, including tool control, housekeeping, shadow boards and cleanliness checks at the end of each shift. Tools are counted at the start and end of shifts in areas designated by the quality manager.

5.7 **Handling, storage, packaging and preservation.** Parts are protected against damage, contamination and corrosion during handling, storage and shipment. Packaging specifications are defined for each part number. Shelf-life-limited materials are controlled through first-expired, first-out and are quarantined when they reach their limit.

## 6. Measurement and Calibration

6.1 **Calibration.** All gauges, instruments, test equipment and measuring machines that affect product quality are uniquely identified, calibrated at defined intervals against standards traceable to the National Institute of Standards and Technology, and labeled with the due date. Equipment that is overdue or found out of tolerance is removed from service immediately.

6.2 **Out-of-tolerance findings.** When an instrument is found out of tolerance at calibration, the calibration technician notifies the quality manager, who determines the effect on product measured since the previous calibration and decides whether a recall, re-inspection or customer notification is needed.

6.3 **Measurement system analysis.** Gauge repeatability and reproducibility studies are performed for key characteristics and whenever a gauge or method changes. The gauge's variation must consume no more than 10 percent of the tolerance for key characteristics; between 10 and 30 percent is acceptable only with the approval of the quality manager.

6.4 **Coordinate measuring machines.** Programs for coordinate measuring machines are controlled documents. Changes are verified against a master part before release. The CMM room is held at 68 degrees Fahrenheit, plus or minus one degree, and temperature records are reviewed weekly.

## 7. Nonconforming Product

7.1 **Identification and containment.** Nonconforming product is identified, tagged with a red label and segregated in a marked quarantine area. It must not be used, shipped or mixed with conforming product. Suspect product is held until it is evaluated.

7.2 **Material review board.** The MRB, composed of a representative of Quality, Engineering and Manufacturing, meets as needed to decide disposition: scrap, rework to the original requirement, repair, return to supplier or use as is. Use-as-is and repair dispositions require customer approval when the customer has reserved this right. All dispositions are recorded.

7.3 **Escapes.** If a nonconforming part is suspected to have been shipped, the quality manager must notify the customer within twenty-four hours of confirmation, begin containment at Keystone, the customer and any intermediaries, and open an investigation. The Director of Quality reports each escape to the Chief Operating Officer within one business day.

7.4 **Counterfeit and suspect parts.** Keystone buys only from authorized sources. Material found to be counterfeit, suspect or unapproved is quarantined, reported to the customer and, where required, to government authorities such as the Government-Industry Data Exchange Program.

## 8. Corrective and Preventive Action

8.1 A corrective action is opened for every customer complaint, internal escape, audit finding of major severity and repeated nonconformance. The owner responds with containment within twenty-four hours, a root cause analysis within ten business days, and a corrective action plan with owners and dates. Quality verifies effectiveness after ninety days.

8.2 Root cause analysis uses structured methods such as 5-Why, Ishikawa diagrams or 8D. "Operator error" is not accepted as a root cause without explanation of why the system allowed the error.

8.3 Lessons from corrective actions are shared across plants through the monthly quality review, and process FMEAs are updated.

## 9. Audits and Management Review

9.1 **Internal audits.** The Quality department maintains an annual audit schedule that covers every process and every shift at each plant. Auditors are trained and independent of the area audited. Findings are classified as major, minor or opportunity, and are tracked until closed.

9.2 **External audits.** Registrar and customer audits are coordinated by the quality manager of the plant. The Director of Quality is notified of any major finding within one business day.

9.3 **Management review.** The Chief Operating Officer chairs the management review each quarter. Inputs include customer feedback, on-time delivery, parts per million defective, cost of poor quality, audit results, corrective action status, supplier performance and risks. Outputs are decisions on improvement, resources and objectives.

## 10. Training and Competence

10.1 Each job has a competency matrix that lists the required skills, training and certifications. Operators are certified on a machine or process by a qualified trainer before working unsupervised. Inspectors are certified annually in visual standards and measuring methods.

10.2 Quality awareness training is part of new hire orientation and is repeated annually. Topics include the quality policy, stop-work authority, the importance of traceability, foreign object prevention and the consequences of nonconformance.

## 11. Roles and Responsibilities

11.1 **Chief Operating Officer.** Sponsors the QMS, chairs management reviews and ensures resources are available.

11.2 **Director of Quality.** Owns this policy, maintains certifications, represents Keystone to registrars and customers on quality matters and approves quality procedures.

11.3 **Plant quality managers.** Implement the QMS at the plant, manage inspection and metrology, chair the MRB and lead corrective actions.

11.4 **Plant managers.** Ensure that production processes follow the control plan and that quality is not compromised for schedule.

11.5 **Supervisors and leads.** Verify setups, monitor in-process checks, and ensure operators have the training and equipment they need.

11.6 **Operators and inspectors.** Follow work instructions, record results accurately, identify nonconforming product and exercise stop-work authority.

11.7 **Supplier quality engineers.** Qualify and monitor suppliers and manage supplier corrective actions.

## 12. Exceptions

12.1 Requests to deviate from a procedure in this policy are submitted to the Director of Quality, who may approve a temporary deviation for up to ninety days if product quality and customer requirements are not compromised. Longer deviations require the approval of the Chief Operating Officer.

12.2 Customer-imposed requirements that differ from this policy are recorded in the project plan and followed unless they conflict with law.

## 13. Enforcement

13.1 Falsification of inspection records, shipment of known nonconforming product without disposition, or disregard of stop-work authority are serious violations that may result in termination and, where required, in notification of customers and regulators.

13.2 Employees may report quality concerns, including pressure to ship nonconforming product, to the Director of Quality, to Human Resources or through the ethics line, without fear of retaliation.

## Appendix A. Quality Metrics, Audit Checklist and Worked Examples

A.1 **Core metrics.** The following metrics are reported monthly for each plant and consolidated for the quarterly management review.

| Metric | Definition | Corporate target |
|---|---|---|
| On-time delivery | Lines shipped on or before the promised date divided by lines due | 96 percent or better |
| Customer parts per million (PPM) | Nonconforming parts returned by customers per million shipped | Below 150 |
| Internal first-pass yield | Parts accepted at final inspection without rework | 97 percent or better |
| Scrap and rework cost | Cost of scrapped and reworked product as a share of sales | Below 1.8 percent |
| Corrective action timeliness | Corrective actions closed by their due date | 90 percent or better |
| Calibration on-time rate | Instruments calibrated by their due date | 100 percent |
| Supplier PPM | Nonconforming parts received per million, by supplier | Below 500 |
| Escapes | Number of confirmed nonconforming parts shipped to a customer | Zero |

A.2 **Reading a control chart.** Operators and leads should know the basic signals that a process has changed. A single point beyond a control limit calls for an immediate check of the part and the process. Seven consecutive points on one side of the center line, or seven points steadily rising or falling, indicate a shift or trend even if every point is inside the limits. A run of points hugging the center line may mean the sample is hiding variation, for instance because measurements from two machines are being mixed. When a signal appears, the operator stops, contains the parts made since the last good check, notes the time and calls the lead. Adjustments are made only when a signal shows that the process has changed; adjusting a stable process increases variation.

A.3 **Process audit checklist.** Internal auditors use the following questions in each area. Is the current released revision of the router and drawing available at the point of use? Does the operator know the key characteristics and the reaction plan? Are gauges identified, within calibration and appropriate for the tolerance? Are first-piece records complete for the last three setups? Are nonconforming parts tagged and in quarantine? Is material identification maintained? Are tools and fixtures stored properly and checked for wear? Is housekeeping at the level required to prevent foreign object damage? Has training been recorded for everyone working in the area? Are customer-specific instructions posted?

A.4 **Worked example: a drifting bore.** A night-shift operator on a horizontal machining center measures a bore diameter on every fifth part and plots the result. Over six parts the readings move steadily toward the upper limit, though each remains within tolerance. The operator recognizes the trend, stops after the sixth part, and measures the last five parts made since the previous check. All five conform. The lead checks tool wear and finds the finishing boring bar is worn on its insert. The insert is changed, the first piece is checked and verified by the inspector, and the run continues. The operator records the event, the parts affected and the action taken on the traveler. Because the trend was caught before any part left the tolerance band, no nonconforming product was made, and no corrective action was needed; the tool life setting for that operation is updated by the process engineer the following morning so that the insert is changed earlier.

A.5 **Worked example: customer complaint.** A customer reports that three of fifty parts in a recent lot have a burr on an internal cross-hole that interferes with assembly. The customer service representative logs the complaint and notifies the plant quality manager, who opens a corrective action the same day. Containment includes inspecting stock at Keystone and in transit and sending a sorting team to the customer, if requested. Within ten business days, the team shows that a deburring step was moved to another operation during a routing change and the new instruction did not specify the cross-hole. The root cause is the routing change process, which did not require review of operations with deburring notes. Corrective action adds that review to the engineering change checklist and a verification step to the first-piece inspection. After ninety days, Quality confirms that no recurrence has occurred in three lots, and closes the corrective action.

A.6 **Cost of poor quality.** Finance and Quality jointly report the cost of poor quality each quarter, grouped as prevention (training, planning, calibration), appraisal (inspection, testing, audits), internal failure (scrap, rework, downtime) and external failure (returns, warranty, sorting, expediting). The report is used to prioritize improvement projects and to show whether investments in prevention are reducing failure costs.
