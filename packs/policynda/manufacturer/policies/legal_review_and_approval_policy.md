# Legal Review and Approval Policy

**Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc.**
Policy owner: General Counsel
Policy approver: Chief Executive Officer
Effective date: January 6, 2025
Review cycle: annually
Document number: KPM-LG-001

---

## 1. Purpose

1.1 Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc. ("Keystone") enters into thousands of written commitments each year: purchase orders, supply and tooling agreements, distribution contracts, customer quality agreements, engineering services statements of work, leases, software subscriptions and many others. Each of these allocates risk. Each can bind Keystone for years. Most are routine, and some are not.

1.2 This policy defines who may commit Keystone to what, when the Legal Department must review a document before it is signed, which drafting standards apply to documents that Keystone prepares or accepts, and which deviations from those standards must be approved at a higher level. It is meant to let routine business move quickly while making sure the unusual or risky matters receive proper attention.

1.3 The General Counsel is responsible for this policy. The Legal Department provides templates, playbooks and advice, but business owners remain accountable for the commercial decisions they make within the authority they have been given.

## 2. Scope and Definitions

2.1 **Scope.** This policy applies to all Keystone employees, officers and agents who negotiate, review, approve or sign any written commitment on behalf of Keystone or any subsidiary. It applies worldwide. Special rules for government contracts are set out in the Government Contracts Compliance Manual.

2.2 **Definitions.**

- **Counterparty:** the organization or individual on the other side of the commitment, such as a supplier, customer, distributor or joint development partner.
- **Deal:** a single transaction or relationship governed by one set of documents.
- **Total deal value:** the greater of the committed spend or revenue over the full term of the deal or the expected spend or revenue over the first three years.
- **Deal value band:** the classification of a deal by total deal value. A **low value** deal has a total deal value below $250,000. A **medium value** deal has a total deal value of $250,000 or more but less than $2,000,000. A **high value** deal has a total deal value of $2,000,000 or more. Deals in which no money changes hands but where Keystone shares intellectual property of strategic importance are treated as high value by default unless the General Counsel decides otherwise.
- **Template:** a form approved by the Legal Department for repeated use.
- **Playbook:** the guidance that lists the standard position, the acceptable fallback positions and the escalation level for each term that matters in a given type of document.
- **Deviation:** any term that departs from the standard position in a template or playbook beyond the approved fallback.
- **Signatory:** an employee holding delegated signature authority under section 7.

## 3. Drafting Standards for Agreements with Outside Parties

3.1 **Letting the other side use its advisers.** No business can operate without lawyers, accountants, consultants, customs brokers and agents, and Keystone expects the same of its counterparties. An agreement that governs the exchange of our information must allow the recipient to pass what it receives to its outside advisers, consultants and agents, provided they are bound to keep it secret. Wording that forbids the recipient from consulting any outside person at all is not acceptable.

3.2 **Use of templates.** Legal-approved templates are the default starting point. Business owners must use the template for the type of document unless the counterparty refuses, in which case the Legal Department reviews the counterparty's paper. Templates are stored in the contract repository and may be modified only by the Legal Department.

3.3 **Information that Keystone may receive from others.** Keystone is a company that makes things, and so are many of its counterparties. Over time, the same or similar information can reach us from a different source without any breach on our part: from a customer's competitor, from a published standard, from our own research. We will not sign a document that treats such an event as a violation. Each agreement should therefore confirm that the recipient remains free to obtain information resembling ours from a third party.

3.4 **Plain language and completeness.** Documents should state the actual deal. Side letters, handshake understandings and email commitments that add to or vary a signed document are not permitted unless approved by the Legal Department and attached to the contract record.

3.5 **Entire agreement and amendments.** Amendments must be in writing, signed by authorized signatories of both parties, and routed through the same review and approval path as the original document. Informal changes, such as a purchase order that adds terms inconsistent with a master agreement, are not effective without Legal Department review.

3.6 **Commercial terms checklist.** Reviewers verify, at a minimum, that each document states the parties' correct legal names and addresses, the scope of work, prices and payment terms, delivery or performance dates, warranties, limits on liability, insurance, indemnities, termination rights, notices, assignment and subcontracting.

## 4. Terms That Require Special Care

4.1 **Limitation of liability and indemnities.** Keystone's standard position is a mutual cap on liability equal to the fees paid or payable in the twelve months before a claim, with carve-outs for bodily injury, gross negligence, willful misconduct and infringement indemnities. A cap lower than the standard, an uncapped indemnity from Keystone, or a waiver of consequential damages that is not mutual requires approval from the General Counsel. For medium and high value deals, the Chief Financial Officer must also approve any exposure above $5,000,000.

4.2 **Money payable on breach.** Courts frequently refuse to enforce sums that look like punishment, and a fixed charge can come back to bite us as easily as it can help us. Keystone does not sign an agreement that fixes a penalty or liquidated damages amount to be paid when a party breaches its duty of secrecy, and it does not ask the other side to accept one. Remedies for such a breach are left to the general law, including injunctive relief and damages in proportion to the loss.

4.3 **Intellectual property.** Documents that assign, license or encumber Keystone's intellectual property require review by the Intellectual Property Counsel. Licenses to Keystone's patents or Tier 1 material may be granted only with the approval of the Chief Technology Officer, on advice from the General Counsel.

4.4 **Non-compete and exclusivity.** Any commitment by Keystone not to compete, not to sell to specified customers or not to buy from other suppliers requires General Counsel approval. Exclusivity granted by Keystone for longer than three years requires the Chief Executive Officer.

4.5 **Government and regulated customers.** Documents involving federal, state or local government customers, flow-down clauses, cybersecurity requirements such as DFARS 252.204-7012, or customer-imposed quality requirements are reviewed by the Legal Department and the Director of Quality before signature.

4.6 **Insurance and indemnity from Keystone's side.** Keystone's insurance program is maintained by the Risk Manager. No employee may agree to an insurance coverage requirement beyond Keystone's existing program without the Risk Manager's approval.

4.7 **Automatic renewal.** A document that renews automatically for a period longer than twelve months, or that requires more than ninety days' notice to prevent renewal, requires approval by the General Counsel. The contract repository sends an alert to the document owner 120 days before any renewal date.

## 5. Duration of Obligations

5.1 **Upper limit on the secrecy period.** We do not want to carry open-ended obligations indefinitely, and neither does a court want to enforce them. The period during which a recipient must keep our information secret, and during which we must keep theirs secret, may not exceed 120 months. A longer period, or an indefinite one, can only be agreed if the General Counsel approves it in advance. The Trade Secret Protection Policy sets the minimum period.

5.2 **Upper limit on obligations that continue after termination.** Duties that are expressed to continue after an agreement has been terminated must not extend past 84 months from termination. The Trade Secret Protection Policy sets the minimum.

5.3 **Contract term and renewal.** The term of a supply or distribution agreement should be no longer than five years for production material and three years for services, unless the business case demonstrates a benefit. Terms longer than these limits require the approval of the General Counsel and, for medium and high value deals, the Chief Operating Officer.

5.4 **Records.** The business owner must make sure that the signed document and all amendments are uploaded to the contract repository within five business days of signature. Documents are retained as set out in the Records Retention Schedule.

## 6. Governing Law and Disputes

6.1 **Choosing the law.** Documents that Keystone signs must be governed by the law of Ohio or the law of Delaware. If the counterparty will not agree to either, the business owner must request the approval of the General Counsel before agreeing to any other governing law.

6.2 **Forum and dispute resolution.** The preferred forum for disputes is the state or federal courts located in Montgomery County, Ohio, or New Castle County, Delaware. Binding arbitration is acceptable for international transactions where enforcement of a court judgment may be difficult, provided the rules of the International Centre for Dispute Resolution or the American Arbitration Association apply and the seat of arbitration is in the United States. Mediation is encouraged as a first step.

6.3 **International deals.** For transactions with counterparties outside the United States, the Legal Department decides whether local counsel should review the document. Documents involving export-controlled technical data are also reviewed under the Export Control Policy.

6.4 **Jury waiver and limitation periods.** Where permitted by law, Keystone seeks a mutual waiver of jury trial. A contractual period for bringing claims shorter than the statutory limitation period requires approval by the General Counsel.

## 7. Review and Signature Authority

7.1 **Legal review thresholds.** Legal Department review is required before signature for any document that (a) has a total deal value of $250,000 or more, (b) is not on a Legal-approved template, (c) contains a deviation from the playbook, (d) involves a government customer, or (e) involves intellectual property. Low value deals on an approved template without deviations may be signed without Legal review.

7.2 **Signature authority.** Signature authority is delegated as follows, subject to the limits in the Delegation of Authority register:

| Total deal value | Authorized signatory |
|---|---|
| Under $50,000 | Department manager |
| $50,000 to under $250,000 | Director or plant manager |
| $250,000 to under $1,000,000 | Vice President of the sponsoring function |
| $1,000,000 to under $5,000,000 | Chief Operating Officer or Chief Financial Officer |
| $5,000,000 and above | Chief Executive Officer, with notice to the Board |

7.3 **Splitting.** A deal may not be divided into parts to avoid a review or approval threshold. Related commitments with the same counterparty within a twelve-month period are aggregated.

7.4 **Electronic signatures.** Electronic signature through the Company-approved platform is acceptable for all documents except those that must be notarized or that the General Counsel designates.

7.5 **Turnaround.** The Legal Department aims to complete review of template-based documents within three business days and non-standard documents within ten business days after receiving a complete request. Business owners should allow time for escalations.

7.6 **Approval record.** Every approval of a deviation is recorded in the contract repository with the name of the approver, the date, the deviation and the reason. Approvals are valid only for the document for which they were given and do not create a precedent unless the General Counsel adopts them into the playbook.

## 8. Contract Repository and Playbooks

8.1 The Legal Department maintains the contract repository, which is the single record of Keystone's signed documents, approvals and key dates. Business owners are responsible for the accuracy of the metadata they enter, including counterparty, value, term, renewal date and responsible manager.

8.2 Playbooks are updated at least once a year. The General Counsel reviews recurring deviations and decides whether to move a position into the template or to tighten the playbook.

8.3 Business owners may ask the Legal Department for a short briefing on a playbook; sessions are offered quarterly for sales, sourcing and engineering staff.

## 9. Roles and Responsibilities

9.1 **General Counsel.** Owns this policy, approves deviations assigned to the General Counsel, maintains playbooks and reports to the Chief Executive Officer and the Audit Committee on legal risk.

9.2 **Legal Department.** Reviews documents, negotiates non-standard terms, maintains templates and the contract repository, and trains business owners.

9.3 **Business owners.** Prepare the business case, negotiate commercial terms within their authority, send documents for review in good time, and ensure that signed documents are filed.

9.4 **Signatories.** Sign only documents that have completed the required reviews and approvals and that are within their delegated authority.

9.5 **Chief Financial Officer.** Approves exposure above the thresholds in section 4.1 and reviews payment and credit terms.

9.6 **Chief Technology Officer and Intellectual Property Counsel.** Review intellectual property terms as described in section 4.3.

## 10. Exceptions

10.1 Exceptions to this policy that are not assigned to a named approver are decided by the General Counsel. A request must be in writing, identify the document, the section, the business reason and the risk, and propose any mitigation.

10.2 The General Counsel may delegate approval of low-risk exceptions to a named member of the Legal Department by written delegation, which is filed in the contract repository.

10.3 Exceptions are valid only for the transaction for which they are granted.

## 11. Enforcement

11.1 Signing a document without the required review or authority, splitting a deal to avoid thresholds, or concealing a deviation from the Legal Department is a violation of this policy and of the Code of Business Conduct. Violations can result in discipline up to and including termination.

11.2 Internal Audit tests a sample of signed documents each year against this policy and reports exceptions to the Audit Committee.

## Appendix A. Escalation Contacts and Review Timeline

A.1 **Who to call.** Questions about templates and routine documents go to the Legal Department's contract desk. Questions about intellectual property go to the Intellectual Property Counsel. Questions about export-controlled technology go to the Export Compliance Manager. Questions about government customers go to the Government Contracts Counsel. Questions about employment matters go to the employment counsel in the Legal Department, working with Human Resources. Disputes and threatened claims are reported at once to the General Counsel, who decides whether to retain outside counsel.

A.2 **Review timeline.** A typical review of a non-standard document follows these steps. Day one: the business owner submits the request through the contract intake form with the draft, the deal value and the business summary. Day two: the contract desk assigns a lawyer and confirms the review path. Days three to six: the lawyer reviews, marks up the document and meets the business owner. Days seven to ten: negotiation with the counterparty, with the lawyer joining calls when necessary. Day eleven onward: final approvals, signature and filing. Complex matters can take longer. Business owners should submit early, and flag the date by which they need a signature.

A.3 **Intake form contents.** The intake form asks for the counterparty's full legal name and address, the type of document, a description of the business purpose, the total deal value and its band, the term, the renewal mechanism, the Keystone business owner and sponsor, the proposed signatory, any special risks such as government involvement or export-controlled items, and the date by which signature is needed.

A.4 **Legal holds.** When litigation, an investigation or a government inquiry is reasonably anticipated, the General Counsel issues a legal hold notice identifying the categories of documents to be preserved. Recipients must suspend deletion of the listed materials, including email, chat messages, files and paper records, until released in writing. Failure to comply can result in court sanctions and discipline.

A.5 **Outside counsel.** Only the Legal Department may engage outside counsel on behalf of Keystone, except that the Chief Financial Officer may engage tax and audit advisers and Human Resources may engage employment counsel with Legal Department consent. Engagements follow the outside counsel guidelines, which require budgets, staffing plans and monthly invoicing. The General Counsel approves any engagement expected to cost more than $50,000.
