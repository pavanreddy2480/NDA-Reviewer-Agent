# Employee Confidentiality Policy

**Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc.**
Policy owner: Vice President, Human Resources
Policy approver: Chief Executive Officer
Effective date: January 20, 2025
Review cycle: annually
Document number: KPM-HR-007

---

## 1. Purpose

1.1 People are the most important asset of Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc. ("Keystone"), and they are also the most common route by which information reaches the outside world, whether through carelessness, through a conversation at a trade show, or, occasionally, through a deliberate decision to take something to a competitor. Most of the damage that a manufacturer suffers from loss of confidential information does not come from hackers. It comes from well-meaning people who did not realize that something they were saying or carrying was sensitive.

1.2 This policy explains what Keystone expects from its employees and individual contractors about the information they learn through their work, what Keystone commits to in return, how the protection of information is handled when people join, change roles and leave, and which employee rights are preserved no matter what.

1.3 The policy is intended to be understood by shop-floor operators, engineers, office staff and executives alike. It is not a substitute for the Trade Secret Protection Policy, which defines how information is classified, or for the Engineering Data Handling Policy, which governs technical data, but it explains how those rules apply to individuals.

## 2. Scope and Definitions

2.1 **Scope.** This policy applies to all Keystone employees, full-time and part-time, regular and temporary, as well as interns, apprentices, and individual contractors, including contract engineers and staffing-agency workers who are placed at Keystone sites. It applies in all states and countries in which Keystone operates, subject to local law, which prevails where it is more protective of the individual.

2.2 **Definitions.**

- **Employee:** any person described in section 2.1.
- **Confidential information:** non-public information that Keystone owns or holds in trust for a customer, supplier or partner, and that an employee learns through work. It includes trade secrets, technical data, pricing, cost data, customer and supplier information, business plans, financial results that have not been announced, personnel information about other people and security arrangements.
- **Personnel:** employees, officers, contractors and others who represent or work for an organization.
- **Hiring manager:** the manager who has the authority to hire into an open position.
- **Exit checklist:** the form that records the return of Keystone property and the removal of access when employment ends.

## 3. Obligations of Employees and Related Commitments

3.1 **Sharing inside the other side's organization.** Keystone relies on its own workforce to get work done, and it expects the same of the organizations it deals with. An engineer at a supplier cannot do a job without help from a colleague. Whenever Keystone signs an agreement that governs how the other side treats our information, the agreement must permit the recipient to share some of that information with those of its own employees who need it for the purpose of the work.

3.2 **Duties during employment.** Employees must use confidential information only for Keystone's business, must not disclose it to anyone outside Keystone unless authorized, must disclose it inside Keystone only to colleagues who need it, and must take reasonable care to protect it. These duties exist regardless of whether the information is marked, and they apply to information that an employee has learned orally. Employees must not take confidential information for personal use, to a new employer or for the benefit of a competitor.

3.3 **Not luring away colleagues.** Experienced machinists, programmers and engineers are hard to find, and the departure of a whole team can paralyze a cell. For that reason, each agreement with an individual employee or contractor engineer must commit the individual not to solicit Keystone's personnel to leave. Where California law governs the individual's agreement, no such commitment is included, because California law does not allow it. Managers should not ask California-based employees to sign a version containing the commitment.

3.4 **After employment.** Employees' duties to protect confidential information continue after they leave, for as long as the information remains confidential. Former employees may use their general skills, knowledge and experience, but may not use or disclose Keystone's trade secrets or other confidential information. HR reminds departing employees of these duties during the exit process, and the exit letter lists the categories of information that remain protected.

3.5 **Information from previous employers.** Employees must not use or bring to Keystone confidential information belonging to former employers or to others. Hiring managers are required to remind new hires, in the offer letter and again at onboarding, that they must not bring such information to Keystone. A hiring manager who becomes aware that a new employee has done so must tell the Legal Department immediately.

3.6 **Customer and supplier information.** Information that customers and suppliers entrust to Keystone must be treated with the same care as Keystone's own information, and in some cases with greater care because of customer requirements. Employees must follow any special handling instructions recorded in the project plan.

## 4. Notices and Protected Rights

4.1 **Inventions.** Employees assign to Keystone the inventions and works they create within the scope of their employment. Employees must promptly disclose such inventions to the Intellectual Property Counsel. State laws that limit assignment, including those in Ohio, Illinois, Minnesota and California, are respected, and the assignment does not extend to inventions that the law says are the employee's own.

4.2 **The whistleblower immunity notice.** Federal law protects people who disclose a trade secret in confidence to a government official or to a lawyer solely to report or investigate a suspected violation of law, and it protects disclosure made under seal in a court filing. The law requires an employer to tell its workers about this protection in any agreement with an employee that deals with trade secrets, and if the employer does not, it can lose some remedies. Keystone therefore requires that each agreement with an employee that is governed by United States law, whether federal or state, include a notice of this immunity.

4.3 **Protected activity.** Nothing in this policy or in any agreement with Keystone prevents an employee from filing a complaint with, communicating with or providing information to a government agency such as the National Labor Relations Board, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, the Occupational Safety and Health Administration or the Securities and Exchange Commission, without notice to Keystone. Nor does it limit the right of employees to discuss wages, hours and working conditions with each other or with others, as protected by Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act.

4.4 **No retaliation.** Keystone prohibits retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith, who takes part in an investigation or who exercises a right described in this policy. Concerns can be raised with a manager, with Human Resources, with the Compliance Officer or through the anonymous ethics line.

## 5. Joining, Moving and Leaving

5.1 **Joining.** Before the first day of work, HR provides the offer letter and the employment documents, including the inventions assignment and acknowledgment of this policy. During the first week, new employees complete the trade secret briefing, the code of conduct training and the information security module. Access to systems is granted according to role.

5.2 **Changing roles.** When an employee transfers, the former manager and the new manager review access rights and remove those no longer needed. If the move is to a role that has access to Tier 1 material, the information owner must approve the access before it is activated.

5.3 **Leaving.** Human Resources starts the exit process as soon as notice is given or a termination decision is made. The manager completes the exit checklist: return of badges, keys, laptops, phones, tools, notebooks, samples and documents; removal of system access on the last day; and a review of what the employee has been working on. For departures involving employees with Tier 1 access, Information Security reviews download activity for the last ninety days before departure, and the Intellectual Property Counsel may arrange a conversation with the employee about continuing duties.

5.4 **Immediate removal.** Where an employee is dismissed for cause or is leaving to work for a competitor, access may be removed immediately and the employee may be escorted from the premises. Personal items are returned later through HR.

5.5 **Exit interview.** HR offers each departing employee an exit interview. The employee is asked to confirm that all Keystone property has been returned, that no Keystone information remains in personal accounts or devices, and that the employee understands the continuing duties described in section 3.4.

## 6. Communications and Social Media

6.1 Employees must not post, share or comment on social media about unannounced products, customer programs, internal results or incidents. Photographs and videos taken inside plants must not be posted without permission from Corporate Communications.

6.2 Employees who are approached by journalists, analysts, investors or other outsiders for information about Keystone must direct them to Corporate Communications. Employees may share Keystone-approved public announcements and marketing content.

6.3 Employees who use professional networking sites must keep their profiles factual and must not disclose customer names, program names or details of their work that are not already public. Employees are asked to review the Social Media Guidelines available on the intranet.

6.4 Employees may speak publicly about the industry as private citizens but must make clear that they speak for themselves.

## 7. Personal Devices and Accounts

7.1 Employees may access Keystone email and calendar on a personal phone only through the managed mobile application, which separates Keystone data from personal data and allows Keystone to remove its data if the phone is lost.

7.2 Employees must not store Keystone information in personal email, personal cloud storage, personal notebooks or personal messaging applications. If an employee receives Keystone information on a personal account by mistake, the employee must report it to Information Security and delete it.

7.3 Photographs of drawings, screens or parts taken with personal phones are prohibited in controlled areas of Keystone facilities. Plant managers may authorize use of phones for specific tasks, such as scanning barcodes, and the authorization is recorded.

## 8. Training

8.1 All employees complete confidentiality awareness training annually. Employees who handle Tier 1 material complete an additional module. Hiring managers and people managers complete training on the topics in sections 3.5 and 5.

8.2 Training completion is tracked by HR. Managers whose staff are overdue are notified monthly, and Information Security suspends access to Tier 1 material for people whose training is more than thirty days overdue.

## 9. Roles and Responsibilities

9.1 **Vice President, Human Resources.** Owns this policy, ensures that onboarding and exit procedures are carried out, and reports to the Chief Executive Officer on policy compliance.

9.2 **Managers.** Brief new staff, enforce the rules on access, complete the exit checklist and raise concerns promptly.

9.3 **Human Resources.** Maintains employment documents, delivers training, runs the exit process and handles employee questions.

9.4 **Legal Department and Intellectual Property Counsel.** Prepare the standard employee documents, advise on the application of state law, handle any claim for breach and lead investigations of suspected misappropriation.

9.5 **Information Security.** Manages access, monitoring and devices.

9.6 **Employees.** Follow this policy, complete training, protect information and report concerns.

## 10. Exceptions

10.1 Requests for exceptions to this policy are made in writing to the Vice President, Human Resources, who consults the General Counsel. Exceptions that concern Tier 1 material are also referred to the Chief Technology Officer.

10.2 No exception can reduce the protected rights set out in section 4.

## 11. Enforcement

11.1 A violation of this policy may lead to disciplinary action up to and including termination, subject to local law and the terms of any applicable collective bargaining agreement. Keystone may also seek legal remedies where the law allows.

11.2 Managers who fail to carry out their responsibilities under this policy may also be disciplined.
