# Export Control Policy

**Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc.**
Policy owner: Export Compliance Manager
Policy approver: General Counsel
Effective date: March 10, 2025
Review cycle: annually
Document number: KPM-CO-005

---

## 1. Purpose

1.1 Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc. ("Keystone") makes components for aircraft, spacecraft, defense systems, medical devices and industrial machinery. Many of these items, and the technology used to design and make them, are controlled by United States export laws. Violations can lead to civil penalties of over $370,000 per violation under the Export Administration Regulations, criminal fines of up to $1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to twenty years, loss of export privileges and debarment from government work. For a company of our size, a violation could threaten the business.

1.2 This policy establishes the program through which Keystone complies with United States export control and sanctions laws, including the Export Administration Regulations (EAR), the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR), the regulations administered by the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) and the anti-boycott rules. It also reflects the export laws of other countries in which Keystone operates, in particular Mexico.

1.3 The program has five parts: classification of items and technology; screening of parties and destinations; licensing and authorization; control of access to controlled technology; and training, recordkeeping and audit.

## 2. Scope and Definitions

2.1 **Scope.** This policy applies to every Keystone employee, officer and contractor, and to all Keystone facilities. It applies to every export, re-export or transfer of goods, software and technology, including physical shipments, electronic transmissions, hand-carried items, visits by foreign nationals, discussions with foreign nationals inside or outside the United States, and cloud access from abroad.

2.2 **Definitions.**

- **Export:** an actual shipment or transmission of an item out of the United States, or the release of technology or source code to a foreign person in the United States (a "deemed export").
- **Re-export:** a shipment or transmission of an item from one foreign country to another foreign country.
- **Transfer (in-country):** a change in end use or end user within the same foreign country.
- **Foreign person:** any person who is not a US citizen, a lawful permanent resident, or a protected individual under federal rules, and any foreign entity.
- **US person:** a US citizen, a lawful permanent resident and a protected individual.
- **ECCN:** Export Control Classification Number, assigned to an item on the Commerce Control List.
- **USML category:** a category on the United States Munitions List, covering defense articles.
- **Technology:** information necessary for the development, production or use of an item, which can take the form of technical data or technical assistance.
- **Restricted party:** a person or entity on a government list of parties with whom dealings are prohibited or restricted, including the Specially Designated Nationals list and the Entity List.
- **License exception:** an authorization in the EAR that permits an export without a license under stated conditions.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

3.1 **General Counsel.** Approves this policy, has final authority over decisions to export or decline to export when there is uncertainty, and decides on voluntary disclosures to government agencies.

3.2 **Export Compliance Manager (ECM).** Owns this policy and runs the program. The ECM classifies items and technology, maintains the classification database, screens parties, applies for licenses and monitors compliance with license conditions, trains employees, maintains records and performs audits.

3.3 **Empowered Official.** The ECM is designated as Keystone's Empowered Official under ITAR, with authority to sign license applications and to refuse to sign any application or approve any export that the official believes may violate the law, without being overruled by management.

3.4 **Plant managers and the shipping supervisor.** Ensure that shipments are not released unless the system shows that classification, screening and any needed license are complete.

3.5 **Engineering and program management.** Provide the information the ECM needs for classification, including product technical details and end-use information, and identify foreign person access to technology.

3.6 **Sales and customer service.** Obtain end-use and end-user information from customers and report suspicious inquiries immediately.

3.7 **Human Resources.** Provides citizenship and immigration status information needed to determine access to controlled technology, in compliance with anti-discrimination laws, and notifies the ECM before hiring foreign persons into positions with access to controlled technology.

3.8 **All employees.** Follow this policy, complete training and report possible violations immediately.

## 4. Classification

4.1 Every Keystone product, component, software item and technology is classified by the ECM before its first export, and the classification is recorded in the enterprise resource planning system. The classification identifies whether the item is subject to the ITAR (and the USML category), subject to the EAR (and the ECCN, or EAR99 where none applies) or not subject to either.

4.2 Engineering provides a classification request form for each new part family. For parts made to customer designs, the customer's classification is obtained in writing and checked by the ECM. Where classification is uncertain, the ECM may request a commodity jurisdiction determination from the Department of State or a commodity classification from the Bureau of Industry and Security.

4.3 Classifications are reviewed whenever a product is materially modified, a regulation changes or at least every three years.

4.4 Items controlled under the ITAR are produced only at the designated facilities and processed in the segregated areas established for defense work. Employees working on ITAR items must be US persons unless the ECM has obtained a license or other authorization.

## 5. Screening of Parties and Destinations

5.1 **Restricted party screening.** Before accepting an order, shipping an item, providing a quote to a new party, engaging a distributor or supplier, or hosting a visitor from another organization, the responsible employee must ensure that every party to the transaction has been screened against the consolidated screening list using the Company's screening tool. This includes customers, consignees, freight forwarders, banks and intermediaries. Screening is repeated whenever a list is updated and in any event annually for active parties.

5.2 **Embargoed and sanctioned destinations.** Keystone does not do business, directly or indirectly, with parties in Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, or the Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk regions of Ukraine, or with any other destination or party covered by a comprehensive US sanctions program, unless the General Counsel approves it in writing and any license required has been issued.

5.3 **Red flags.** Employees must stop the transaction and inform the ECM if they observe any of the following: a customer is reluctant to give end-use information; the product's capabilities do not match the stated purpose; the customer is unfamiliar with the product's technical characteristics; the shipping route is unusual; the delivery address is a freight forwarder or a post office box; the customer offers to pay cash for an expensive item; a customer declines routine installation or maintenance; or the order includes unusual packing or labeling requests.

5.4 **End-use and end-user statements.** For sales of controlled items, and for sales to destinations the ECM designates as higher risk, the customer must provide a signed statement identifying the end user and end use and confirming that the items will not be re-exported or transferred without authorization.

5.5 **Prohibited end uses.** Keystone does not knowingly supply items for use in nuclear explosive devices, missile technology, chemical or biological weapons, or military end uses in countries where the EAR restricts such sales, without the necessary authorization.

## 6. Licensing and Authorization

6.1 **Determination.** Before each export of a controlled item or technology, the ECM determines whether a license is required, or whether a license exception, a no-license-required determination or an existing authorization applies. The determination considers the classification, the destination, the end user and the end use.

6.2 **License applications.** Applications for licenses are submitted only by the ECM or the Empowered Official. Employees must not communicate with government licensing offices on export matters without the ECM.

6.3 **License conditions.** The ECM records license conditions, including value and quantity limits, expiry dates and required reporting, in the license register and informs the shipping supervisor and the sales representative. A license is used only for the items, parties and destination listed.

6.4 **Technical assistance and manufacturing licenses.** Where Keystone provides defense services such as design assistance, training or technical consultation to foreign persons, or shares manufacturing know-how, a technical assistance or manufacturing license from the Department of State is required under the ITAR, and the ECM must be involved from the earliest discussion.

6.5 **Foreign trips and visitors.** Employees traveling abroad with laptops, phones or hardware containing controlled technology must obtain ECM approval beforehand and use loaner devices where recommended. Visits to Keystone facilities by foreign persons are registered at least ten business days in advance with the ECM, who determines whether the visit requires a license, controls areas and information visible to visitors and approves the visit agenda.

## 7. Access to Controlled Technology

7.1 **Technology control plan.** The ECM maintains a technology control plan that identifies controlled technology, the locations where it is stored, the people authorized to access it and the controls that prevent unauthorized access, including badge-controlled areas, logical access restrictions in the PLM system and the enclave maintained by Information Security.

7.2 **Deemed exports.** Before foreign persons, including employees, interns, contractors and visitors, are given access to technology, the ECM must determine whether a deemed export license is required. Offers of employment to foreign persons in engineering and manufacturing engineering roles are not finalized until the ECM completes this assessment.

7.3 **Electronic transmissions.** Controlled technology is not sent by email to destinations outside the United States. Cloud tools used to store or share controlled technology must keep data on servers in the United States and prevent access by foreign persons. Information Security and the ECM approve such tools.

7.4 **Production in Mexico.** Production at the Monterrey assembly cell involves technology that has been authorized for export to Mexico. The ECM reviews the authorization annually, and employees at Monterrey may not provide technology to persons in other countries without approval.

## 8. Shipping and Documentation

8.1 **Pre-shipment checks.** The shipping system blocks release of an export order until the ECM or a trained designee has recorded the classification, the screening result, the license determination and the shipping documentation.

8.2 **Electronic export information.** The shipping supervisor files electronic export information in the Automated Export System where required, using the classification and value approved for the order. Filings must be accurate and complete. The ECM reviews a sample of filings each month.

8.3 **Commercial documents.** The commercial invoice and packing list must show the correct description, value, country of origin, ECCN or USML category, and any license or exception number. Statements required by the regulations, such as destination control statements, are included on the invoice.

8.4 **Customs brokers and forwarders.** Freight forwarders and customs brokers are engaged only after screening and are given written instructions. The ECM must approve routing for controlled items.

## 9. Recordkeeping

9.1 Records of classifications, licenses, screening results, shipping documents, training and audits are kept for at least five years from the later of the date of export, the expiry of the license or the end of the transaction, as the regulations require. ITAR records are kept for at least five years from expiration of the authorization.

9.2 Records are kept in the export compliance repository, which is backed up and accessible to the ECM, Internal Audit and Legal. Employees must not discard export records without ECM approval.

## 10. Training

10.1 All employees complete a short awareness module during onboarding. Employees in sales, customer service, engineering, program management, shipping, purchasing and information technology complete role-based training each year. Employees who handle ITAR items complete additional training.

10.2 The ECM keeps a record of attendance and test results. Employees with overdue training lose authority to release or approve exports until training is complete.

## 11. Audits and Reporting of Violations

11.1 The ECM audits export transactions each quarter by sampling a number of orders from each plant and testing compliance with this policy. Internal Audit performs an independent review annually, and the results are reported to the Audit Committee.

11.2 Any employee who suspects a violation must report it immediately to the ECM or the General Counsel, or through the ethics line. If a violation is confirmed, the General Counsel decides, in consultation with outside counsel, whether to make a voluntary disclosure to the Department of Commerce, the Department of State or the Treasury Department, and in doing so seeks the benefit of mitigation available under the regulations.

11.3 Keystone does not retaliate against anyone who reports a possible violation in good faith.

## 12. Anti-Boycott

12.1 United States law prohibits participation in unsanctioned foreign boycotts, in particular the Arab League boycott of Israel. Employees must not agree to any request to participate in a boycott, supply information about boycott activities or refuse to do business with boycotted parties. All boycott-related requests received in purchase orders, letters of credit, questionnaires or other documents must be sent to the ECM at once. Certain requests must be reported to the Department of Commerce, and the ECM handles those reports.

## 13. Exceptions

13.1 This policy has no exception that permits an export in violation of law. Operational exceptions to internal procedures, such as the timing of screening for low-risk transactions, require the written approval of the ECM and the General Counsel and are reviewed at the next quarterly audit.

## 14. Enforcement

14.1 Violation of this policy may lead to discipline, up to and including termination. Employees who knowingly violate export laws may also be subject to personal civil and criminal liability.

14.2 Managers are accountable for ensuring that their teams follow the policy, and failure to supervise may also result in discipline.

## Appendix A. Frequently Asked Questions

A.1 **Do we need a license to send a drawing to a customer in Canada?** It depends on the classification of the technology and on whether Canada is a destination that requires a license for that classification. Items on the EAR99 list do not need a license for most destinations including Canada. ITAR technical data generally does require authorization even for Canada unless an exemption applies. Ask the ECM before sending anything.

A.2 **A new engineer on a work visa has joined the team. Can that engineer see our turbine blade drawings?** Not until the ECM has determined whether the drawings are controlled and, if so, whether the engineer's citizenship or permanent residency allows access. Release of controlled technology to a foreign person in the United States counts as an export to that person's home country. The manager should contact the ECM before the engineer's first day, not after.

A.3 **A customer's purchasing agent has asked us to ship parts to a freight forwarder in Dubai, saying the final destination is not yet known. May we proceed?** No. Shipping to a forwarder without a confirmed final destination is a red flag. The sales representative must ask for the end user and the final destination in writing. If the customer cannot provide them, the order must not ship, and the ECM must be told.

A.4 **We want to exhibit at a trade show in Germany. May we take sample parts and brochures?** The ECM needs to classify the samples and check whether a license or exception applies to temporary exports. Brochures that contain only general marketing descriptions normally do not require a license, but brochures with dimensional or process data may. A temporary export checklist is available from the ECM, and it must be completed at least fifteen business days before travel.

A.5 **A supplier in Mexico asks for a copy of a fixture design so it can quote. What should I do?** Do not send it. Fixture designs may be technology controlled under the EAR, and the supplier must be screened. Send the request to the ECM with the drawing number, and the ECM will tell you what may be released. For Monterrey, an authorization already exists for certain technology, but it covers only the assembly cell and not outside suppliers.

A.6 **Can I take my work laptop on holiday to Spain?** Employees should not travel with a laptop containing controlled technology without approval. For personal trips, leave the work laptop at home unless your manager and the ECM have approved. If you must take one, a loaner laptop without engineering files is available from IT.

A.7 **A distributor in Singapore wants to re-sell our parts to a customer in Vietnam. Is that allowed?** Re-export from Singapore to Vietnam may require authorization depending on the classification. The sales representative must tell the ECM about the intended resale, and the distributor will be asked for the end-user information. Do not approve or encourage any re-export before the ECM has cleared it.

A.8 **What should I do if a government agent calls or visits asking about an export?** Be courteous, take the person's name, agency and contact details, do not answer substantive questions or provide documents, and contact the General Counsel at once. The General Counsel will arrange the response with outside counsel.

A.9 **Where can I find the classification of a part?** The classification is shown on the part master record in the enterprise resource planning system and on the sales order. If a part number has no classification, shipping will be blocked. Send a classification request form to the ECM.

A.10 **What is the difference between the ITAR and the EAR?** The ITAR, administered by the Department of State, controls defense articles and services specifically designed for military use, as listed on the United States Munitions List. The EAR, administered by the Department of Commerce, controls items for commercial use and "dual-use" items that have both commercial and military applications, as listed on the Commerce Control List. Some parts made by Keystone are subject to one regime and some to the other, and the difference matters because the ITAR is generally stricter and has fewer exceptions.
