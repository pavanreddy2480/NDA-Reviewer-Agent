# Intellectual Property Policy

**Company:** Northwind Cloud, Inc.
**Document owner:** Office of the General Counsel (IP Counsel)
**Policy number:** LEG-012
**Effective date:** May 12, 2025
**Applies to:** All Northwind workers, contractors and consultants who create, use or share Northwind intellectual property or the intellectual property of others

---

## 1. Purpose, Scope and Definitions

### 1.1 Purpose

Northwind Cloud, Inc. ("Northwind") creates value primarily through software: its platform source code, machine-learning models, data schemas, user interface designs, documentation, training materials, brand names and the know-how of its engineers. This Policy explains how Northwind identifies, protects, uses and, where appropriate, shares its intellectual property ("IP"), and how it avoids infringing the IP of others.

### 1.2 Scope

The Policy applies to all IP that Northwind owns, licenses or receives from others, in every country where it operates. It applies to employees, officers, interns, contractors and consultants, and to anyone else who has access to Northwind IP.

### 1.3 Definitions

- **Intellectual Property** means patents, copyrights, trade secrets, trademarks, trade dress, domain names, database rights, moral rights and all other rights in inventions, works of authorship and know-how, whether registered or not.
- **Northwind IP** means IP owned by Northwind, including IP created by workers within the scope of their work.
- **Third-Party IP** means IP owned by others, including customers, vendors, partners and open source communities.
- **Invention** means any new or improved process, algorithm, design, technique, product or method, whether or not patentable.
- **Trade secret** means information that derives value from being secret and is subject to reasonable measures to keep it secret.
- **Value Band** has the meaning in the Contract Approval Matrix.
- **IP Counsel** means the attorneys in the Legal Department with responsibility for IP matters.
- **IP Review Board** means the committee of the Chief Technology Officer, the General Counsel, the VP Engineering and the Chief Product Officer that decides on filings and open-source releases.

## 2. Protecting What We Hand Over and What We Receive

### 2.1 Ownership stays put

No clause may transfer ownership of our information, or of anything derived from it, to the other side. This applies to the underlying information, to improvements, analyses and models built on it, and to any feedback or suggestions that would reveal it. When a customer or partner asks for a special arrangement on ownership of work created together, the request goes to IP Counsel and is dealt with in a separate, signed development or services agreement rather than in the paperwork that governs ordinary exchanges of information. Section 4.5 of the Confidentiality and NDA Standard contains the related rule about rights not being granted.

### 2.2 Independent development on high-value deals

For deals in the high value band, we do not accept language that lets the other side independently develop information similar to ours. The reasoning is that large, strategic deals typically involve the architectures, algorithms and roadmaps that give Northwind its edge, and a counterparty that is free to build something similar can claim independent development after learning how Northwind's systems work. In lower value bands IP Counsel may accept such language where the information shared is not core to Northwind's competitive position. The Confidentiality and NDA Standard states the related rule on rights not being granted.

### 2.3 Freedom to obtain similar information elsewhere

The agreement must preserve the right of the receiving party to obtain similar information lawfully from an independent third party. Northwind receives information from many sources and must remain free to use publicly available materials, open source code and information supplied by other companies, without a claim that it was taken from a particular Counterparty.

### 2.4 Clean-room practices

Where Northwind engineers are exposed to a Counterparty's technical information and are later asked to work on a similar product, the engineering manager must consult IP Counsel. IP Counsel may require a clean-room process, documented separation of teams or a cooling-off period to guard against claims of contamination.

## 3. Employees and Contributors

### 3.1 Notice of immunity for employees

Agreements with employees governed by United States law must include the notice of immunity for confidential disclosure to government officials and attorneys that federal trade secret law requires. Without the notice, Northwind could lose the ability to recover exemplary damages and attorney fees in a trade secret case against the employee. Human Resources and the Legal Department maintain the approved notice language in the clause library, and Human Resources checks for it when employee paperwork is issued.

### 3.2 Invention assignment

All employees and contractors sign an invention assignment agreement in the form approved by the General Counsel before starting work. Under that agreement, IP created within the scope of work, or using Northwind resources, belongs to Northwind. State laws that limit assignment (for example in California, Washington and Illinois) are reflected in the approved forms, and the form for each state is determined by the employee's work location.

### 3.3 Disclosure of inventions

Workers must promptly disclose any invention made in the course of their work to IP Counsel through the invention disclosure form. IP Counsel evaluates the invention and recommends whether to file a patent application, protect it as a trade secret, publish it defensively or take no action.

### 3.4 Moonlighting and outside work

Workers must disclose outside activity that could involve IP related to Northwind's business, including freelance engineering, advisory roles, academic collaborations and open-source contributions made in their own time. The Conflicts Committee decides whether the work may continue and on what conditions.

### 3.5 Departing workers

When a worker leaves, the manager and Human Resources confirm that all Northwind devices, data and credentials have been returned or revoked, and remind the worker of their continuing obligations regarding Northwind IP. IT disables access on the last day of work.

## 4. Patents

### 4.1 Patent strategy

Northwind files patents selectively, on inventions that are central to the platform and likely to be of enduring value. The IP Review Board meets quarterly to consider invention disclosures. Budget for filings is set annually by the Chief Financial Officer on the recommendation of the Chief Technology Officer.

### 4.2 Inventor recognition

Named inventors on a filed application receive a recognition award under the Inventor Recognition Program, and a further award when a patent issues. Details are published on the intranet.

### 4.3 Freedom to operate

Before launching a feature that may implicate known patents, product managers may ask IP Counsel for a freedom-to-operate review. Workers must not read, discuss or circulate third-party patents for the purpose of designing around them without first speaking to IP Counsel, because doing so can increase exposure to claims of willful infringement.

## 5. Copyrights and Software

### 5.1 Copyright notices

All Northwind source files carry a copyright notice and a reference to the license under which they are made available. Documentation, marketing collateral and training materials carry the notice "© Northwind Cloud, Inc. All rights reserved."

### 5.2 Third-party software

No third-party code may be added to a Northwind product without approval under the Open Source Software Policy or, for commercial components, under the Vendor and Third-Party Risk Policy.

### 5.3 Generative AI tools

Engineers and other workers may use approved generative AI tools to assist their work, subject to the following rules. They must not enter Restricted information or personal data into any tool that is not approved by the Security team and the Privacy Office. They must review generated code for license contamination and security flaws. Generated content that is incorporated into Northwind products is subject to the same review and approval as any other contribution.

## 6. Trademarks and Brand

### 6.1 Brand use

The Marketing department controls use of the Northwind name, logo and product names. Outside parties, including customers and partners, may use them only under a written trademark license or as permitted by the brand guidelines.

### 6.2 New names

Before adopting a new product or feature name, Marketing asks IP Counsel to clear it by searching trademark registers and domain names in the countries of intended use. IP Counsel files applications for names that have strategic value.

### 6.3 Domain names

All domain names are registered by IT in the company's name, on the corporate registrar account. Workers must not register domain names that include Northwind marks in their own name.

## 7. Trade Secrets

### 7.1 What counts

Northwind's trade secrets include source code, model weights and training data, infrastructure architecture, performance tuning methods, security configurations, product roadmaps and pricing models.

### 7.2 Reasonable measures

To keep trade secret protection, Northwind classifies such materials as Restricted, limits access to those with a need to know, logs access, requires training and exit procedures, and ensures that those given access are bound by written obligations. The Confidentiality and NDA Standard describes the classification levels and handling controls.

### 7.3 Suspected misappropriation

A worker who believes that Northwind trade secrets have been taken, leaked or misused must immediately notify the General Counsel and the Security team. The General Counsel may authorize forensic review, preservation of evidence and enforcement action.

## 8. Open Source

Northwind contributes to a limited number of open source projects, subject to the approval of the IP Review Board. Contributions must be made through the company account, not a personal account, and must be reviewed for the removal of credentials, customer data and proprietary code. The Open Source Software Policy governs inbound use of open source components.

## 9. Respecting the IP of Others

Northwind workers must not copy, use or distribute third-party IP without permission. In particular, they must not install unlicensed software, use images or fonts that are not licensed for commercial use, copy text from competitor documentation, or bring any previous employer's confidential materials to Northwind. A worker who receives a claim that Northwind has infringed the IP of another must forward it at once to IP Counsel and must not respond. The Legal Department keeps a log of claims and responds to takedown notices in line with the Digital Millennium Copyright Act.

## 10. Roles and Responsibilities

- **General Counsel** owns the Policy and chairs the IP Review Board.
- **IP Counsel** advises on filings, clearances, claims, clean-room practices and the rules in Parts 2 and 3.
- **Chief Technology Officer** is accountable for technical measures to protect source code and for patent strategy.
- **Human Resources** ensures that invention assignment forms and notices are issued and filed.
- **Managers** make sure their teams follow the Policy, disclose inventions and complete training.
- **Marketing** manages brand and trademark use.
- **Security team** operates controls over Restricted repositories.
- **All workers** protect Northwind IP and respect the IP of others.

## 11. Training

New engineers complete IP and open-source training in their first month. All workers complete an annual refresher. IP Counsel gives targeted sessions to product managers on clearance searches and freedom-to-operate questions.

## 12. Enforcement

Failure to follow this Policy may result in discipline up to termination. The Company may also seek legal remedies against any person who misappropriates Northwind IP or infringes the IP of others.

## Appendix A. Invention Disclosure Process

1. **Disclose.** The inventor completes the disclosure form describing the problem, the solution, how it differs from known approaches, any public disclosures, and any use of third-party or open source code.
2. **Triage.** IP Counsel acknowledges the disclosure within five business days and, with the engineering manager, rates its business value and patentability.
3. **Review.** The IP Review Board considers highly rated disclosures at its next quarterly meeting. Time-sensitive disclosures can be reviewed by email.
4. **Decide.** The Board decides to file a patent application, to protect the invention as a trade secret, to publish defensively or to take no action.
5. **Prosecute.** If filing, outside patent counsel prepares the application with the inventor. The inventor reviews drafts and signs necessary declarations.
6. **Maintain.** IP Counsel tracks deadlines, pays fees and reports annually on the portfolio.

## Appendix B. Marking Guidelines

| Asset | Required marking |
|---|---|
| Source files | Copyright notice and license identifier in the header |
| Product screens | Copyright line in the footer |
| Slide decks for external audiences | Copyright line on the title and closing slides |
| Documentation | Copyright line on the first page and in the footer |
| Brand assets | Trademark symbols at first prominent use |
| Internal design documents | Classification label and owner |

## Appendix C. Patent Portfolio Review

Each year IP Counsel and the Chief Technology Officer review the patent portfolio. Each patent or application is rated as core, useful or surplus. Surplus assets may be abandoned, licensed or sold to lower costs. Core assets are mapped to products and to competitors, so that the company can see which assets give real leverage. The review also considers whether engineering roadmaps are creating new inventions that have not been disclosed. The Chief Financial Officer receives a summary of costs and the proposed budget for the next year.

## Appendix D. Common Questions

**Can I use a stock photograph I found online in a slide?** Only if it comes from a source licensed for commercial use, such as the company's stock library, and the license allows the use you plan. Images found through a web search are usually protected.

**Can I reuse code I wrote at my previous employer?** No. Code written for a previous employer almost always belongs to that employer. Do not bring code, documents or data from a previous employer.

**Who owns a model fine-tuned on a customer's data?** Ownership depends on the commercial terms agreed with that customer. Engineers must check with IP Counsel before training any model on customer data, and must record the dataset and license in the model registry.

**Can I post a technical blog about something I built at Northwind?** Yes, after the Marketing and Engineering leads have reviewed the draft to confirm that it does not reveal trade secrets or unreleased plans, and that it uses approved branding.
