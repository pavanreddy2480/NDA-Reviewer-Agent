# Confidentiality and NDA Standard

**Company:** Northwind Cloud, Inc.
**Document owner:** Office of the General Counsel
**Policy number:** LEG-004
**Effective date:** March 1, 2025
**Applies to:** All employees, contractors and officers of Northwind Cloud, Inc. and its subsidiaries

---

## 1. Purpose

Northwind Cloud, Inc. ("Northwind", "we", "us") builds and operates a multi-tenant software-as-a-service platform for enterprise customers in North America and Europe. Our customers, vendors and partners trust us with source code, product roadmaps, pricing, security architecture, customer lists and, very often, personal data about their own employees and clients. Our own competitive position depends on the same kind of information flowing in the other direction.

This Standard sets out how Northwind classifies and protects confidential information, who is allowed to see it, and the minimum protections the Legal Department expects to find in the written agreements that govern our exchange of such information with outside parties. It is intended to give business teams a single, practical reference so that they can move quickly on commercial opportunities without exposing the company to avoidable risk.

## 2. Scope and Definitions

### 2.1 Scope

This Standard applies to every Northwind worker and to all information that Northwind creates, receives or holds, in any form: electronic files, source repositories, tickets, chat messages, slide decks, whiteboards, printed pages and spoken remarks. It applies in every office, in remote work locations and on any device used for company business.

### 2.2 Definitions

- **Confidential Information** means non-public information that Northwind or a third party has entrusted to Northwind and that a reasonable person would understand to be sensitive. It includes information that is not labelled.
- **Counterparty** means a customer, vendor, partner, prospective employee or other outside person or company with which Northwind exchanges Confidential Information.
- **Disclosing party** and **receiving party** mean, in any given exchange, the party that shares information and the party that receives it. In a two-way exchange each party is both.
- **Information Owner** means the Northwind executive accountable for a body of information, for example the Chief Technology Officer for source code or the Chief Financial Officer for unreleased financial results.
- **Deal Owner** means the Northwind employee sponsoring a commercial relationship with a Counterparty.
- **Legal Reviewer** means an attorney, paralegal or contract specialist in the Legal Department who has been assigned to a request.

## 3. Classification of Information

### 3.1 Classification levels

Northwind uses four levels. Each level has handling rules that apply regardless of the medium in which the information is held.

| Level | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Approved for release to anyone | Marketing pages, published documentation, press releases |
| Internal | Meant for workers only; low harm if exposed | Org charts, internal wiki pages, meeting notes |
| Confidential | Harm to Northwind or a Counterparty if exposed | Customer lists, pricing models, unreleased roadmaps, contract drafts |
| Restricted | Severe harm if exposed; need-to-know only | Source code for core services, encryption keys, security assessments, unreleased financial results, personal data in bulk |

### 3.2 Who classifies

The Information Owner sets the classification of the information in their charge. A worker who creates a document and is unsure of its level should treat it as Confidential until the Information Owner or the Security team says otherwise. Information received from a Counterparty takes the level that the Counterparty's own labelling or the nature of the information suggests, and never a level below Confidential.

### 3.3 Reclassification

Information may be reclassified downward only by the Information Owner, and only after consulting the Security team. A record of the decision must be kept in the document management system. Information may be reclassified upward by any manager who believes the existing level is too low.

## 4. Protections We Expect in Counterparty Agreements

This Part describes what the Legal Department looks for when Northwind signs, or asks a Counterparty to sign, a written agreement for the exchange of Confidential Information. The Legal Department publishes the model language separately in the clause library; this Standard states the principle behind each protection. A Deal Owner may not agree to depart from these protections without the approvals described in Part 7.

### 4.1 Purpose of use

Every agreement we sign or propose must limit the receiving party's use of our information to the purposes the agreement itself states, and no other purpose. The stated purpose should be specific enough that a reader outside the deal team can tell whether a proposed use falls inside it, for example "evaluating a potential subscription to the Northwind platform" rather than "business purposes".

### 4.2 Spoken disclosures

Information that we share in meetings, calls or demonstrations must be covered even though it is spoken and never written down. Product engineers regularly give architecture walkthroughs and security briefings that are never followed by a written summary, and an agreement that protects only documents would leave those conversations exposed.

### 4.3 Duties that continue

The agreement must make clear that the duties of confidence continue to bind the parties after the agreement has ended or expired. How long those duties continue is addressed in Part 6 and in the Records Retention and Disposal Policy, which sets the outer limit on how long Northwind can administer them.

### 4.4 Legal compulsion

If the other side is compelled by law, regulation or a court or agency process to disclose our information, it must tell us before it does so. Prompt notice gives us the chance to seek a protective order or to narrow the request. When Northwind is itself the subject of a subpoena or a government request, the General Counsel decides how to respond and no worker may reply directly.

### 4.5 No grant of rights

No clause may be read as handing the other side a licence, ownership interest or any other right in the information we disclose. Where a commercial relationship does involve a licence, that licence belongs in a separate, signed commercial agreement. The Intellectual Property Policy explains how Northwind treats ownership of information more generally.

### 4.6 Protections for information received

When Northwind receives Counterparty information, workers must follow the handling rules in Part 8 of this Standard, store the information only in approved systems, and record the receipt in the deal file.

## 5. Customer and Partner Relationships

### 5.1 Breadth of the definition

In agreements with customers and partners, the definition of protected information must reach business, financial and commercial information and may not be confined to technical material alone. Our enterprise customers routinely share procurement plans, user volumes and budget constraints with our sales engineers, and our partners share go-to-market plans that have no technical content at all.

### 5.2 Hiring and approaching people

An agreement with a customer must not bar either side from approaching or hiring the other side's people. Our customers include some of the largest employers in the sectors we serve, and a clause of this kind could limit our own recruiting in ways that the business has never approved. Restrictions of this kind in partner relationships are handled under the Contract Approval Matrix.

### 5.3 The relationship itself

The other side must agree not to reveal that the parties have negotiated or entered into the agreement. Several of our customers are named reference accounts only after their own communications teams have cleared a case study, and an early leak of a pending deal can affect both sides' negotiations with others.

### 5.4 Remembered information

We never accept a clause that lets the other side keep using whatever it happens to remember from our information, often called a residuals clause. Our engineers and product managers talk to Counterparty staff at length, and such a clause would permit the other side's staff to carry away and reuse what they learned.

## 6. Duration of Protection

### 6.1 Minimum period

The duty of confidence must run for at least 24 months. Any shorter period needs written approval from the Deputy General Counsel before the agreement is signed. When personal data is involved, the longer minimum in the Data Protection and Privacy Policy applies instead.

### 6.2 Maximum period

The duty of confidence may not be stated to run for more than 60 months. Open-ended obligations are difficult to administer, cannot be monitored by the business, and conflict with the records schedule.

### 6.3 Surviving obligations

Obligations that survive the end of the agreement must survive for at least 24 months. The upper limit on how long such obligations may be stated to survive is set in the Records Retention and Disposal Policy.

## 7. Exceptions and Approvals

A request to depart from any protection in Parts 4 through 6 is made through the Legal Intake portal. The Legal Reviewer will record the proposed departure, the business reason and the compensating controls offered. Approvals are given by the persons named in the relevant section, or, where none is named, by the General Counsel. Approval is granted in writing, applies to one agreement only, and does not create a precedent for later requests. The Legal Department keeps a register of approvals for audit purposes.

## 8. Handling Rules for Confidential and Restricted Information

### 8.1 Storage

Confidential and Restricted information must be stored only in systems approved by the Security team: the company document management system, the engineering source-control platform and the corporate cloud storage tenant. Personal cloud accounts, personal email and unapproved messaging tools must not be used.

### 8.2 Sharing inside Northwind

Access to Restricted information is granted by the Information Owner on a need-to-know basis and reviewed every quarter. Access to Confidential information may be granted by a department head. Access is removed on the day a worker changes roles or leaves the company.

### 8.3 Sharing outside Northwind

Before Confidential or Restricted information is sent to anyone outside Northwind, the sender must confirm that a signed agreement is in place with the recipient, using the Legal Intake portal's lookup tool. Information may be transmitted only by approved secure channels. Restricted information must additionally be encrypted in transit and at rest.

### 8.4 Printing and physical media

Restricted information must not be printed except with the Information Owner's approval. Printed Confidential information must be collected from the printer promptly, kept in a locked drawer when unattended and destroyed in a cross-cut shredder or a locked shred bin. Removable media may be used only with encryption and must be registered with IT.

### 8.5 Meetings and public places

Workers must not discuss Confidential or Restricted matters in elevators, restaurants, airports, co-working lounges or other public places where they might be overheard. Video calls involving Restricted information must use a private room or headphones. Workers must not leave screens unlocked.

### 8.6 Visitors

Visitors to Northwind offices must sign in at reception, wear a visitor badge and be escorted at all times. Whiteboards in meeting rooms must be erased after use, and meeting rooms must be checked for left-behind documents.

## 9. Roles and Responsibilities

- **General Counsel** owns this Standard, approves exceptions where no other approver is named and reports to the Audit Committee annually on compliance.
- **Deputy General Counsel** supervises the Legal Intake process and approves the departures assigned to that office.
- **Information Owners** classify information, grant access and review access lists every quarter.
- **Deal Owners** identify at the outset of a relationship what information will be exchanged, request a Legal Reviewer through the Legal Intake portal and keep the deal file complete.
- **Legal Reviewers** review requests, apply this Standard and record exceptions in the register.
- **The Security team** maintains approved systems, investigates suspected exposure and advises on classification.
- **Managers** make sure their teams complete training and follow this Standard.
- **All workers** protect the information entrusted to them and report suspected exposure immediately.

## 10. Training and Awareness

All new workers complete confidentiality training within their first two weeks and annually after that. Engineers with access to Restricted source code complete an additional module on secure handling. The Legal Department runs quarterly lunch-and-learn sessions for sales and customer success teams, focused on the questions most often raised in the Legal Intake portal. Completion is tracked by Human Resources and reported to department heads each quarter.

## 11. Reporting a Suspected Exposure

A worker who suspects that Confidential or Restricted information has been lost, misdirected or accessed without authority must report it at once to the Security team through the incident hotline or the security mailbox. The report should say what information was involved, who might have seen it, when it happened and whether any Counterparty is affected. The Security team opens an incident and the General Counsel decides whether a Counterparty or regulator must be notified. No worker may contact a Counterparty about a suspected exposure without the General Counsel's approval. Reports made in good faith are never grounds for discipline.

## 12. Monitoring and Audit

The Security team monitors access to Restricted repositories and reviews unusual download or sharing activity. Internal Audit samples signed counterparty agreements each year and reports the results to the General Counsel and the Audit Committee. The sample covers whether the Legal Intake portal was used, whether approvals were recorded and whether the deal file is complete. Findings are tracked until closed.

## 13. Enforcement

A breach of this Standard may lead to disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or contract, and, where the law allows, civil or criminal referral. Managers who knowingly allow a breach, or who retaliate against a worker for reporting one, are also subject to discipline. The General Counsel may suspend a worker's access to Confidential and Restricted information while an investigation is under way.

## 14. Relationship to Other Policies

This Standard should be read with the Vendor and Third-Party Risk Policy, the Data Protection and Privacy Policy, the Contract Approval Matrix, the Intellectual Property Policy, the Records Retention and Disposal Policy and the Information Security Acceptable Use Policy. If this Standard conflicts with a more specific policy on the same subject, the more specific policy controls, unless the General Counsel decides otherwise in writing.

## Appendix A. Frequently Asked Questions

**Who decides what classification a customer's data takes?** The Information Owner for the relevant system decides, in consultation with the Security team. Customer content hosted in the platform is always treated as Restricted, because Northwind cannot know what each customer has stored.

**May I take a Confidential document home?** Only on a company-managed device and only if you need to work on it outside the office. Do not print it at home, and do not leave it open where family members or visitors can see the screen.

**May I forward a Confidential email to my personal account so that I can read it on my phone?** No. Install the approved mobile application and read it there. Forwarding company mail to personal accounts is prohibited by the Information Security Acceptable Use Policy.

**What do I do if I receive something that was clearly not meant for me?** Stop reading, do not forward it, tell the sender and the Security team, and delete it once they confirm. If the material appears to belong to another company, tell the Legal Department as well.

**Can I mention a customer's name in a conference talk?** Only if the customer has approved the use in writing through Marketing. Customer names, logos and metrics are Confidential until they are approved for reference use.

**How long should I keep my own notes?** Notes of meetings that evidence a decision or commitment should be saved to the deal file. Personal scratch notes should be destroyed when they are no longer useful, in line with the Records Retention and Disposal Policy.

## Appendix B. Examples of Classification

| Item | Level | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Public product documentation | Public | Published on the website |
| Quarterly all-hands slides without financials | Internal | Of interest to staff only |
| A customer's usage report | Confidential | Contains the customer's business metrics |
| Production database snapshot | Restricted | Contains personal data in bulk |
| Unreleased pricing proposal | Confidential | Harm if competitors see it |
| Signing keys for software releases | Restricted | Compromise would affect every customer |
| Board materials before release | Restricted | Material non-public information |
| Interview notes about a job candidate | Confidential | Personal and sensitive |
| Penetration test report | Restricted | Details unpatched weaknesses |
| Sales call recording | Confidential | May contain customer business plans and personal data |

## Appendix C. Responsibilities Checklist for Deal Owners

Before sharing information with a Counterparty, the Deal Owner should be able to answer yes to each of the following.

1. Is the information at the lowest level of detail needed for the purpose?
2. Has the Information Owner agreed that it can be shared?
3. Has a Legal Reviewer been assigned, and is the signed paperwork on file?
4. Will the information be sent through an approved channel?
5. Is the list of Counterparty recipients known and recorded in the deal file?
6. Is a date set for review of what has been shared and for what must be returned or removed?
7. Does the deal file record the Northwind people who have been told about the exchange?

## Appendix D. Quarterly Access Review Procedure

Each quarter the Security team sends every Information Owner a list of the people who hold access to their Restricted information. The owner confirms, removes or queries each entry within ten business days. Entries not confirmed in time are removed automatically. The Security team keeps the results for three years and sends a summary to the General Counsel. Owners should look especially for people who have changed teams, contractors whose engagements have ended and accounts that have not been used in ninety days.
