# Code of Business Conduct

**Company:** Northwind Cloud, Inc.
**Document owner:** Chief Compliance Officer, reporting to the General Counsel
**Policy number:** CMP-001
**Effective date:** January 20, 2025
**Applies to:** All directors, officers, employees, contractors and interns of Northwind Cloud, Inc. and its subsidiaries

---

## 1. A Message From Our Chief Executive Officer

Northwind earned the trust of its customers one release, one support ticket and one renewal at a time. That trust is the most valuable thing we own, and it can be damaged much faster than it can be built. This Code of Business Conduct (the "Code") describes the standards of honesty, fairness and respect that we expect from each other. It cannot cover every situation. Where it is silent, ask yourself whether you would be comfortable having your decision described in the press, explained to your family and defended to a customer. If you are unsure, ask. Nobody has ever been disciplined at Northwind for asking a question in good faith.

## 2. Purpose and Scope

The Code applies to everyone who works for Northwind Cloud, Inc. ("Northwind") anywhere in the world, to members of the Board of Directors when acting in that role, and to agents and representatives who act on Northwind's behalf. Managers have extra responsibility: they must lead by example, make sure that their teams know the Code and never pressure anyone to break it.

## 3. Definitions

- **Conflict of interest** means a situation in which a personal interest, relationship or outside activity could influence, or appear to influence, a decision made for Northwind.
- **Government official** means anyone who works for or on behalf of a government, a government-owned company, a political party or a public international organization, and includes employees of public universities and public hospitals.
- **Family member** means a spouse or partner, parent, child, sibling, in-law, grandparent, grandchild and anyone who shares your household.
- **Ethics hotline** means the independent, third-party reporting service available around the clock by phone and web.
- **Compliance Office** means the Chief Compliance Officer and the team who administer the Code.

## 4. Our Core Principles

1. **We obey the law.** We comply with the laws and regulations of every place where we operate.
2. **We deal honestly.** We do not lie, mislead, cheat or steal, and we keep our promises.
3. **We treat people with respect.** We value diversity, and we do not tolerate harassment, discrimination or retaliation.
4. **We put customers first.** We build products that serve customers well and we are straightforward about their strengths and limits.
5. **We protect what we are trusted with.** We guard company assets and the property of others.
6. **We speak up.** We raise concerns early and support others who do.

## 5. Respect in the Workplace

### 5.1 Equal opportunity

Northwind provides equal employment opportunity to all workers and applicants regardless of race, color, religion, sex, pregnancy, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, national origin, age, disability, veteran status, genetic information or any other characteristic protected by law. Decisions about hiring, pay, promotion, discipline and termination are made on merit.

### 5.2 Harassment

Harassment, whether sexual, racial or of any other kind, is prohibited in the office, at company events, online and in any setting connected to work. Harassment includes unwelcome comments, jokes, images, messages, touching, exclusion and threats. Workers who experience or witness it should report it to their manager, Human Resources or the ethics hotline.

### 5.3 Accommodation

Northwind will make reasonable accommodations for disability, pregnancy and religious practice unless doing so would cause undue hardship. Workers who need an accommodation should contact Human Resources.

### 5.4 Health and safety

Northwind provides a safe workplace. Workers must follow safety rules, report hazards and injuries immediately, and never work under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs. Violence or threats of violence are not tolerated, and weapons are not allowed on company premises.

## 6. Conflicts of Interest

### 6.1 General rule

Workers must avoid situations where their personal interests conflict, or appear to conflict, with their duties to Northwind. When a conflict cannot be avoided, it must be disclosed to the manager and the Compliance Office as soon as it arises.

### 6.2 Common conflicts

Examples requiring disclosure and approval include:

- a financial interest of more than one percent in a company that is a competitor, customer or vendor of Northwind;
- hiring, supervising or influencing the pay of a family member;
- accepting a position as a director, adviser or employee of another company, particularly a competitor or vendor;
- using Northwind's time, equipment or information for outside business activities;
- directing company business to a firm in which you or a family member has an interest; and
- taking for yourself an opportunity that you learn about through your work for Northwind.

### 6.3 Review

The Compliance Office reviews each disclosure and decides whether to approve it, require changes or prohibit it. Decisions are recorded, and approvals are reviewed annually. Conflicts involving officers or directors are decided by the Audit Committee.

## 7. Gifts, Meals and Entertainment

Workers may give or receive gifts, meals and entertainment only if they are modest, infrequent, legal, openly given, consistent with accepted business custom and not intended to influence a business decision. A single gift with a value over $100, or any gift from a firm that is taking part in a tender, must be disclosed to the Compliance Office. Cash and cash equivalents are never allowed. Gifts to government officials are subject to special rules and require the General Counsel's approval in advance.

## 8. Anti-Corruption and Anti-Bribery

Northwind prohibits bribery in any form. Workers must not offer, promise, give, request or accept anything of value to improperly influence a decision or to obtain an advantage, whether the recipient is a public official or a private person. The U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, the U.K. Bribery Act and similar laws apply to Northwind everywhere in the world. Workers must not make facilitation payments, and must be careful when engaging resellers, consultants and other intermediaries, who must be vetted by the Compliance Office before they work for Northwind. Political contributions and charitable donations in Northwind's name require the Chief Executive Officer's and General Counsel's approval.

## 9. Fair Competition

Northwind competes vigorously and fairly. Workers must not agree with competitors on prices, discounts, bids, customers, territories or output, and must not exchange competitively sensitive information with competitors, including at trade associations and conferences. Workers must not make false or misleading statements about competitors' products, and may gather competitive intelligence only from public sources or ethically obtained information. Questions about pricing practices, bundling, exclusivity or dealings with resellers should go to the Legal Department before action is taken.

## 10. Sales Practices and Accuracy With Customers

Salespeople must describe the platform accurately and avoid promising features, dates or service levels that are not approved. Order forms must be complete and accurate, and nothing may be promised through informal assurances. Revenue must be recorded only when it has been earned under the company's accounting policies. Workers must never alter dates, backdate documents or shift revenue to meet a target.

## 11. Accurate Books and Records

Northwind's books, records and financial statements must be complete, accurate and timely. Workers must never falsify documents, create off-the-books accounts, misclassify expenses or conceal transactions. Anyone who becomes aware of any questionable accounting or auditing matter should report it to the Chief Financial Officer, the Compliance Office or the ethics hotline. Reports about accounting matters go directly to the Audit Committee. Workers must cooperate with auditors and must never mislead or pressure them.

## 12. Insider Trading

Workers who learn material non-public information about Northwind or other companies, such as unreleased financial results, pending acquisitions, major customer wins or losses or security incidents, must not buy or sell securities on the basis of it, and must not pass it on to others. Directors, officers and designated employees may trade in Northwind securities only during open trading windows and with pre-clearance from the General Counsel.

## 13. Protecting Company Assets

Workers must use company assets, including funds, equipment, software and the Northwind name, only for legitimate business purposes. Theft, carelessness and waste have a direct impact on the company's profitability. Details of acceptable system use appear in the Information Security Acceptable Use Policy.

## 14. International Business

Northwind complies with export controls, economic sanctions and customs laws. Workers must not engage with parties on the U.S. sanctions lists, and must check with the Compliance Office before dealing with a country or entity that is subject to restriction. Northwind does not take part in boycotts that violate U.S. law. Workers who work with colleagues or customers in other countries should respect local laws and customs, and apply the higher of local law and this Code where they differ.

## 15. Working With Governments

Dealings with government customers and public bodies are subject to special rules on procurement, gifts, lobbying and conflicts. Workers who work on government business must follow the Public Sector Handbook. Any inquiry from a regulator, law enforcement agency or investigator should be sent to the Legal Department immediately.

## 16. Communications and Social Media

Only designated spokespersons may speak for Northwind to the media, analysts and investors. Workers who receive an inquiry should refer it to Communications. Workers may use social media in their personal lives but must be clear that they speak for themselves, must not disclose non-public information about Northwind, and must not harass others.

## 17. Raising Concerns and Non-Retaliation

### 17.1 How to raise a concern

Workers who believe the Code, a law or a company policy has been or may be violated should speak to their manager, Human Resources, the Compliance Office, the Legal Department or the ethics hotline. Reports may be made anonymously where the law allows.

### 17.2 Investigations

The Compliance Office reviews each report promptly and decides who should investigate. Investigations are fair, thorough and as discreet as possible. Workers must cooperate and tell the truth. They must not interfere with an investigation or discuss it with others except as directed.

### 17.3 Non-retaliation

Northwind forbids retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith or takes part in an investigation. Retaliation is itself a violation of the Code and will lead to discipline.

## 18. Accountability and Discipline

Violating the Code or the law can lead to discipline, up to and including termination, and may lead to civil or criminal penalties. Managers who direct or approve violations, or who fail to act when they know of them, are also accountable. Disciplinary decisions are made consistently by Human Resources with the Compliance Office. Waivers of the Code for directors and executive officers may be granted only by the Board and will be disclosed as required by law.

## 19. Training and Annual Certification

All workers complete Code training on joining and every year, and certify that they have read and understood the Code and have disclosed any actual or potential conflicts. Managers also complete training on handling reports and avoiding retaliation. Completion rates are reported to the Audit Committee.

## Appendix A. Questions to Ask Before You Act

1. Is it legal?
2. Is it consistent with the Code and Northwind's policies?
3. Does it treat customers, colleagues, partners and the company fairly?
4. Would I be comfortable if it were reported in the news?
5. Would I be comfortable explaining it to my manager and the Compliance Office?
6. Is there any chance that it benefits me personally at the expense of Northwind?
7. If I am unsure, have I asked someone who can help?

## Appendix B. Examples of Situations to Raise

**A vendor offers season tickets to a major league baseball team.** Politely decline or consult the Compliance Office. Season tickets are not modest or infrequent, and the vendor may have a bid before Northwind.

**A manager asks a team member to record a sale in the quarter even though the customer has not yet signed.** This is improper revenue recognition. Report it to the Chief Financial Officer or the ethics hotline.

**A colleague's spouse owns a company that bids for a Northwind purchase.** The colleague must disclose the relationship and must not take part in the decision.

**A friend at a competitor asks about next quarter's pricing.** Do not discuss it. Competitively sensitive information must not be exchanged with competitors.

**A teammate frequently makes jokes about a coworker's accent.** This may be harassment. Report it to Human Resources or the ethics hotline, or speak to the manager.

**A customer's procurement officer hints that a gift would help the renewal go smoothly.** Decline and report it to the Compliance Office at once.

## Appendix C. Ethics Hotline Operating Procedures

The ethics hotline is operated by an independent provider and is available in more than twenty languages, around the clock. Callers may remain anonymous. The provider gives each reporter an access code, so that they can follow up and answer questions without revealing their identity. Reports are sent to the Chief Compliance Officer, or, for matters concerning the Compliance Office, the Chair of the Audit Committee. The Compliance Office acknowledges reports within two business days, opens a case, assigns an investigator and gives the reporter progress updates where possible. The Compliance Office reports statistics to the Audit Committee every quarter, including the number and type of reports, time to close, substantiation rates and outcomes.

## Appendix D. Investigation Principles

Investigations follow principles of fairness and proportionality. Investigators remain impartial and declare any conflicts. They gather facts from documents and interviews, give the subject an opportunity to respond, and document their findings. Where appropriate, they recommend remedial steps, such as training, process changes, discipline or reports to authorities. Human Resources is consulted before discipline. Records of investigations are kept by the Compliance Office for seven years and are available to the Audit Committee.

## Appendix E. Community Involvement

Northwind encourages workers to take part in their communities. The company offers each worker two paid days a year to volunteer with a charity of their choice and matches donations up to $1,000 per worker per year to registered nonprofit organizations. Workers must make clear that they speak for themselves when taking part in community or political activity, and must not use company time or resources for political campaigns.
