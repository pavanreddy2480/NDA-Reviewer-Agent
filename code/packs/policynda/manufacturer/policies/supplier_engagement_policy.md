# Supplier Engagement Policy

**Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc.**
Policy owner: Vice President, Supply Chain
Policy approver: Chief Operating Officer
Effective date: January 15, 2025
Review cycle: annually
Document number: KPM-SC-004

---

## 1. Purpose

1.1 Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc. ("Keystone") buys raw material, forgings, castings, bar stock, outside processing, tooling, gauges, packaging and services from roughly 480 active suppliers. A large share of what we ship is therefore made, treated or inspected in part by someone else. The quality, cost and delivery performance of that supply base is a direct driver of our results, and the way we share technical information with suppliers is a direct driver of how well our own know-how is protected.

1.2 This policy sets the rules for how Keystone finds, qualifies, onboards, manages and, when necessary, exits suppliers. It sets out what Keystone requires when it shares its technical information with a supplier, and it defines the roles and approval levels that apply to supplier decisions.

1.3 The objectives are to secure a reliable supply of conforming material and services at a competitive total cost; to reduce risk from single-source, financial, geopolitical, cybersecurity and compliance exposures; to protect Keystone's designs, processes and tooling when they are shared; and to treat suppliers fairly and consistently.

## 2. Scope and Definitions

2.1 **Scope.** This policy applies to all purchases of goods and services made on behalf of Keystone by any employee, at all facilities. It covers direct suppliers of production material and processing, indirect suppliers of equipment, maintenance, repair and operating supplies and professional services, and logistics providers. It does not apply to purchases governed by the Capital Expenditure Policy except where noted, or to distribution partners, who are covered by the Partner and Distributor Policy.

2.2 **Definitions.**

- **Supplier:** an organization that sells goods or services to Keystone, or that is being considered to do so.
- **Critical supplier:** a supplier whose failure to deliver would stop or seriously delay production for a Keystone customer, or that performs a special process requiring customer or industry accreditation.
- **Special process:** a manufacturing process whose output cannot be fully verified by later inspection, for example heat treatment, plating, anodizing, welding, non-destructive testing and chemical processing.
- **Approved Supplier List (ASL):** the controlled list in the enterprise resource planning system of suppliers who have been qualified for specific commodity codes.
- **Deal value band:** the classification of a deal as low, medium or high value under the Legal Review and Approval Policy.
- **Technical package:** the drawings, models, specifications, process sheets, tooling definitions and other technical information that Keystone gives a supplier so that the supplier can quote or produce a part.

## 3. Sharing Keystone Information with Suppliers

3.1 **Getting our materials back.** When the relationship with a supplier ends, Keystone's technical package, tooling data and any physical samples must not stay in the supplier's hands. Every nondisclosure agreement with a supplier must oblige the supplier to give back or destroy the information we have provided once the agreement is over.

3.2 **How fast materials must come back.** The return or destruction has to be completed within 30 days of the end of the agreement or of our written request, whichever comes first. A longer period can be allowed only if the Vice President, Supply Chain approves it in writing. The Engineering Data Handling Policy sets a floor under this period so that the supplier has a realistic chance to locate everything.

3.3 **What we send and when.** Sourcing teams release technical packages in stages, not all at once. At the request-for-quote stage, buyers send only the drawing, material specification and quantities needed to price the part. The full technical package, including process sheets, fixture concepts and inspection methods, is released only after the supplier has been shortlisted and the buyer has confirmed in the sourcing system that the supplier is on the ASL or has a qualification in progress. Tier 1 material under the Trade Secret Protection Policy is released to a supplier only with the written approval of the information owner and is logged in the Trade Secret Register.

3.4 **Hiring each other's people.** Keystone recruits machinists, programmers and quality technicians from the same regional labor markets as many of its suppliers, and some of our customers are our neighbors. We do not want to be bound to a promise that stops us from hiring someone because they once worked for a supplier or customer, and we do not accept such a promise from our counterparties in return. For that reason, a nondisclosure agreement with a supplier or a customer must not contain a clause that bars either side from approaching or hiring the other's personnel, and no agreement governed by California law, whoever the counterparty is, may contain that kind of employee-restricting clause.

3.5 **Marking and transmitting technical packages.** Buyers send technical packages only through the supplier portal or by encrypted transfer. Email attachments are acceptable for Tier 3 material only. The portal applies a watermark carrying the supplier's name and the date to every downloaded drawing, and the buyer records the portal transaction number in the supplier file.

3.6 **No leftovers for bigger deals.** On deals that fall in the medium or high value band, a supplier must not be allowed to keep any of our information once it has returned or destroyed the rest. The agreement may not carve out archival copies, residual knowledge or retained working files for these deals. If a supplier insists on an exception, for instance because its own quality system requires a record to be retained, the Chief Technology Officer must approve the exception in writing before the agreement is signed.

3.7 **Supplier sub-tier use.** A supplier that wants to pass part of the work to a sub-tier supplier must obtain the written consent of the Keystone buyer before releasing any Keystone technical package to the sub-tier. The buyer consults the quality department for special processes and informs the Intellectual Property Counsel if Tier 1 material is involved.

## 4. Supplier Selection and Qualification

4.1 **Sourcing strategy.** Category managers prepare a sourcing strategy for each commodity group every year. The strategy identifies spend, number of qualified sources, risk factors, target savings and planned supplier development. Sole-source relationships must be justified in writing and reviewed annually.

4.2 **Competitive bidding.** Purchases above $50,000 per year in a commodity require at least three written quotations unless the buyer documents a sole-source justification approved by the category manager. Purchases above $500,000 per year require the approval of the Vice President, Supply Chain for the selection decision. Purchases above $2,000,000 per year require the Chief Operating Officer.

4.3 **Qualification.** Before a supplier is added to the ASL for a commodity, supplier quality engineering completes a qualification. The depth of qualification depends on risk. A low-risk supplier of standard catalog items needs only a questionnaire and a financial screening. A supplier of production parts requires an on-site audit against the Company's supplier quality manual and ISO 9001 or equivalent certification. A special-process supplier additionally requires the accreditation demanded by our customers, such as Nadcap for heat treatment, welding or non-destructive testing, and a first-article inspection report.

4.4 **Financial and compliance screening.** Procurement checks every new supplier against restricted-party lists, performs a financial health review using a commercial rating service and confirms that the supplier is not debarred from federal contracting where a customer requirement applies. Suppliers located outside the United States are additionally screened under the Export Control Policy and the Anti-Bribery Policy.

4.5 **Conflict minerals and supply chain due diligence.** Buyers must collect conflict minerals declarations from suppliers of materials that may contain tantalum, tin, tungsten or gold. Suppliers are expected to cooperate with our customers' due diligence requests and to flow down these requirements to their own sub-tier suppliers.

## 5. Onboarding and Risk Tiering

5.1 After qualification the buyer sets up the supplier in the enterprise resource planning system, assigns commodity codes and enters payment terms. Bank details are verified by Accounts Payable using a call-back to a known contact, never solely from an email request.

5.2 Each supplier is assigned a risk tier. Critical suppliers are Tier A and are reviewed quarterly. Suppliers of production material or processing that are not critical are Tier B and are reviewed twice a year. All other suppliers are Tier C and are reviewed annually or when performance triggers an alert.

5.3 For Tier A suppliers the sourcing team prepares a continuity plan that covers alternate sources, safety stock, tooling ownership and recovery time targets. Keystone-owned tooling at a supplier is tagged, listed in the asset register and verified by physical inspection every year.

5.4 Suppliers must provide the insurance coverage described in the supplier manual, including general liability of at least $2,000,000 per occurrence, product liability for suppliers of finished components, and cyber liability of at least $5,000,000 for suppliers that connect to Keystone systems or handle Keystone technical data electronically.

5.5 Suppliers that connect to Keystone systems or exchange technical data electronically complete the information security questionnaire and, for Tier A suppliers, accept an annual security review by Keystone Information Security.

## 6. Performance Management

6.1 **Scorecards.** Supplier quality engineering and the buyer issue a scorecard each quarter that measures on-time delivery, quality (parts per million defective), responsiveness, cost performance and corrective action closure. Targets are 95 percent on-time delivery and fewer than 500 parts per million defective for production suppliers.

6.2 **Corrective action.** A nonconformance triggers a supplier corrective action request. The supplier must acknowledge within two business days, contain the problem within five business days and provide a root cause and permanent corrective action within thirty days. Repeated or serious nonconformances move the supplier to probation, which restricts new business awards until performance recovers.

6.3 **Development.** Where a strategic supplier shows potential, Keystone may invest in supplier development, including kaizen events, shared training and joint cost-reduction projects. The Vice President, Supply Chain approves development investments above $25,000.

6.4 **Disqualification and exit.** A supplier may be removed from the ASL for persistent poor performance, loss of required certification, financial distress, integrity violations or failure to meet the requirements of this policy. Before exit, the buyer, supplier quality and the Intellectual Property Counsel prepare an exit plan that covers transfer of tooling, recovery of Keystone information and open orders. The buyer confirms in writing, and files the confirmation, that the supplier has complied with the return or destruction requirement in section 3.

## 7. Ethics in Sourcing

7.1 **Conflicts of interest.** Employees must not take part in a sourcing decision if they, or a close family member, have a financial interest in a bidder or a personal relationship that could influence the decision. They must disclose such interests to their manager and the Compliance Officer under the Code of Business Conduct.

7.2 **Gifts and entertainment.** Employees may accept only modest gifts of nominal value, not exceeding $50 in a calendar year from any single supplier, and may never accept cash, gift cards or anything offered during an active bid. Meals associated with a business meeting are acceptable if reasonable and infrequent. The Anti-Bribery Policy applies to all dealings with government-owned or government-connected suppliers.

7.3 **Fair dealing.** Buyers treat all bidders equally, apply the same information to all, protect bidders' proprietary pricing from other bidders and communicate decisions promptly. Reverse auctions may be used for standard commodities with the approval of the category manager but not for special processes or critical components.

7.4 **Supplier diversity.** Keystone encourages participation by small, minority-owned, women-owned, veteran-owned and other diverse businesses and counts such spend where federal customers require reporting.

7.5 **Forced labor and human rights.** Suppliers must comply with applicable labor laws, may not use forced, prison or child labor, and must permit Keystone or its customers to verify compliance. Suspected violations are reported to the Compliance Officer.

## 8. Roles and Responsibilities

8.1 **Vice President, Supply Chain.** Owns this policy, approves supplier selections in the ranges set in section 4.2, approves exceptions to the return period in section 3.2, and reports supply risk to the Chief Operating Officer each quarter.

8.2 **Category managers.** Prepare sourcing strategies, lead competitive bids and manage supplier relationships in their commodity.

8.3 **Buyers.** Issue requests for quote and purchase orders, manage the release of technical packages according to section 3, record portal transactions and confirm return or destruction of materials at exit.

8.4 **Supplier quality engineering.** Qualifies suppliers, audits them, issues scorecards and manages corrective actions.

8.5 **Chief Technology Officer.** Approves exceptions described in section 3.6 and decides which Tier 1 material may be released to a supplier, on the advice of the information owner.

8.6 **Intellectual Property Counsel.** Advises on the protection of technical packages, reviews the exit plan for Tier A suppliers and checks the Trade Secret Register.

8.7 **Accounts Payable.** Verifies supplier bank details and performs three-way matching of orders, receipts and invoices before payment.

## 9. Exceptions

9.1 Exceptions to this policy must be requested in writing through the exception form in the sourcing system, stating the policy section, the reason, the risk and the period. Unless a section names a different approver, exceptions are approved by the Vice President, Supply Chain. Exceptions that affect quality are also reviewed by the Director of Quality.

9.2 Exceptions are recorded, reviewed quarterly and reported to the Chief Operating Officer. Approved exceptions expire automatically after twelve months unless renewed.

## 10. Enforcement

10.1 Failure to follow this policy may lead to disciplinary action. Intentional circumvention, such as splitting purchases to avoid approval thresholds, is treated as a serious violation of the Code of Business Conduct.

10.2 Internal Audit tests compliance with this policy each year, including a sample of supplier file reviews, and reports results to the Audit Committee of the Board.

## Appendix A. Scorecard Weights and Onboarding Checklist

A.1 **Scorecard weights.** The quarterly supplier scorecard combines the following weighted measures for production suppliers: quality (parts per million defective and corrective action closure), 35 percent; delivery (on-time and in-full), 30 percent; cost (price competitiveness and cost reduction contribution), 15 percent; responsiveness and communication, 10 percent; and sustainability and compliance, 10 percent. A score of 90 or above is rated preferred, 75 to 89 approved, 60 to 74 conditional and below 60 probation. Conditional suppliers receive an improvement plan, and suppliers on probation may not receive new business awards.

A.2 **Onboarding checklist.** The buyer ensures that the following are on file before the first order: completed supplier information form; tax documentation; bank verification record; qualification result; insurance certificates; quality manual acknowledgment; conflict minerals declaration where relevant; restricted-party screening result; information security questionnaire where relevant; and a signed acknowledgment of the supplier code of conduct.

A.3 **Supplier code of conduct.** Suppliers are asked to confirm in writing that they comply with the laws that apply to them; that they prohibit forced and child labor; that they provide a safe workplace; that they comply with environmental laws; that they do not offer or accept bribes; that they respect the intellectual property of others; and that they let Keystone verify compliance with reasonable notice.

A.4 **Dual sourcing.** For parts that account for more than 5 percent of a customer program's revenue, or that are made from long-lead material, the category manager considers a second source. Qualification of the second source starts with a sample order and a full first article inspection, and a trial lot must be accepted before the second source is added to the ASL for the part. The sourcing team records the split of volume between sources, which in normal conditions is no more than 80 percent to the primary source.

A.5 **Price changes and raw material surcharges.** Suppliers may request price increases only with documented justification, at least sixty days before the change, and with evidence such as index movements for raw material. The category manager reviews the request against the index and market, negotiates, and obtains the approval of the Vice President, Supply Chain for increases that exceed 3 percent on annual spend above $250,000. Surcharges for raw material are tied to published indices and reviewed quarterly.
