# Engineering Data Handling Policy

**Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc.**
Policy owner: Vice President, Engineering
Policy approver: Chief Technology Officer
Effective date: February 20, 2025
Review cycle: annually
Document number: KPM-EN-003

---

## 1. Purpose

1.1 Engineering data is the working material of Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc. ("Keystone"). It includes the CAD models and drawings that define our parts and our customers' parts, the programs that drive our numerically controlled machines, process sheets, fixture designs, inspection plans, coordinate measuring machine routines, simulation results, test data and the revision history that connects them. Errors in this data become scrap, rework, customer escapes and, in the worst cases, safety events. Loss of this data to a competitor erodes the advantage we have built over decades.

1.2 This policy sets out how engineering data is created, controlled, stored, shared with people outside Keystone, returned and disposed of. It describes the controls that apply to special categories of data, namely personal data and technical data controlled under export laws. It supplements the Trade Secret Protection Policy, which establishes the tiers of classification, and the Information Security Standard, which specifies technical safeguards.

## 2. Scope and Definitions

2.1 **Scope.** This policy applies to all Keystone employees, contractors and agents who create, modify, review, store or transmit engineering data, wherever they work. It covers engineering, manufacturing engineering, tooling, quality, program management, sales engineering and any other function that uses technical data.

2.2 **Definitions.**

- **Engineering data:** any technical information used to design, manufacture, inspect, test, install or maintain a product, including drawings, models, bills of materials, NC programs, process parameters, work instructions, test reports and software.
- **PLM:** the product lifecycle management system, which is the system of record for engineering data and its revisions.
- **Controlled document:** an engineering document that is released through the PLM change process and carries a revision level.
- **NC program:** a program that instructs a computer-numerical-control machine tool.
- **Customer-furnished data:** engineering data that originates with a customer and is provided to Keystone for production or quotation.
- **Outside party:** any person or organization outside Keystone, including suppliers, distributors, customers, consultants and regulators.
- **Personal data:** information that identifies or relates to an identifiable individual, such as names combined with contact details, employee records, biometric data, government identifiers, health information and images in which a person can be recognized.
- **Technical data:** information required for the design, development, production, repair, operation or maintenance of items controlled under the export laws of the United States.

## 3. Controlling Engineering Data and Sharing It Externally

3.1 **Principle.** Engineering data is released outside Keystone only when there is a business need, the data is at the lowest classification that satisfies the need, the recipient is authorized under the applicable Keystone policy, and the transfer is made through an approved channel. Employees must never send engineering data to a personal email account or an unapproved storage service for convenience.

3.2 **Working copies.** Engineers on the other side of a project need to work. A supplier's programmer may have to load a model into a CAM system, keep an archive copy to track revisions and make printouts for the floor. Any agreement under which we supply information to an outside party must therefore allow the recipient to make copies of portions of what we provide where that is needed for the task, subject to the other protections in the agreement.

3.3 **Approved channels.** Engineering data is transmitted outside Keystone only through the supplier or partner portal, the managed secure file transfer service or, for very large models, an encrypted drive sent by tracked courier. Email attachments may carry only Tier 3 information. Employees must not use consumer file sharing, messaging applications or social media to exchange engineering data.

3.4 **Customer-furnished data.** Data provided by a customer is treated at a classification no lower than the customer requires. The program manager records any customer-specific handling requirements, such as restrictions on offshore access or on the use of cloud services, in the project plan. Customer-furnished data may be shared with a Keystone supplier only if the customer's requirements permit it and the program manager has recorded the sharing in the project file.

3.5 **No reliance on stamps.** Keystone marks its drawings and models as set out in the Trade Secret Protection Policy, but we know that stamps get lost during conversion, screenshots and file renaming. Where Keystone is the disclosing side, the agreement we sign must protect our information whether or not it carries a legend, and must not make protection depend on whether each item was marked or identified as secret.

3.6 **Version control.** Only the released revision of a controlled document may be used for production. Engineers must not use drawings printed more than one day earlier without checking the revision in the PLM system. Obsolete documents are removed from the floor when a new revision is released, and are marked "Superseded" if retained for reference.

3.7 **Design reviews.** Design reviews that include outside participants require an agenda stating the data to be shown, the approval of the information owner for any Tier 1 material, a list of participants, and a record of who attended. Screen sharing is limited to the specific application window needed and must not display the full desktop.

## 4. Return and Disposal

4.1 **Recovering our data.** When a supplier, partner or other outside party finishes the work for which it received engineering data, the project engineer or buyer confirms in writing that the data has been returned to Keystone or destroyed by the recipient. The confirmation is filed in the PLM project record. Where a supplier says that it cannot destroy backup media immediately, the project engineer asks for a written statement of when the backups will be overwritten.

4.2 **A realistic period to give back.** Locating every copy of a data package, including archives and backups, takes time. The time allowed to the other side for returning or destroying our materials must be at least 10 days, so that the recipient can find and process all of it. The Supplier Engagement Policy sets the upper limit of that period, and exceptions to the upper limit are decided there.

4.3 **Disposal of data inside Keystone.** Electronic engineering data that is no longer needed and is not subject to a legal hold or retention requirement is removed from workstations and shared drives by the information owner. Media that held Tier 1 or Tier 2 data are wiped using the method specified in the Information Security Standard or physically destroyed. A certificate of destruction is obtained from the shredding vendor for hard drives.

4.4 **Departing employees.** On the last day of employment, the engineering manager reviews with the departing engineer the location of all working files and confirms that they are in the PLM or other approved repository. Local copies on laptops are removed by IT after the files are verified.

## 5. Special Categories of Data

5.1 **Personal data.** Engineering work rarely requires personal data, but some projects do: ergonomic studies with photographs of operators, wearable device trials, access logs and time studies, or medical device projects with clinical contacts. When personal data is to be exchanged with an outside party, the agreement must require the recipient to use the personal data solely for the purpose stated and to protect it with appropriate security safeguards. Project engineers must also minimize the personal data collected and obtain Human Resources or Legal review before transferring employee data.

5.2 **Longer protection for personal data.** Personal data does not lose its sensitivity quickly. If personal data is going to be shared, the period during which the recipient must keep information secret must run for at least 84 months. The Legal Review and Approval Policy sets the maximum permitted period and the approval needed for a longer one.

5.3 **Controlled unclassified information.** Customers in the defense and aerospace sectors may provide technical data marked as controlled unclassified information. Such data is stored only in the segregated enclave maintained by Information Security, may be accessed only by US persons designated by the program manager, and must be handled according to the customer contract and the applicable federal requirements. Incidents involving controlled unclassified information must be reported to Information Security within one hour of discovery because of short regulatory reporting deadlines.

5.4 **Export-controlled technical data.** Technical data that relates to items on the United States Munitions List or the Commerce Control List requires an export license or other authorization before it is released to certain foreign persons or destinations. When the recipient of our information is located outside the United States, or when the governing law of the agreement is not that of a US state, the agreement must contain an undertaking by the recipient to comply with applicable export control laws and not to export or re-export our technical data without the required authorization. The Export Control Policy sets out classification, licensing and screening procedures.

5.5 **Cloud and offshore access.** Use of cloud services to hold engineering data requires approval of Information Security. Engineering data classified as Tier 1 may not be stored on cloud services located outside the United States. Offshore access by Keystone employees to export-controlled technical data is permitted only if authorized by the Export Compliance Manager.

## 6. Controls in the PLM System

6.1 **Access.** PLM access is role-based. Engineers have edit access within the product families they support and read access elsewhere. Supplier quality engineers and buyers have read access to released documents. Access to the engineering vault where Tier 1 documents are held is limited to people on the Trade Secret Register.

6.2 **Change control.** Changes to released documents follow the engineering change process, which requires an engineering change request, an impact assessment by manufacturing, quality and sourcing, approval by the change board and release in the PLM. Emergency changes may be approved by the Vice President, Engineering and are reviewed by the change board at its next meeting.

6.3 **Audit trail.** The PLM records who viewed, changed, downloaded and printed each document. Information Security reviews unusual download activity monthly, including large downloads by employees who have given notice of resignation.

6.4 **Review of access.** Managers review the access of their staff every six months. Access that is not reviewed within thirty days of the request from Information Security is suspended.

## 7. NC Programs and Shop Floor Systems

7.1 NC programs are stored in the central program repository and are transferred to machines through the direct numerical control network. Programs may not be kept on removable media on the shop floor except media issued by IT and recorded in the asset log.

7.2 Programmers must record the revision of the drawing and the process sheet in the header of each program. Changes to proven programs, including feed and speed changes made at the machine, must be reported to the programmer and approved before they are applied in production.

7.3 Machine controllers are placed on a segmented network without direct internet access. Third-party maintenance technicians connecting laptops or diagnostic tools to controllers must be escorted and use equipment scanned by IT.

7.4 First-article programs and fixtures developed for a customer are considered the property of the party identified in the purchase agreement. Where Keystone owns them, they are protected under the Trade Secret Protection Policy.

## 8. Backup and Recovery

8.1 The PLM and the program repository are backed up nightly with weekly full backups held off site. Backup media are encrypted. Restoration tests are performed every quarter, and Information Security reports results to the Chief Technology Officer.

8.2 Engineering data stored on local drives is not backed up by IT and should be moved to the PLM or approved shared repositories promptly.

8.3 Recovery time objectives are four hours for the PLM and eight hours for the program repository. The business continuity plan describes alternate working arrangements while systems are restored.

## 9. Roles and Responsibilities

9.1 **Vice President, Engineering.** Owns this policy, approves emergency changes and ensures that engineering staff are trained on it.

9.2 **Chief Technology Officer.** Approves this policy, decides on exceptions that affect Tier 1 data and receives reports on data incidents.

9.3 **Information owners and project engineers.** Classify data, select the lowest classification that meets the need, release data to outside parties through approved channels and confirm return or destruction.

9.4 **Information Security.** Operates the PLM security controls, the enclave for controlled unclassified information and the monitoring program, and investigates incidents.

9.5 **Export Compliance Manager.** Classifies technical data, issues licenses, screens recipients and trains engineering staff on export controls.

9.6 **Program managers.** Record customer handling requirements and make sure that the project team knows them.

9.7 **Human Resources and Legal.** Review transfers of personal data and advise on privacy requirements.

## 10. Exceptions

10.1 Requests for exceptions are submitted in writing to the Vice President, Engineering, who forwards to the Chief Technology Officer any request affecting Tier 1 data. Exceptions are for a defined period and recorded in the PLM project record.

10.2 Exceptions that would breach export control laws or customer requirements are not permitted.

## 11. Enforcement

11.1 Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment. Deliberate removal of engineering data for personal or third-party benefit may also result in civil or criminal action.

11.2 Information Security and Internal Audit test compliance annually and report to the Audit Committee.
