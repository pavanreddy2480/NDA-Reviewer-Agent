# Partner and Distributor Policy

**Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc.**
Policy owner: Vice President, Sales and Business Development
Policy approver: Chief Executive Officer
Effective date: February 10, 2025
Review cycle: annually
Document number: KPM-SL-002

---

## 1. Purpose

1.1 Keystone Precision Manufacturing, Inc. ("Keystone") reaches many of its customers through a network of independent distributors, sales representatives, integrators and value-added resellers, and it develops some products jointly with technology and manufacturing partners. These partners extend our reach into regions and industries that we could not serve efficiently on our own. They also carry our brand, handle our technical information, speak to our customers and, in some cases, hold stock on our behalf.

1.2 This policy sets out how Keystone chooses, appoints, supports, monitors and ends relationships with distribution and development partners. It establishes standards for how we share technical and commercial information with partners, and it assigns the approvals required at each stage.

1.3 The aims are to grow profitable sales through reputable partners; to make sure partners represent Keystone honestly and lawfully; to protect Keystone's brand, designs, price lists and customer relationships; and to ensure that Keystone's rights and obligations are clearly allocated.

## 2. Scope and Definitions

2.1 **Scope.** This policy applies to every Keystone employee who proposes, negotiates, manages or terminates a relationship with a partner. It applies to relationships worldwide. Relationships with suppliers are governed by the Supplier Engagement Policy and relationships with end customers by the order-to-cash procedures of the Sales Operations Manual.

2.2 **Definitions.**

- **Partner:** any organization that distributes, resells, represents, integrates, co-develops, co-markets or co-manufactures Keystone products. The term includes distributors, sales agents, manufacturers' representatives, system integrators, joint development partners and contract manufacturers that produce to Keystone designs.
- **Distributor:** a partner that buys Keystone products for resale in a defined territory or market segment.
- **Sales representative:** a partner that solicits orders for Keystone for a commission but does not take title to goods.
- **Joint development partner:** a partner with whom Keystone collaborates on the design of a new product, process or material.
- **Strategic partner:** a partner whose relationship falls in the high deal value band under the Legal Review and Approval Policy.
- **Territory:** the geographic area or industry segment for which a partner has been given sales rights.
- **Restricted party:** a person or entity appearing on a government list of persons with whom business is prohibited or restricted.

## 3. Sharing Information with Partners

3.1 **Principle.** Partners receive only the information they need to perform their role. Distributors typically need catalog data, application notes, price lists and training material. Joint development partners need more detailed technical data, but only for the project. No partner receives Tier 1 material under the Trade Secret Protection Policy without the written approval of the Chief Technology Officer, who may attach conditions.

3.2 **Government and court demands.** Partners operate in many jurisdictions and are sometimes served with subpoenas, regulatory orders or demands from public authorities that could force them to hand over what we have given them. Keystone wants to learn of such a demand in time to respond, for instance by seeking a protective order. Every nondisclosure agreement with a partner, and with any other organization, must therefore require the recipient to inform Keystone when a statute, regulation or legal process compels it to disclose our information.

3.3 **Partner portal.** Distributors access product data, price lists and order status through the partner portal. Portal accounts are individual, are provisioned by Sales Operations after the partner contract has been signed, and are reviewed every quarter. Distributors must remove the access of departing staff within two business days. Price lists and cost-plus formulas are published only for the distributor's own territory.

3.4 **Commercial information counts too.** What a distributor or a customer learns from us goes beyond engineering. It includes our pricing, discount structures, customer lists, forecasts, capacity, lead times and plans for new products. A nondisclosure agreement with a partner or a customer therefore must not define protected information so narrowly that it covers only technical matter. We will not accept wording under which business and commercial information is left outside the protection.

3.5 **Marketing materials.** Partners may use only the marketing materials, logos and product descriptions approved by Corporate Communications. Changes to approved materials require prior written approval. Partners may not issue press releases that name Keystone without approval from the Vice President, Sales and Business Development and Corporate Communications.

## 4. Partner Relationships

4.1 **Keeping the relationship out of the news.** Competitors watch our distribution network, and an early announcement about a new alliance or a joint project can reveal our plans. The nondisclosure agreement with a partner must require the partner not to disclose that the agreement was concluded or that the parties negotiated it, except as Keystone has agreed in writing.

4.2 **Territory and exclusivity.** Territories are defined in the partner contract by country, region or industry segment. Exclusive territories are granted only with the approval of the Vice President, Sales and Business Development, for a limited period and with minimum purchase or performance commitments. Exclusivity of more than three years requires the approval of the Chief Executive Officer.

4.3 **Strategic partners and similar developments.** Where the relationship is in the high deal value band, typically a joint development or a long-term manufacturing arrangement, the nondisclosure agreement must not contain wording that lets the partner independently create information resembling what we disclose. This restriction concerns high value partner relationships only; distributors and sales representatives in the low and medium bands are governed by the standard form approved by the Legal Department.

4.4 **Joint development projects.** Before a joint development begins, the project sponsor completes a project charter that identifies the objective, the contributions of each side, who owns improvements, how background and foreground technology are handled, and who bears the costs. The Intellectual Property Counsel and the Chief Technology Officer review the charter. No design work or exchange of technical data begins until the Legal Department confirms that the governing documents are in place.

4.5 **Pricing and antitrust.** Distributors set their own resale prices. Keystone employees may suggest resale prices only where counsel has confirmed that it is lawful, and they must never agree with a distributor, or with competing distributors, on resale prices, territories or customers beyond what the approved contract provides. Employees must not discuss pricing, bids, markets or capacity with competitors, including at trade association meetings.

4.6 **Partner conduct.** Partners must comply with the Keystone Partner Code of Conduct, which covers anti-bribery, export control, sanctions, data privacy, fair competition, labor and human rights and environmental standards. Partners must report any known or suspected violation to Keystone promptly.

## 5. Selection and Appointment

5.1 **Business case.** A Keystone employee who proposes a new partner submits a business case that describes the market opportunity, the partner's capabilities and track record, the expected volumes, the proposed territory, and the cost and risk. Business cases for distributors expected to exceed $1,000,000 in annual sales require approval by the Vice President, Sales and Business Development. Business cases for relationships expected to exceed $5,000,000 require approval by the Chief Executive Officer.

5.2 **Due diligence.** Before appointment, Compliance performs due diligence proportional to risk. Every partner is screened against restricted-party lists. For partners that will interact with government officials, or that operate in countries with elevated corruption risk, due diligence also includes ownership checks, reference checks, an adverse-media search and a review of the partner's own compliance program. The results are recorded in the partner file.

5.3 **Contract.** Keystone does not ship product or share technical data with a partner before the partner contract is signed under the authority matrix of the Legal Review and Approval Policy. Standard distributor contracts use the template approved by the Legal Department. Departures from the template are approved as described in that policy.

5.4 **Onboarding.** Sales Operations provisions portal access, product training and credit limits. The partner's staff complete an onboarding module covering product knowledge, Keystone's quality expectations, the Partner Code of Conduct and basic export control and anti-bribery requirements.

5.5 **Credit.** Credit limits for distributors are set by Finance based on financial statements, payment history and trade references. Limits above $250,000 require the approval of the Chief Financial Officer. Distributors must provide updated financial statements annually.

## 6. Monitoring and Performance

6.1 **Business reviews.** Each distributor with annual sales above $500,000 is reviewed at least twice a year against sales targets, inventory levels, customer satisfaction, quality complaints and compliance. The regional sales director leads the review and documents actions.

6.2 **Inventory and returns.** Distributors report stock levels monthly. Stock rotation and return rights are limited to the amounts and periods specified in the contract. Returns of damaged or non-conforming goods are processed through the quality department with a return authorization.

6.3 **Audits.** Keystone may audit a partner's compliance with the contract and this policy, including its books and records relating to Keystone products, with reasonable notice. Internal Audit and Compliance may perform unannounced reviews of partners in high-risk jurisdictions.

6.4 **Red flags.** Employees must report to Compliance indications of improper payments, requests for unusual commissions, payments to third countries or unrelated accounts, requests to hide the identity of the customer, and refusals to provide required documents or certifications. A partner that triggers a red flag is suspended from new orders until Compliance completes its review.

## 7. Termination and Transition

7.1 Before a partner relationship is ended, the regional sales director and the Legal Department prepare a transition plan covering open orders, stock buy-back, customer communications, warranty support, the removal of portal access and recovery of Keystone materials.

7.2 Notice of termination is given in accordance with the contract and applicable law. In some states and countries, distributor relationships are protected by statute, and the Legal Department must be consulted before notice is given.

7.3 On termination the partner must stop using Keystone trademarks and marketing materials, must stop representing itself as a Keystone partner, and must return or destroy materials belonging to Keystone. Sales Operations disables portal access on the termination date and confirms the removal in the partner file.

## 8. Roles and Responsibilities

8.1 **Vice President, Sales and Business Development.** Owns this policy, approves new partner relationships within the thresholds above, approves exclusive territories and reports on the partner network to the Chief Executive Officer each quarter.

8.2 **Regional sales directors.** Manage partner performance, lead business reviews, escalate red flags and ensure partners follow Keystone policies.

8.3 **Sales Operations.** Maintains the partner database, administers the portal, processes credit applications and archives contract documents.

8.4 **Compliance Officer.** Performs due diligence, trains partners and investigates red flags.

8.5 **Legal Department.** Maintains the standard partner contract templates, advises on territory and antitrust questions and reviews all non-standard terms.

8.6 **Chief Technology Officer.** Approves releases of Tier 1 material to partners and the technical elements of joint development projects, and decides on the exceptions mentioned in section 4.3.

8.7 **Chief Financial Officer.** Approves credit limits above the threshold and reviews partner payment performance.

## 9. Exceptions

9.1 Requests for exceptions to this policy are made in writing to the Vice President, Sales and Business Development, who consults Compliance and the Legal Department. Exceptions affecting technical information also require the Chief Technology Officer.

9.2 No exception may be granted that would breach law, the Anti-Bribery Policy or the Export Control Policy.

9.3 Exceptions are time-limited, recorded in the partner file and reviewed at the next business review.

## 10. Enforcement

10.1 Employees who do not follow this policy may face disciplinary action. Partners who breach the Partner Code of Conduct may be suspended or terminated, and Keystone may seek remedies available under the contract and law.

10.2 Internal Audit reviews a sample of partner files each year and reports its findings to the Audit Committee.

## Appendix A. Partner Onboarding Checklist and Reporting Calendar

A.1 **Onboarding checklist.** The regional sales director confirms that the following are complete before a distributor receives its first order: business case approved at the right level; due diligence report on file; restricted-party screening cleared; contract signed and filed; credit limit approved; portal accounts created for named users; onboarding training completed by at least two members of staff; territory map and customer list agreed; marketing materials provided; partner code of conduct acknowledged; and the first-ninety-days plan agreed.

A.2 **Reporting calendar.** Distributors submit monthly sales and inventory reports by the fifth business day of the following month. Quarterly, they provide a pipeline forecast and a summary of customer visits. Annually, they provide financial statements, a compliance certification and an update of their ownership and key personnel. Keystone reviews the reports in the monthly sales operations meeting and uses them for forecasting and capacity planning.

A.3 **Distributor incentives.** Keystone offers distributors volume rebates, tiered by calendar-year purchases, and a separate marketing development fund, up to 1.5 percent of purchases, for approved joint marketing activities. Funds are paid on proof of performance, and unused funds expire at the end of the year. Rebates and funds are approved by the regional sales director up to $50,000 and by the Vice President, Sales and Business Development above that amount.

A.4 **Warranty and returns.** Distributors pass on the standard Keystone warranty to customers and do not offer broader warranties without written approval. Warranty claims are submitted through the portal with the part number, lot, date code and a description of the problem, and are evaluated by the quality department. A return authorization is issued before any goods are returned, and goods returned without one will not be accepted.

A.5 **Trade shows and events.** When a partner exhibits Keystone products at a trade show, the partner may display only approved materials, may demonstrate only released products and must refer technical questions from visitors who are not existing customers to Keystone. Keystone staff who attend the event with a partner follow the visitor and communication rules of the Trade Secret Protection Policy.
