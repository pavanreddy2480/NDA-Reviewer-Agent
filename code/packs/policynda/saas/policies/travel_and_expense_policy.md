# Travel and Expense Policy

**Company:** Northwind Cloud, Inc.
**Document owner:** Finance Operations, reporting to the Chief Financial Officer
**Policy number:** FIN-003
**Effective date:** January 2, 2025
**Applies to:** All employees, officers and contractors who incur business expenses on Northwind's behalf

---

## 1. Purpose

Northwind Cloud, Inc. ("Northwind") expects its people to travel and spend money when it helps customers, builds the business or develops the team, and expects them to do so sensibly. This Policy explains what Northwind will pay for, how to book travel, how to claim reimbursement, and how spending is reviewed. It is designed to be fair, easy to follow and consistent with the tax rules that apply to the company.

## 2. Scope

The Policy applies to all business travel, meals, lodging, entertainment, transport, conference and training costs, and other work-related expenses paid for by the worker, charged to a company card or invoiced to Northwind. It applies to domestic and international travel.

## 3. Definitions

- **Traveler** means anyone traveling on Northwind business.
- **Approver** means the Traveler's manager or other person authorized to approve expenses, who must be at least one level above the Traveler.
- **Corporate card** means a card issued by Northwind's bank in the worker's name for business spending.
- **Travel Management Company (TMC)** means the agency appointed by Northwind to book air, rail, hotel and car services through the online booking tool.
- **Expense system** means the cloud application where expense reports are created, approved and reimbursed.
- **Per diem** means a fixed daily allowance for meals and incidentals when traveling.

## 4. Principles

1. Spend company money as carefully as you would spend your own.
2. Choose the lowest reasonable fare or price that meets the business need.
3. Keep original, itemized receipts.
4. Never mix personal and business expenses on the same report.
5. Honesty is non-negotiable. Falsifying an expense is grounds for termination.

## 5. Approvals

### 5.1 Pre-approval of travel

Travel must be approved in advance by the Approver. The following additionally require approval from a department head:

- international travel;
- trips exceeding five days;
- trips for which the total estimated cost exceeds $5,000; and
- travel by more than three people from the same team on the same trip.

### 5.2 Approval of expense reports

Expense reports are approved by the Approver. Reports exceeding $10,000 also need approval from the Chief Financial Officer or a delegate. Approvers may not approve their own expenses, or those of anyone who is more senior.

## 6. Booking Travel

### 6.1 Use the booking tool

Workers must book air, hotel, rail and car rental through the TMC's online booking tool, which applies company rates and policy rules and ensures that Northwind can locate travelers in an emergency. Bookings made outside the tool will be reimbursed only if the Traveler explains why in the expense report.

### 6.2 Advance booking

Flights should be booked at least fourteen days before departure where possible. Fares booked later than seven days before departure require an explanation.

### 6.3 Air travel

- Economy class is used for flights of less than six hours.
- Premium economy is allowed for flights of six hours or more.
- Business class is allowed only for flights of more than ten hours with the written approval of the Chief Financial Officer, or for executives of the level of Vice President and above on flights of more than eight hours.
- Travelers should choose the lowest logical fare within two hours of the preferred departure or arrival time.
- Seat upgrades, early boarding fees and lounge memberships are personal expenses unless approved for medical reasons.

### 6.4 Lodging

Travelers should use hotels offered by the booking tool, which have negotiated rates. The nightly room rate cap, including taxes, is $275 in most U.S. cities and $375 in New York, San Francisco, Boston, Washington, D.C., London, Paris and Tokyo. Travelers who stay with friends or family may not claim lodging allowances.

### 6.5 Ground transport

Travelers should choose the least expensive reasonable transport: public transport or shuttle where practical, then a ride-hailing service, then a taxi. Rental cars must be of a compact or midsize class and booked through the TMC. Travelers should decline the rental company's optional insurance because Northwind's corporate policy covers them. Mileage for the use of a personal car is reimbursed at the rate set by the Internal Revenue Service.

### 6.6 Rail

Travelers on the Northeast corridor may use rail instead of air if the cost and travel time are comparable. Coach or business class (not first class) is allowed.

## 7. Meals and Entertainment

### 7.1 Meals while traveling

Travelers receive reimbursement for actual, reasonable meal costs up to the daily limit of $90 in the United States ($120 in the high-cost cities listed in Section 6.4) and $100 elsewhere abroad. Alcohol is reimbursed only in moderation as part of a business meal with a customer or prospect and must not exceed $30 per person.

### 7.2 Meals in the home office area

Meals within fifty miles of a Traveler's regular work location are not reimbursable, except for team events approved by a department head and meals with customers or prospects that serve a documented business purpose.

### 7.3 Customer entertainment

Entertainment of customers and prospects must have a clear business purpose, be modest, and be consistent with the Code of Business Conduct. The expense report must list the names, employers and titles of the attendees, and the business purpose. Entertainment for government officials requires advance approval of the General Counsel.

### 7.4 Team events

Team meals and events are limited to $75 per person and must be pre-approved by the department head. Company-wide events are budgeted by the People team.

## 8. Other Expenses

### 8.1 Conferences and training

Registration fees for conferences, courses and certifications must be pre-approved by the manager and booked in the learning system where possible. Workers who attend sponsored events should share what they have learned with their teams.

### 8.2 Communications

Employees who are required to use a personal mobile phone for work receive a monthly stipend of $60 instead of reimbursement of actual charges. Overseas data charges are reimbursed with a receipt if the worker used the company's recommended roaming plan.

### 8.3 Home office and equipment

Remote workers receive a one-time home office allowance of $500 and a monthly internet stipend of $50. Equipment such as laptops and monitors is ordered through IT. Furniture and peripherals purchased by workers are reimbursed only up to the allowance.

### 8.4 Gifts

Gifts to customers, vendors and employees are limited to $100 per person per year and must be recorded in the gift register. Cash and gift cards must not be given to customers, vendors or officials. The Anti-Corruption guidance in the Code of Business Conduct applies.

### 8.5 Non-reimbursable expenses

Northwind does not reimburse: fines and penalties; personal grooming; childcare; spouse or family travel; in-room movies and minibar; personal entertainment; lost or stolen personal property; traffic or parking violations; and tips beyond 20 percent.

## 9. Corporate Cards

### 9.1 Issue and use

Corporate cards are issued to workers who travel or make purchases regularly. The card is for business use only. Workers must not use it for personal expenses, even if they intend to repay the company.

### 9.2 Reconciliation

Charges must be matched to receipts and submitted in the expense system within thirty days of the statement date. Charges not submitted within sixty days are treated as taxable income to the worker and may be deducted from pay as the law permits. Cards of workers who are repeatedly late may be suspended.

### 9.3 Lost or stolen cards

A lost or stolen card must be reported to the card issuer and to Finance Operations immediately.

## 10. Expense Reports

### 10.1 Timing

Reports should be submitted within thirty days of the end of the trip or the date of the expense. Reports submitted more than ninety days after the expense will not be reimbursed unless the CFO approves.

### 10.2 Content

Each expense item needs a date, merchant, amount, currency, business purpose and category. Receipts are required for any single item of $25 or more, and for all lodging, regardless of amount. Where a receipt is lost, the worker completes a missing receipt declaration, which is allowed no more than three times a year.

### 10.3 Foreign currency

Expenses in foreign currencies are converted at the rate in the expense system on the date of the transaction. Workers should not exchange large amounts of cash.

### 10.4 Reimbursement

Approved reports are reimbursed to the worker's bank account by direct deposit, generally within seven business days of final approval.

## 11. Safety and Wellbeing

Travelers must carry the TMC's emergency number and follow safety advice issued by Northwind. Travelers should tell their managers of their itinerary, avoid unnecessary risks and report any incident immediately. Northwind's travel insurance covers medical emergencies and evacuation, and Travelers are encouraged to review the policy summary before they go.

## 12. Sustainability

Northwind encourages workers to reduce travel emissions by preferring virtual meetings where appropriate, choosing rail for short routes, flying direct and booking hotels with environmental certification. The booking tool shows an estimate of carbon emissions for each flight option.

## 13. Roles and Responsibilities

- **Chief Financial Officer** owns the Policy and approves exceptions.
- **Finance Operations** manages the expense system, the corporate card program and audits.
- **Approvers** check that expenses are legitimate, necessary and within policy before approving them.
- **Department heads** pre-approve travel and set departmental budgets.
- **Travelers** follow the Policy, keep receipts and submit reports on time.
- **Internal Audit** samples reports each quarter.

## 14. Audit and Enforcement

Finance Operations performs random audits and automated checks for duplicates, split transactions, weekend spending and policy breaches. Workers must cooperate with audits. Fraud, falsification or repeated violations may lead to discipline, up to termination, repayment of the amounts involved and referral to law enforcement.

## 15. Exceptions

An exception to this Policy requires written approval from the Chief Financial Officer before the expense is incurred, except in emergencies, when approval must be sought within five business days.

## Appendix A. Worked Examples

**Example 1: Customer visit.** A solutions engineer flies from New York to Chicago for a two-day customer workshop. She books economy on the booking tool, stays at a hotel with a nightly rate of $240, claims meals up to the daily limit and takes a ride-hailing service from the airport. Her manager pre-approved the trip. Total cost is under $2,500, so no additional approval is needed.

**Example 2: Overseas conference.** A product director wants to attend a four-day conference in Lisbon. International travel needs approval from the department head, who also confirms the registration fee. The flight is nine hours, so premium economy is allowed. The hotel cap for Lisbon is the general international cap of $275.

**Example 3: Team offsite.** A manager organizes a one-day offsite for ten people. The department head approves a budget of $750 for meals ($75 per person). The manager pays with a corporate card, lists attendees on the report and attaches the itemized receipt.

**Example 4: Missing receipt.** A traveler loses a $42 taxi receipt. She completes a missing receipt declaration, with the date, amount, route and business purpose. She has used this twice this year, so the declaration is accepted.

**Example 5: Personal extension.** An employee extends a business trip with two personal days. The company pays only what the business trip would have cost; the employee pays for additional hotel nights and any higher airfare caused by the extension.

## Appendix B. Mileage and Per Diem Rates

Mileage for the use of a personal vehicle on business travel is reimbursed at the rate published each year by the Internal Revenue Service. Finance Operations updates the rate in the expense system each January. For locations outside the United States, the daily meal allowance is based on the lower of the actual cost and the rate published for that city by the U.S. Department of State, unless the Chief Financial Officer approves a higher rate for a particular trip. Workers who travel frequently should keep a travel log to support mileage claims.

## Appendix C. Tax Considerations

Certain expenses are taxable to the worker if they do not meet the requirements of the tax rules: for example, undocumented expenses, personal use of a corporate card, and reimbursements for commuting. Finance Operations reports such amounts through payroll. Workers should keep receipts for seven years, in line with the records schedule. International workers should ask Finance Operations about local tax rules, which may differ from U.S. rules. Value-added tax on expenses incurred abroad may be reclaimed by the company where receipts include the required details, so workers should ask for formal invoices from hotels and restaurants outside the United States.

## Appendix D. Frequently Asked Questions

**May I claim breakfast if it is included in my hotel rate?** No. Deduct included meals from your daily limit.

**May I buy a train ticket for my partner?** No. Spouse and family travel is not reimbursable.

**Can I tip more than 20 percent?** Tips above 20 percent of the pre-tax bill are not reimbursed. In countries where tipping is not customary, small tips for exceptional service are acceptable.

**What if my flight is cancelled and I need an extra night?** Call the TMC's emergency line. Reasonable additional hotel and meal costs are reimbursed with receipts.

**Who approves my manager's expenses?** The next person up the reporting line. If your manager reports to the CFO, the CFO's delegate in Finance approves.

**Can I use my own frequent flyer points?** Yes. Workers may keep the points earned on business travel, and the company does not claim them.
