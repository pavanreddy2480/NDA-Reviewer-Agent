# Contract Approval Matrix

**Company:** Northwind Cloud, Inc.
**Document owner:** Office of the General Counsel, with the Chief Financial Officer
**Policy number:** LEG-007
**Effective date:** February 3, 2025
**Applies to:** All Northwind workers who negotiate, review, approve or sign agreements on the company's behalf

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Only a small number of people at Northwind Cloud, Inc. ("Northwind") may bind the company. This Matrix explains who they are, what each of them may sign, when additional approvals are required, and how the Legal Department fits into the process. It is intended to keep sales moving, to ensure that the right executives see the commitments that matter, and to give auditors a clear record of who authorized what.

The Matrix covers every written agreement under which Northwind promises to pay money, provide services, license technology, share information or accept obligations to third parties. This includes customer subscription agreements, order forms, reseller and referral agreements, vendor contracts, statements of work, amendments, renewals, side letters and click-through terms accepted on the company's behalf. It does not cover employment offer letters, which are governed by the Human Resources delegation schedule.

## 2. Definitions

- **Authorized Signatory** means an officer or other employee named in Schedule A who may sign on the company's behalf.
- **Contract Value** means the total committed spend or revenue over the initial term, including any minimum commitments but excluding optional renewals.
- **Value Band** means the classification of a deal by Contract Value: low (under $50,000), medium ($50,000 to $500,000) and high (above $500,000). Deals that give a customer or partner access to Restricted information, or that the Chief Revenue Officer designates as strategic, are treated as high regardless of their Contract Value.
- **Deal Desk** means the sales operations team that reviews pricing, discounts and order form terms.
- **Standard Paper** means Northwind's own template agreements, which have been pre-approved by Legal.
- **Non-Standard Paper** means an agreement on a counterparty's form, or on Northwind's form with negotiated changes to pre-approved language.
- **Counterparty** means the other party to the agreement.
- **Legal Intake portal** means the web tool through which review requests are submitted and tracked.

## 3. Governing Law

### 3.1 Choice of law

Agreements must be governed by the law of Delaware or New York, and any other governing law requires prior written approval from the General Counsel. Northwind is incorporated in Delaware and headquartered in New York, and its outside counsel, insurance program and litigation experience are concentrated in those two jurisdictions. A request for another governing law must state the Counterparty's reason, the law proposed, and the deal's Contract Value, and must be submitted through the Legal Intake portal before the Deal Owner makes any commitment on the point.

### 3.2 Venue and dispute resolution

Disputes under customer and vendor agreements are heard in the state or federal courts located in New York County, New York, or in Delaware, unless the General Counsel approves arbitration or another forum. Jury waivers and limitations of liability follow the clause library. Class-action waivers are not used in business-to-business agreements.

### 3.3 Foreign counterparties

For agreements with counterparties outside the United States, the Deal Owner states the Counterparty's country on the intake form. The Legal Department may require local-law advice where an agreement will be performed abroad, and the Chief Financial Officer approves any agreement under which Northwind would invoice in a currency other than U.S. dollars.

## 4. Special Approval Triggers

### 4.1 Staff-related restrictions in partner agreements

In partner agreements, any restriction on soliciting the other side's staff may last no longer than 12 months, and a longer restriction requires approval from the Chief Revenue Officer. The Chief Revenue Officer considers whether the partner's staff are critical to Northwind's go-to-market plan and whether the restriction is reciprocal. Approval is recorded in the Legal Intake portal and expires with the agreement.

### 4.2 Agreements governed by California law

Where California law governs, the agreement must not contain any clause that restricts the other party from competing or from working in its profession or trade. California courts treat such restraints as void, and a clause that attempts one can expose Northwind to statutory penalties and a claim for fees. The Legal Reviewer deletes any such clause that appears in a Counterparty draft and tells the Deal Owner.

### 4.3 Other triggers

In addition to the matters above, the following always require review by a Legal Reviewer before signature:

- an uncapped indemnity or liability exceeding twelve months of fees;
- exclusivity, most-favored-customer pricing or non-compete commitments by Northwind;
- source code escrow or source code license;
- service level credits exceeding five percent of monthly fees;
- an obligation to comply with the Counterparty's policies that Northwind has not reviewed;
- audit rights exceeding once per year; and
- any commitment to use specific subcontractors or to host in a specific country.

## 5. Signature Authority

### 5.1 Schedule A: Delegations

| Role | Customer revenue agreements | Vendor and procurement agreements |
|---|---|---|
| Chief Executive Officer | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Chief Financial Officer | Up to $5,000,000 | Up to $2,000,000 |
| Chief Revenue Officer | Up to $2,000,000 | Not authorized |
| Chief Technology Officer | Not authorized | Up to $1,000,000 for engineering tools and cloud capacity |
| General Counsel | Standard Paper and any Legal-negotiated agreements up to $1,000,000 | Standard Paper and any Legal-negotiated agreements up to $500,000 |
| VP Sales | Standard Paper up to $250,000 | Not authorized |
| Senior Director, Customer Success | Renewals on Standard Paper up to $100,000 | Not authorized |
| Department heads | Not authorized | Up to $50,000 within approved budget |
| Procurement Manager | Not authorized | Up to $25,000 on Standard Paper |

An Authorized Signatory may not sign an agreement in which they have a personal interest, or approve an agreement that they negotiated and that falls above the next lower threshold, without a second approver at the same or higher level.

### 5.2 Aggregation and splitting

Related agreements with one Counterparty are added together when determining the correct signatory. A deal may not be divided into several smaller agreements to stay under a threshold. Multi-year renewals use the total Contract Value for the entire term.

### 5.3 Electronic signature

Agreements are signed through the company's approved e-signature platform. Wet-ink signatures are accepted only on the Legal Department's request. Signatories may not delegate their credentials to assistants or colleagues.

## 6. Review Routing

### 6.1 Standard Paper

Agreements on Standard Paper with no changes to pre-approved language need only Deal Desk review of pricing and discounts and signature by an appropriate Authorized Signatory. Discounts above 25 percent of list price require the VP Sales; above 40 percent, the Chief Revenue Officer.

### 6.2 Non-Standard Paper

A Deal Owner submits Non-Standard Paper to the Legal Intake portal with the draft, the Counterparty's name and country, the Contract Value, a summary of the business case and the desired signing date. Legal commits to the following review times.

| Contract Value | Initial response | Target to sign-off |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 3 business days | 7 business days |
| Medium | 2 business days | 10 business days |
| High | 1 business day | 15 business days, with a named Legal lead |

### 6.3 Finance review

The Deal Desk and Finance review revenue recognition implications, payment terms longer than net 60, non-standard billing, multi-year price protection and any change that would require a special accounting treatment. The Chief Financial Officer decides disputed matters.

### 6.4 Security and privacy review

Deals involving customer security questionnaires, Restricted information or personal data are routed to the Security team and the Privacy Office, who respond within the time frames in the Legal Intake portal.

## 7. Approval Records

Each approval is recorded in the Legal Intake portal, which keeps the version of the agreement reviewed, the name and role of the approver, the date and any conditions. The executed version is stored in the contract repository, and the repository entry is linked to the intake record. A contract signed without a corresponding intake record is reported to the General Counsel and the Audit Committee.

## 8. Amendments, Renewals and Terminations

### 8.1 Amendments

An amendment is approved at the same level as the original agreement, calculated on the combined Contract Value after the amendment. Pure administrative amendments (change of notice address, correction of party name) may be signed by the Legal Department.

### 8.2 Renewals

Auto-renewals are disabled by default. A renewal on unchanged terms is approved by the signatory who would be authorized for the new term's Contract Value. A renewal on changed terms follows the routing for Non-Standard Paper.

### 8.3 Termination

Notices of termination or non-renewal are sent by the Legal Department, after the Business Owner confirms that the offboarding steps in the Vendor and Third-Party Risk Policy have been scheduled. A Deal Owner may not tell a Counterparty that Northwind will terminate without Legal's approval.

## 9. Emergency Approvals

Where an agreement must be signed outside business hours to protect service continuity, safety or a legal deadline, any two members of the executive team, one of whom must be the General Counsel or the Chief Financial Officer, may approve it by email. The Deal Owner then files the full intake within two business days.

## 10. Roles and Responsibilities

- **General Counsel** owns the Matrix, approves departures where named, and reports annually to the Audit Committee.
- **Deputy General Counsel** manages Legal Intake and approves staffing of reviews.
- **Chief Revenue Officer** approves the matters assigned in Part 4 and sets strategic-deal designations.
- **Chief Financial Officer** is accountable for financial thresholds and vendor spend.
- **Deal Owners** start the process early, give complete information and do not make verbal commitments.
- **Deal Desk** reviews pricing and order forms.
- **Legal Reviewers** apply the Matrix and record approvals.
- **Internal Audit** samples executed agreements annually against the Matrix.

## 11. Training and Communication

New sales, procurement and customer success staff receive training on the Matrix during onboarding. The Legal Department sends a reminder each quarter, highlights any changes to Schedule A and publishes a short digest of the most common intake errors.

## 12. Exceptions and Escalation

A person who believes this Matrix prevents a sensible business outcome may escalate to the General Counsel and the Chief Financial Officer jointly. Their decision is final and is recorded in the Legal Intake portal. Exceptions to signature authority require the written approval of the Chief Executive Officer.

## 13. Enforcement

Signing, or promising to sign, outside the authority in Schedule A is a serious breach. The company may decline to be bound by an agreement signed without authority, and the person responsible may face discipline up to termination. Repeated failures to use Legal Intake will be reported to the person's department head and to the Audit Committee.

## Appendix A. Worked Examples of Signature Authority

**Example 1.** A mid-market customer wants a one-year subscription worth $180,000 on Northwind's standard order form. The Deal Desk confirms that the discount is under 25 percent. The VP Sales may sign, since the Contract Value is below $250,000 and the paper is standard.

**Example 2.** A large enterprise wants a three-year subscription worth $1,800,000 on its own form. The deal is high band and Non-Standard Paper. A Legal Reviewer with a named Legal lead handles it. The Chief Revenue Officer may sign up to $2,000,000 once Legal has signed off. If the Counterparty asks for a service level credit above five percent of monthly fees, Legal must approve that point too.

**Example 3.** Engineering wants to buy a three-year commitment on observability tooling worth $900,000. The Chief Technology Officer may sign up to $1,000,000 for engineering tools, but because the vendor will receive production logs, the Security team and the Privacy Office must complete their review first.

**Example 4.** Facilities proposes a two-year lease on additional office space with a total commitment of $3,400,000. This is a procurement agreement beyond the Chief Financial Officer's threshold for vendor agreements, so the Chief Executive Officer must sign after the Audit Committee has been told.

**Example 5.** A department head wants to split a $70,000 consulting engagement into two statements of work of $35,000 each. Under Section 5.2 the two are added together, so the department head's limit of $50,000 is exceeded and a higher signatory is required.

## Appendix B. Service Level for Legal Intake

Legal measures itself on first response time, time to sign-off and the proportion of requests that need more than two rounds of comments. The Deputy General Counsel publishes these figures monthly, along with the number of requests by value band, the top five reasons for escalation and the number of requests returned for missing information. Sales leadership and the Legal Department review the figures together each quarter, with the aim of reducing delay without reducing control.

## Appendix C. Information Needed for a Complete Intake

A request submitted to the Legal Intake portal is complete when it includes: the Counterparty's full legal name and country of incorporation; the type of counterparty (customer, vendor, partner or employee); the Contract Value and Value Band; the draft or the form to be used; the products or services involved; whether personal data or Restricted information will be handled; the Northwind business sponsor; the date by which signature is needed and why; and a short description of any points that have already been agreed with the Counterparty. Incomplete requests are returned without review.

## Appendix D. Maintenance of Schedule A

The General Counsel and the Chief Financial Officer review Schedule A at least once a year and whenever an Authorized Signatory changes roles or leaves. Human Resources notifies the Legal Department of departures and role changes within one business day, and the Legal Department removes the signatory's authority on the effective date. A new signatory must complete signature-authority training before being added to the schedule. The e-signature platform is configured so that only people on Schedule A can complete the signature step for Northwind.
