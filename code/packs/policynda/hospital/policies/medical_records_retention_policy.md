# Medical Records Retention Policy

**Bayview Health Partners**
Policy owner: Health Information Management, with the Office of the General Counsel
Applies to: all Bayview facilities and all records, in any medium
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Next scheduled review: January 1, 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Bayview Health Partners ("Bayview") creates and receives an enormous volume of records. A medical record supports patient care today, defends a malpractice claim tomorrow and may be needed by a researcher in ten years. Keeping records too briefly breaks the law and endangers patients. Keeping them too long increases the cost of storage, the cost of responding to legal requests and the damage if a breach occurs. This policy sets out how long each type of record is kept, how it is stored, when it must be preserved because of a legal hold, and how it is destroyed.

### 1.2 Scope

The policy applies to all records created or received by Bayview in the course of its business, in paper, electronic, imaging, audio, video and any other form, and to the copies of those records held by business associates on Bayview's behalf. It covers the designated record set, which is the medical and billing record and any other record used to make decisions about patients, as well as administrative, financial, research and human resources records.

### 1.3 Related policies

This policy is read with the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy, the Confidentiality Agreement Policy and the Business Associate and Vendor Policy. When a record is held by a vendor, the vendor's duty to return or destroy it is governed by the vendor agreement and by those policies.

---

## 2. Definitions and Roles

### 2.1 Definitions

**Record** means recorded information, regardless of medium, created or received in the transaction of Bayview's business.

**Designated record set** means the medical records and billing records about individuals, the enrollment, payment, claims adjudication and case management record systems, and any other records used to make decisions about individuals.

**Legal hold** means a written instruction from the Office of the General Counsel to preserve records that are or may be relevant to litigation, a government investigation, an audit or a regulatory inquiry.

**Retention period** means the minimum length of time a record must be kept, counted from the trigger event stated in the schedule.

**Destruction** means rendering records unreadable and unrecoverable by an approved method.

### 2.2 Roles

**Health Information Management (HIM)** is responsible for the medical record, keeps the retention schedule, and supervises storage and destruction.

**Office of the General Counsel** issues and releases legal holds and resolves questions about the schedule.

**Records Coordinators** are named by each department to manage that department's records, apply the schedule and sign destruction authorizations.

**Information Technology** keeps electronic systems that support retention, including archiving, backup and legal hold functions.

**Privacy Office** advises on the confidentiality of records and the safe destruction of media.

## 3. Retention Obligations Owed to Bayview by Outsiders

Bayview often asks outsiders to hold records or information on its behalf, or to protect information it has given them. This section describes how those obligations relate to the retention periods that Bayview itself is required to observe. The rules about when information must be returned are in the Confidentiality Agreement Policy and the Business Associate and Vendor Policy.

### 3.1 Continuation of obligations

Every agreement must say that the recipient's confidentiality obligations carry on after the agreement itself has ended.

The reason is simple. The sensitivity of clinical and operational information does not end when a project does, and Bayview's own obligations to keep records last well beyond the relationship. This provision works together with the return and destruction provisions of the Confidentiality Agreement Policy: information that is returned or destroyed ceases to be a risk, but anything an outsider properly keeps, for example because the law requires it, stays protected for the continuing period.

### 3.2 Limit on continuing obligations

Those continuing obligations may not extend beyond 180 months.

Fifteen years is longer than the retention period for almost any Bayview record other than records for minors and certain research records. Longer periods are difficult to administer and are often resisted. The Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy sets out the minimum period that applies when personal data is concerned, and the two should be read together.

### 3.3 Records held by business associates

When a business associate holds records for Bayview, Health Information Management confirms at least every two years that the business associate can still find and return them. Business associates are told which retention periods apply to the records they hold, and are told at the end of each retention period whether the records are to be destroyed or returned.

## 4. Retention Schedule

### 4.1 General principles

The schedule in Section 4.2 is a minimum. Longer periods apply where a legal hold is in force, where a statute or accreditation standard requires, or where the record is part of a continuing research study. If periods conflict, the longer prevails. The retention period begins on the trigger event shown in the schedule, and is calculated in full years.

### 4.2 Schedule

| Record | Retention period | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Adult inpatient and outpatient medical records | 10 years | Last date of service |
| Records of minors | Until age 25, or 10 years after last service if longer | Date of birth or last service |
| Emergency department records | 10 years | Date of visit |
| Diagnostic images (radiology, nuclear medicine) | 7 years for reports; 5 years for images (10 years for mammography images where no further exam is done) | Date of study |
| Laboratory reports | 10 years | Date of report |
| Pathology slides and blocks | 10 years for slides; 20 years for blocks | Date of examination |
| Birth and delivery records | 25 years | Date of birth |
| Immunization records | Permanent | Date of administration |
| Billing and claims records | 10 years | Date of final payment |
| Medicare cost reports and supporting records | 10 years | Date of filing |
| Credentialing and peer review files | 10 years after the end of privileges | End of privileges |
| Employee health files | 30 years | End of employment |
| Personnel files | 7 years | End of employment |
| Accounts payable and general ledger | 7 years | End of fiscal year |
| Tax returns and supporting papers | Permanent | Filing date |
| Research records and consent forms | 10 years after the study closes (or longer where sponsor or regulation requires) | Study closure |
| Incident and safety reports | 10 years | Date of event |
| Compliance and HIPAA documentation | 6 years | Creation or last effective date |
| Governing board minutes and bylaws | Permanent | Adoption |
| Security camera footage | 90 days, unless it records an incident | Recording |

HIM keeps the full schedule, including footnotes for unusual record types, on the intranet. Departments that believe their records belong in a different category should ask HIM before applying a shorter period.

### 4.3 Medical records of closed or sold facilities

If a Bayview facility closes or is transferred, the General Counsel arranges for the medical records to be transferred to a custodian who will keep them for the required period and permit patients to obtain copies. The California Department of Public Health and the Medical Board are notified as required.

## 5. Storage and Access

### 5.1 Electronic records

The electronic health record is the legal medical record. Electronic records are backed up daily, with copies held at a second site, and are tested for restoration every quarter. Audit logs for the record are kept for at least six years. Access is controlled according to the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy.

### 5.2 Paper records

Paper records that have not yet been scanned are stored in locked rooms with fire protection and temperature control. Records sent to off-site storage are indexed by box, and each box has a destruction review date. The off-site vendor is a business associate. Records Coordinators keep a log of every request to retrieve a box.

### 5.3 Scanning

Paper may be destroyed after scanning only if HIM has approved the scanning process, the scanned image is complete and legible, and the quality control sample shows an error rate below 1 percent. The scanned image is then the record.

### 5.4 Release of records

Requests for copies of medical records are handled by HIM in accordance with the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy. Requests made by subpoena or court order go through the Office of the General Counsel.

---

## 6. Legal Holds

### 6.1 Issuing a hold

The General Counsel issues a legal hold when Bayview becomes aware of litigation, a claim, an investigation or an audit that could involve records. The hold names the custodians and types of records, and takes effect at once. Information Technology suspends automatic deletion for the covered systems on the same day.

### 6.2 Effect

Records under a hold may not be destroyed, altered or moved, even if the retention period has passed. Custodians confirm in writing that they have received the hold and repeat the confirmation every six months.

### 6.3 Release

Only the General Counsel may release a hold, in writing. After release, the normal schedule applies, and records whose periods have already expired may be destroyed in the next destruction cycle.

---

## 7. Destruction

### 7.1 Authorization

Records may be destroyed only after the retention period has ended, no legal hold applies, and a Records Coordinator and HIM have signed a destruction authorization. HIM keeps a permanent log of what was destroyed, when and by whom, though the log does not contain the content of the records.

### 7.2 Methods

Paper records containing PHI are cross-cut shredded or pulped by an approved vendor, under a business associate agreement, and a certificate of destruction is obtained. Electronic media are cleared, purged or physically destroyed in accordance with NIST Special Publication 800-88, and certificates are retained. Deleting a file or reformatting a disk does not count as destruction.

### 7.3 Events and exceptions

If a record is destroyed by mistake or in the wrong way, the person who finds the error reports it at once to HIM and the Privacy Office. The Privacy Office decides whether the event is a breach.

## 7A. Special Topics

### 7A.1 Records of minors and sensitive services

Records of minors are kept until the patient reaches age 25 or for 10 years after the last service, whichever is later. Records of services that a minor may consent to under California law, such as reproductive health, sexually transmitted infection, mental health and substance use treatment, are marked in the electronic health record so that they are not released to a parent or guardian without the minor's authorization. HIM reviews these markers each quarter. Records of substance use disorder treatment covered by 42 C.F.R. Part 2 are segregated and may be released only as that regulation permits.

### 7A.2 Mergers, closures and ownership changes

If Bayview acquires a practice, HIM takes custody of its records, inventories them, assigns retention dates according to the schedule and, if the records are paper, scans them or stores them. If a Bayview practice is sold to another provider, patients are notified by letter, and a custodian agrees in writing to keep the records for the required period. HIM maintains a list of every record collection that Bayview holds on behalf of another organization, and the date by which each is to be reviewed.

### 7A.3 Disaster recovery

Information Technology and HIM test the recovery of the electronic health record at least twice a year, and the results are reported to the Compliance Committee. Paper records that are essential for patient care are duplicated or digitized so that a single fire or flood cannot destroy them. After a disaster, HIM compiles a list of lost records and tells the Privacy Office, which decides whether a breach notification is needed.

### 7A.4 Email, messaging and text

Email and instant messages are business records. Messages that form part of a patient's care must be moved into the medical record. Other messages are kept for three years by automatic archiving unless the content falls into a category with a longer period, such as a legal hold. Employees may not use personal email or messaging for Bayview records. Texts that relate to patient care are made only through the secure messaging tool.

### 7A.5 Research data

Research records are retained for the period required by the study protocol, the sponsor and applicable regulations, which is generally 10 years after the study is closed, but for studies of drugs or devices intended for approval, at least two years after the marketing application is approved or the investigation is discontinued. Records of studies with minors are kept until the participant reaches age 25 or for ten years, whichever is later. The Principal Investigator may not destroy research records without the approval of the Office of Research Administration.

### 7A.6 Requests from patients to delete records

Patients sometimes ask Bayview to delete information from their records. Bayview cannot erase information that the law requires it to keep, but patients may ask for an amendment under the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy. HIM explains the reasons for retention in writing and tells the patient how to submit an amendment request or a statement of disagreement.

---

## 8. Monitoring, Training and Enforcement

HIM audits compliance with the schedule each year, by sampling departments and off-site boxes. Results go to the Compliance Committee. Records Coordinators complete training on this policy when appointed and every two years. Failure to follow the policy, including destroying records without authorization or ignoring a legal hold, is a serious violation and may result in discipline up to termination, and in the case of intentional destruction of records subject to a hold, referral to law enforcement.

---

## 9. Exceptions

Exceptions to the retention periods are not permitted without the written approval of the General Counsel. Requests are made through HIM and are recorded in the exception register.
