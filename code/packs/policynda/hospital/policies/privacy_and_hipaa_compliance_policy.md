# Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy

**Bayview Health Partners**
Policy owner: Office of the Chief Privacy Officer
Applies to: all workforce members, medical staff, students, volunteers and business associates
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Next scheduled review: January 1, 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Patients trust Bayview Health Partners ("Bayview") with the most personal facts of their lives. This policy explains how Bayview meets that trust and satisfies the federal Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 and its implementing rules (together, "HIPAA"), the California Confidentiality of Medical Information Act ("CMIA"), the Lanterman-Petris-Short Act, the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act ("CCPA"), and related state law. It identifies the people who are responsible, describes the rules for using and disclosing information, and sets out what happens when something goes wrong.

### 1.2 Scope

The policy covers all protected health information ("PHI") that Bayview creates, receives, maintains or transmits, in any form: paper, electronic, spoken or recorded. It also covers other personal information about patients, employees, donors, research subjects and website visitors. It applies to every Bayview hospital, clinic, laboratory, pharmacy, research unit and shared service, and to everyone who works for them.

### 1.3 Relationship to other policies

This policy is the main privacy policy. The Patient Rights Policy describes the rights that patients have and how they are exercised. The Business Associate and Vendor Policy governs vendors that handle PHI. The Confidentiality Agreement Policy governs stand-alone confidentiality agreements. The Medical Records Retention Policy sets out how long records are kept. Sections 3 to 5 of this policy contain the privacy provisions that bear on agreements with outside parties, and should be read with those policies.

## 2. Definitions and Governance

### 2.1 Definitions

**Protected health information** means individually identifiable health information, held or transmitted in any form, that relates to a person's past, present or future health, the provision of care to the person, or payment for that care.

**Personal information** under the CCPA means information that identifies, relates to or could reasonably be linked with a particular California consumer or household. Where PHI is covered by HIPAA or the CMIA, the CCPA's exemptions apply, but Bayview treats all personal information with comparable care.

**Use** means the sharing, employment, application, utilization, examination or analysis of information within Bayview. **Disclosure** means the release, transfer, provision of access to, or divulging of information outside Bayview.

**Minimum necessary** means the standard that requires Bayview to use, disclose and request only the PHI reasonably needed to accomplish the purpose.

**Breach** means the acquisition, access, use or disclosure of PHI in a manner not permitted by HIPAA that compromises its security or privacy, unless a risk assessment shows a low probability that the PHI has been compromised.

**Workforce member** means employees, volunteers, trainees, physicians with privileges and other persons whose work is under Bayview's direct control.

### 2.2 Governance

The **Chief Privacy Officer** is the HIPAA Privacy Officer designated under 45 C.F.R. 164.530 and leads the Privacy Office. The **Chief Information Security Officer** is the Security Officer designated under 45 C.F.R. 164.308. The **Privacy and Security Committee**, chaired by the Chief Compliance Officer, meets monthly to review incidents, audits, risk assessments and policy changes. Each facility has a **Privacy Liaison** who is the first point of contact for staff questions.

## 3. Protecting Information When Dealing With Outside Parties

Bayview sometimes exchanges information with outside parties under a written agreement. This section sets out the privacy protections Bayview needs from those parties. Additional rules for vendors that handle PHI appear in the Business Associate and Vendor Policy, and for stand-alone confidentiality agreements in the Confidentiality Agreement Policy.

### 3.1 Prompt notice of incidents

Every agreement must require the recipient to inform Bayview without delay of any use or disclosure of our information that was not authorized.

Bayview has its own deadlines under HIPAA, which requires notice to affected individuals without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery of a breach, and under California Health and Safety Code section 1280.15, which requires a licensed clinic or hospital to report certain breaches to the California Department of Public Health and the patient within 15 business days. Bayview can only meet those deadlines if an outside party tells it quickly. Section 7 of this policy sets out the internal breach response process that starts when notice arrives. Vendors that may transfer personal data outside the United States are covered by the location rule in the Business Associate and Vendor Policy.

### 3.2 No retained residuals

Where personal data is shared, the agreement may not allow the recipient to hold back any portion of it once the rest has been returned or destroyed.

The Privacy Office sometimes sees proposals that allow a recipient to keep a "residual" copy for archival, regulatory or backup reasons. When the information includes personal data, Bayview cannot accept such proposals, because it would remain responsible for a breach of information that it can no longer control.

### 3.3 No copying

Where personal data is shared, the agreement may not let the recipient make duplicates or reproductions of that data.

Each copy of PHI is another place from which the information can be lost. Where the recipient needs to work from the data, access should be given in a controlled environment so that no separate copies are made. If a business need for copies arises, the business owner consults the Privacy Office before agreeing to anything.

### 3.4 Safeguards

The recipient must be obliged to protect our information using administrative, physical and technical safeguards.

These are the three categories used in the HIPAA Security Rule. Administrative safeguards are policies, risk analysis, training and sanctions. Physical safeguards are facility controls, workstation security and device handling. Technical safeguards are access controls, audit logging, integrity controls and transmission security including encryption.

### 3.5 Privacy review of proposals

The Privacy Office reviews every proposal to share PHI with an outside party before sharing begins. The review considers: the legal basis for the disclosure (treatment, payment, operations, authorization, or a specific exception); whether the minimum necessary standard has been applied; whether the recipient is a business associate; whether the disclosure must be recorded in an accounting of disclosures; whether the information includes substance use disorder records covered by 42 C.F.R. Part 2, mental health records covered by the Lanterman-Petris-Short Act, or HIV test results, which carry additional restrictions under California law; and whether patient authorization is needed.

## 4. Duration and Retention of Obligations

### 4.1 Protection after termination

When personal data is shared, protection must continue after termination for at least 72 months, and only the Chief Privacy Officer may approve a shorter period.

The 72 months correspond to the six-year period for which HIPAA requires Bayview to keep its own compliance documentation. The Chief Privacy Officer will consider a request for a shorter period only in writing and only if the personal data has been returned or destroyed, or has been fully de-identified under the HIPAA standard. The Medical Records Retention Policy sets an upper limit for the period for which such obligations may run, and requests that raise questions about that limit should be addressed to the Office of the General Counsel.

### 4.2 De-identification

Information is de-identified, and no longer PHI, only if it meets either the expert determination method in 45 C.F.R. 164.514(b)(1) or the safe harbor method in 164.514(b)(2), which requires the removal of eighteen listed identifiers and the absence of actual knowledge that the remaining information could identify an individual. The Privacy Office approves any de-identification protocol. Data that has been de-identified by Bayview must not be re-identified, and recipients of de-identified data may not attempt to re-identify it.

### 4.3 Limited data sets

A limited data set excludes direct identifiers but may retain dates, city, state and zip code. It may be used for research, public health and health care operations only under a data use agreement approved by the Privacy Office.

## 5. Sharing Information Inside and Around Bayview

### 5.1 Workforce access

The agreement must allow Bayview to pass the information to its own employees, physicians and other workforce members who need it to do their jobs.

This is consistent with the HIPAA role-based access model. Each job at Bayview is assigned to an access profile that lists the categories of PHI the person needs. Supervisors review profiles each year and whenever a person changes jobs. Access is withdrawn on the day a workforce member leaves.

### 5.2 Advisors, auditors and accreditors

The agreement must also allow Bayview to disclose the information to consultants, auditors, accreditation bodies, outside counsel and other professional advisors.

Bayview is regularly reviewed by The Joint Commission, DNV, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, the California Department of Public Health, external financial auditors and compliance consultants. Counsel and consultants who receive PHI from Bayview in the course of their work are business associates and must sign a business associate agreement first.

### 5.3 Patient access and amendment

Patients have a right to inspect and obtain a copy of their designated record set, usually within 30 days of the request (with a single 30-day extension on written notice), to request an amendment, to receive an accounting of disclosures for the previous six years, to request restrictions on use, and to receive communications by alternative means. Health Information Management administers these rights. Charges are limited to the cost-based fee permitted by HIPAA and California law.

### 5.4 Marketing, fundraising and sale of PHI

PHI may not be used for marketing, and may not be sold, without the patient's written authorization. Fundraising communications may use limited demographic information and dates of service, and must offer a clear way to opt out. The Foundation consults the Privacy Office before any new campaign.

## 6. Workforce Responsibilities

### 6.1 General duties

Every workforce member must access PHI only when needed for a job duty, must not discuss patients in public areas, must lock or log off screens when leaving a workstation, must use only approved devices and messaging tools, and must report any suspected privacy incident immediately. Looking at records out of curiosity, including the records of family members, friends and colleagues, is prohibited and is a violation even if the person does not disclose what was seen.

### 6.2 Training

New workforce members complete privacy and security training before they get access to any system and again every year. Physicians with privileges complete training as part of credentialing. Role-based training is required for workforce members in Health Information Management, Patient Access, Revenue Cycle, Research and Information Technology. Completion is tracked, and access may be suspended if training is overdue by more than 30 days.

### 6.3 Mobile devices and remote work

PHI may be stored on a personal mobile device only through the Bayview-managed secure container. Laptops must have full-disk encryption. Work from home requires manager approval, use of the virtual private network, and a private space where screens cannot be seen or conversations overheard. Texting of orders is not permitted, and secure messaging must be used for any clinical communication that includes PHI.

### 6.4 Photography and recording

Photographs and recordings of patients may be made only for treatment, education or other purposes for which a signed authorization is on file, and only with Bayview-owned or Bayview-managed devices. Staff may not post photographs or recordings of patients, or of patient care areas where patients can be identified, on any social media site; the Social Media Policy gives further rules.

## 6A. Special Categories of Information

### 6A.1 Reproductive health and gender-affirming care

Bayview applies stricter controls to records of reproductive health care and gender-affirming care. These records are marked in the electronic health record. Requests from outside bodies for these records are sent to the Office of the General Counsel, which reviews them under HIPAA, the California Reproductive Privacy Act and other law before anything is released.

### 6A.2 Minors

Parents or guardians usually have access to a minor's records. Where a minor has lawfully consented to care without parental permission, the minor controls access to the record of that care. Staff ask patients who are 12 or older which information may be shared with parents when they are being treated for sensitive conditions.

### 6A.3 Psychotherapy notes and mental health records

Psychotherapy notes kept separately from the medical record receive special protection under HIPAA and may be released only with specific authorization, with a few exceptions. Records of involuntary treatment under the Lanterman-Petris-Short Act may be released only as that Act allows.

### 6A.4 Genetic information

Genetic test results are health information under HIPAA and are also protected by the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act and the California Genetic Information Privacy Act. They may not be used to deny employment or insurance, and employees who administer wellness programs may not collect them.

### 6A.5 Law enforcement and legal process

Requests from law enforcement, courts and attorneys are sent to the Office of the General Counsel and the Privacy Office. Staff must not release records to police at the bedside, except to report identifying information in limited cases that the HIPAA Privacy Rule allows. Subpoenas must be accompanied by a satisfactory assurance or a court order before records are released.

### 6A.6 Public health, abuse reporting and required disclosures

California law requires Bayview staff to report some matters, such as suspected child or elder abuse, injuries by firearm and certain communicable diseases. These reports are made by the staff member who has the duty, with the help of Social Work or the Privacy Office, and are noted in the record.

## 7. Incident Response and Breach Notification

### 7.1 Reporting

Any workforce member who suspects an unauthorized use, access or disclosure of PHI reports it immediately to the Privacy Office through the Privacy Hotline or the incident reporting system. Reports can be made anonymously.

### 7.2 Investigation

The Privacy Office opens an investigation within one business day. The Information Security Office contains any technical problem. The Privacy Office performs the four-factor risk assessment required by the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule: the nature and extent of the PHI, the unauthorized person who used it or to whom it was disclosed, whether the PHI was actually acquired or viewed, and the extent to which the risk has been mitigated. The result is documented.

### 7.3 Notification

If the incident is a breach, the Privacy Office sends written notice to affected individuals without unreasonable delay and in any event within the HIPAA and California deadlines mentioned in Section 3.1. If the breach affects more than 500 residents of a state, it is reported to the Secretary of Health and Human Services and to the media at the same time, and breaches affecting fewer people are reported to the Secretary within 60 days of the end of the calendar year. California law also requires a report to the California Attorney General if more than 500 California residents are affected. Notices explain what happened, what information was involved, what Bayview is doing and what the person can do, and offer credit monitoring where Social Security numbers were exposed.

### 7.4 Learning from incidents

The Privacy and Security Committee reviews each breach, decides on corrective measures, and tracks completion. The Privacy Office reports trends to the Board Compliance Committee every quarter.

## 8. Auditing and Monitoring

The Privacy Office, with Internal Audit, monitors compliance with this policy. Activities include random review of access logs for celebrity, employee and VIP patients, targeted audits of workforce members who have recently been disciplined, periodic reviews of business associate compliance, quarterly review of disclosure accounting, and an annual enterprise-wide security risk analysis under the Security Rule. Findings are given to the manager responsible, who has 30 days to submit a remediation plan.

---

## 9. Exceptions and Enforcement

### 9.1 Exceptions

An exception to this policy requires the written approval of the Chief Privacy Officer, unless the policy names another approver. The request must state the provision, the reason, the risk and any compensating controls. Exceptions are recorded in the Privacy Office register and reported to the Privacy and Security Committee.

### 9.2 Sanctions

Violations are handled under the sanctions policy that HIPAA requires. Sanctions range from coaching and retraining to suspension, termination, loss of privileges, and referral to licensing boards and law enforcement. The severity depends on whether the violation was accidental, a failure to follow policy, or intentional, and on any harm caused. Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith is prohibited.
