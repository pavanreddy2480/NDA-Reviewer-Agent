# Patient Rights Policy

**Bayview Health Partners**
Policy owner: Office of Patient Experience and Advocacy
Applies to: all Bayview hospitals, emergency departments, clinics and home health services
Effective date: January 1, 2026
Next scheduled review: January 1, 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

Every person who comes to Bayview Health Partners ("Bayview") for care is entitled to be treated with dignity, to take part in decisions about his or her care, and to have a way to raise concerns. This policy lists the rights that patients have, explains how staff honor them, and describes how patients and their families can complain when they believe a right has not been respected. The policy implements the Medicare Conditions of Participation at 42 C.F.R. 482.13, the California Patients' Bill of Rights, California Health and Safety Code sections 1262.6 and 1288.4, The Joint Commission standards on rights and responsibilities of the individual, and Section 1557 of the Affordable Care Act.

### 1.2 Scope

The policy applies to every person who provides care or services at Bayview: employees, medical staff, residents and students, volunteers, chaplains and agency clinical staff. It applies to patients of all ages and, where a patient cannot act for himself or herself, to the patient's representative.

### 1.3 Other policies

The privacy of health information is covered in detail by the Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy. This policy refers to it where relevant and does not repeat it.

---

## 2. Definitions

**Patient** means a person who receives, or has asked to receive, care at a Bayview facility, including emergency, inpatient, outpatient, observation and telehealth care.

**Patient representative** means a person who is legally authorized to make decisions for a patient who cannot make them, such as a health care agent named in an advance directive, a conservator, a parent or guardian of a minor, or a surrogate recognized by California law.

**Grievance** means a formal written or verbal complaint about care, safety, abuse, neglect or a violation of rights that is not resolved at the time it is made, or that involves a serious matter.

**Interpreter** means a qualified person who has been trained in medical interpreting and who is competent in both English and the language of the patient, or a qualified video or telephone service.

**Restraint** and **seclusion** have the meanings given in 42 C.F.R. 482.13(e).

## 3. Rights of Patients

### 3.1 Respectful and non-discriminatory care

Patients are treated with courtesy and respect, and with consideration of their cultural, spiritual and personal values. Bayview does not discriminate on the basis of race, color, national origin, ancestry, religion, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, age, disability, marital status, language, source of payment, or immigration status. Staff address patients by their preferred name and pronouns, and the electronic record has fields for them. Patients may ask for a caregiver of a particular gender for intimate examinations whenever it is practical.

### 3.2 Access to care and emergency services

Anyone who comes to an emergency department is screened and, if an emergency medical condition exists, stabilized or transferred appropriately, regardless of ability to pay, as required by the federal Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act. Bayview posts signs that tell patients of this right. Financial questions are not asked until after the medical screening is begun.

### 3.3 Information and communication

Patients have the right to receive information about their diagnosis, treatment, prognosis and expected outcomes in words they can understand. Language services are provided free of charge, at all hours, in person, by phone or by video. Staff may not ask family members, and especially minors, to interpret, except in an emergency when no qualified interpreter is available, or when the patient insists after being told that free interpreter services are on offer. Written materials are available in the threshold languages of the communities Bayview serves, currently Spanish, Vietnamese, Tagalog, Mandarin, Cantonese, Armenian and Russian. Auxiliary aids, such as sign language interpreters, large-print documents, captioning and screen readers, are provided at no cost to patients with disabilities.

### 3.4 Participation in decisions and informed consent

Patients, or their representatives, make decisions about care after being told about the nature of the proposed treatment, its risks and benefits, the alternatives and the consequences of refusing. The physician who will perform a procedure is responsible for obtaining informed consent, and the discussion is recorded in the medical record in addition to the signed form. Patients may refuse treatment to the extent permitted by law and are told of the medical consequences. Consent for minors follows California law, including the minor consent statutes for reproductive, mental health and substance use services.

### 3.5 Advance directives and end-of-life choices

Patients are asked on admission whether they have an advance health care directive, and given information and help to prepare one if they wish. Bayview honors advance directives, POLST forms and legally valid wishes about resuscitation and life-sustaining treatment, and the End of Life Option Act where the statutory requirements are met. A practitioner who has a moral objection to a request must tell the patient promptly and help the patient to transfer to another provider. Palliative care consultations are available to any patient with a serious illness.

### 3.6 Pain management

Patients have the right to have pain assessed and treated. Staff ask about pain at each assessment, use validated scales appropriate to age and cognitive ability, explain options including non-drug approaches, and reassess after any intervention. Patients with a history of substance use disorder receive evidence-based pain treatment, and are not refused pain relief merely because of their history.

### 3.7 Personal safety and freedom from abuse

Patients are entitled to receive care in a safe environment. They are free from abuse, neglect and harassment, including from other patients, visitors and staff. Restraint and seclusion are used only to ensure immediate physical safety when less restrictive measures have failed, on the order of a physician or other licensed practitioner, and for the shortest time possible. Staff trained in de-escalation attend all behavioral emergencies. Each use of restraint is reviewed by the unit manager, and the Patient Safety Committee reviews summary data every quarter.

### 3.8 Visitors and support persons

Patients may decide who may visit and may designate a support person who can be present during care. Bayview does not restrict, limit or deny visitation on the basis of race, color, national origin, religion, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation or disability. A hospital may impose clinically necessary or reasonable restrictions, for example to prevent the spread of infection, and the reasons are explained to the patient and visitors. Patients in labor may have a support person present throughout. Patients with disabilities may have a support person with them when needed to ensure effective communication.

### 3.9 Privacy during care

Staff protect the physical privacy of patients: they knock before entering, close curtains and doors during examinations, cover patients appropriately and discuss care out of earshot of other people. Patients may ask that their name not be listed in the directory. The Privacy and HIPAA Compliance Policy describes the rights of patients over their health information, including the right to see and obtain copies of records.

### 3.10 Spiritual care and cultural needs

Chaplains of many faiths are available around the clock. Patients may keep religious items at their bedside unless a safety issue arises, and may request diet that conforms to religious or cultural preferences. Interfaith spaces are provided in every hospital.

### 3.11 Billing, financial assistance and price transparency

Patients have the right to receive an estimate of their charges before scheduled services and an itemized bill after service. Bayview's financial assistance policy offers free or discounted care to eligible patients as required by California's Hospital Fair Pricing Act: patients in households with incomes up to 400 percent of the federal poverty level may qualify for discounted care, and those with incomes up to 200 percent may receive free care. Billing staff do not refer unpaid accounts to a collection agency until at least 180 days after the first bill and the patient has been given a chance to apply for assistance. Standard charges are posted on the Bayview website in machine-readable form and in a consumer-friendly list of shoppable services.

## 4. Responsibilities of Patients

Patients are asked to give accurate and complete information about their health, to follow the plan of care, to tell staff if they cannot follow it, to treat staff and other patients with courtesy, to respect the property of the hospital, to keep appointments or cancel in good time, and to pay their bills or ask for help with them. Staff explain these responsibilities in a courteous manner and do not use them as grounds for denying care.

## 5. How Staff Put Patient Rights Into Practice

### 5.1 Notice of rights

On admission or registration each patient is given a written statement of rights in a language he or she understands. Rights are posted in registration areas, emergency departments and inpatient units. Patients who are unable to read are given the information orally. Patient Access staff record the date on which the notice was given.

### 5.2 Patient advocates

Each hospital has patient advocates who visit units, listen to concerns, resolve problems on the spot and help patients with grievances. A patient advocate can be reached at any time through the hospital operator, and a telephone number is posted in each patient room.

### 5.3 Patient and Family Advisory Councils

Each hospital has a Patient and Family Advisory Council of former patients and family members who meet monthly with leaders to give advice on policies, signage, education materials and design. Council members complete orientation and are reimbursed for travel and parking.

### 5.4 Education of staff

All workforce members attend orientation on patient rights and complete annual refresher training that covers respectful communication, cultural humility, language access, disability access, restraint and seclusion, abuse reporting and the grievance process. Medical staff members complete the same training as part of their reappointment.

## 6. Complaints and Grievances

### 6.1 Making a complaint

A patient or representative may complain in person, by telephone, in writing, by email or through the website. Complaints may be made to any staff member, who must either resolve the matter on the spot or pass it to the nurse manager or the patient advocate. Patients are told that they may also complain directly to external bodies, which are named on the notice of rights: the California Department of Public Health, the Medical Board of California or the relevant licensing board, The Joint Commission, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services' Quality Improvement Organization, and, for civil rights complaints, the federal Office for Civil Rights.

### 6.2 Handling of grievances

The Office of Patient Experience acknowledges a grievance within 7 days and sends a written response within 30 days. If it needs more time, it tells the patient why and when to expect a reply. The response states the name of a contact person, the steps taken to investigate, the result and the date of completion. The grievance committee of the Board of Directors reviews summary data every quarter, and every complaint of abuse, neglect or discrimination is reviewed by the Chief Compliance Officer.

### 6.3 No retaliation

No patient will be denied care, treated worse or discouraged from complaining because he or she has complained or raised a concern. Any staff member who retaliates against a patient is subject to discipline.

### 6.4 Learning from complaints

Patient advocates classify every complaint by theme and unit. The Patient Safety Committee examines themes each quarter and sets improvement projects for the worst areas. Results appear on the quality dashboard.

## 6A. Special Situations

### 6A.1 Patients with limited English proficiency

Patient Access staff ask each patient for the language in which he or she wants to receive care and record it in the electronic health record. A badge or a language identification card shows staff which interpreter service is needed. The interpreter's name or identification number is recorded in the chart each time an interpreter is used for consent, discharge instructions or an important change in condition. Signs in the threshold languages tell patients that interpreter services are available at no cost.

### 6A.2 Patients who are deaf or hard of hearing, blind or low vision

Staff ask the patient what method of communication works best. Video remote interpreting units are available on every unit, and in-person sign language interpreters can be scheduled in advance. Pocket talkers and amplified phones are available, and televisions in patient rooms have captions. For patients who are blind or have low vision, staff read forms aloud, describe the layout of the room, offer large-print and braille materials, and welcome service animals.

### 6A.3 Transgender and gender-diverse patients

Patients are placed in rooms that fit their gender identity, whenever practical. Staff use the name and pronouns that the patient gives. Medical decisions are made on the basis of clinical need, and staff do not ask for details of a patient's anatomy that are not relevant to care. Incidents of harassment are reported to the unit manager and the Office of Patient Experience.

### 6A.4 Patients in custody

Patients who are in the custody of law enforcement keep their rights to informed consent, privacy during examinations and pain relief, as far as safety permits. Restraints on such patients are used only where a security risk has been determined and are removed during treatment whenever it is safe to do so. Law enforcement officers are asked to wait outside the room for examinations unless there is a specific safety need.

### 6A.5 Patients who are homeless

Discharge planners work with social workers and community partners to make sure that patients without housing are not discharged to the street. California Health and Safety Code section 1262.5 requires a written discharge plan for patients who are homeless, including the offering of a meal, weather-appropriate clothing, transportation, a screening for infectious disease and a referral to a shelter or recuperative care program.

### 6A.6 Patients who lack capacity and have no representative

When a patient lacks capacity and has no representative, the attending physician asks the Ethics Committee for a consultation, and may seek a conservatorship through the Office of the General Counsel. Decisions meanwhile focus on the patient's known wishes and best interests, and urgent treatment is not delayed.

### 6A.7 Ethics consultation

Any patient, family member or staff member may request an ethics consultation at any time by calling the operator. The Ethics Committee responds within one business day or, for urgent cases, within hours. Consultations are advisory, and a record is placed in the chart.

## 7. Enforcement and Review

Failure to follow this policy is a serious matter, and is handled under the corrective action policy. The Office of Patient Experience reviews this policy each year with the Patient and Family Advisory Councils, the Ethics Committee and the Chief Nursing Officer. Questions about the policy should be directed to the Office of Patient Experience and Advocacy.
